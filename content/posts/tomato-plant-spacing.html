Quick answer

Space tomato plants 24 to 36 inches apart for most home gardens, with rows 3 to 4 feet apart. Tight spacing of 18 to 24 inches works for staked, pruned plants. Caged plants need 24 to 36 inches, and unsupported sprawling plants need 36 to 48 inches. Bush (determinate) types can go closer than vining (indeterminate) types.

Tomatoes are generous plants. A seedling that fits in your hand in April can be a 5-foot, 3-foot-wide tangle by August. Gardeners who plant too close see fewer tomatoes, more blight and fruit that never ripens in the shade. Those who plant too far apart waste bed space and mulch.

This guide covers the right spacing for each way of growing tomatoes, how it changes in raised beds and containers, and how many plants fit in a given area. For your own bed dimensions, the plant spacing calculator gives an exact plant count.

Key takeaways
  • Staked and pruned: 18–24 in apart, rows 3–4 ft apart.
  • Caged: 24–36 in apart, rows 4 ft apart.
  • Sprawling on the ground: 36–48 in apart, rows 4–5 ft apart.
  • Containers: one plant per pot of at least 5 gallons; bigger pots are better for vining types.
  • Plant deep: bury two-thirds of the stem so roots form along it.

How far apart should you plant tomatoes?

The right gap depends on the plant's growth habit and how you support it. Indeterminate (vining) tomatoes keep growing and flowering until frost, so they need more room than determinate (bush) tomatoes, which grow to a set height and ripen most fruit within a few weeks. Why does tomato spacing matter so much?

Spacing affects three things that decide your harvest.

  • Airflow and disease. Fungal diseases such as early blight, late blight and septoria leaf spot spread when leaves stay wet. Open spacing lets foliage dry after rain and watering.
  • Light. Tomatoes need 6 to 8 hours of direct sun. Crowded plants shade each other, which lowers fruit set and delays ripening.
  • Roots. Neighboring plants compete for water and nutrients. Tightly packed plants wilt faster in heat and show more blossom-end rot when watering is uneven.

As a rule, a family of four needs somewhere between 3 and 6 healthy plants for fresh eating, depending on variety, and about 8 to 12 if you also want to can or freeze sauce. Those numbers are a gardener's rule of thumb, not an exact yield figure, so adjust to what you actually use.

How should you space tomatoes in a raised bed?

Raised beds warm early and drain well, which tomatoes love, but the narrow width limits layout. In a 4-foot-wide bed:

  • Plant one row down the center at 24 to 30 inches apart on stakes or a trellis, or
  • Plant two staggered rows, 24 inches apart in the row and 24 inches between the rows, for bush or caged types, and
  • Keep plants at least 6 to 12 inches from the board so you can reach the leaves and the fruit doesn't rest on the wood.

A 4 × 8 bed fits 4 to 6 tomatoes comfortably. Tomatoes can follow a cool-season crop in the same bed, so check dates in the planting calendar.

How far apart should tomatoes be in containers?

Grow one tomato per pot. The common guideline is a minimum of 5 gallons for determinate and dwarf types and 10 to 20 gallons for vigorous indeterminate varieties, which need more root room and are less likely to dry out. Space pots 18 to 24 inches apart (edge to edge) so leaves don't touch. A 24-inch pot works for one large plant plus a few basil seedlings around the edge.

How do you plant tomatoes step by step?

  1. Wait for warm soil. Transplant after your last frost date, when nights stay above 50°F and soil is at least 60°F. Find your date with the frost dates by ZIP code tool.
  2. Mark the spots. Use stakes or a tape measure so spacing is exact.
  3. Dig deep. Make a hole deep enough to bury two-thirds of the stem, or lay a leggy plant sideways in a shallow trench. Tomatoes root along buried stems, which gives a sturdier plant.
  4. Strip the lower leaves that would be buried or touch the soil.
  5. Install support now, before the roots grow. Pushing a stake or cage in later damages them.
  6. Water well and mulch with 2 to 3 inches of straw or shredded leaves to reduce soil splash, a major cause of leaf disease.

How do determinate and indeterminate tomatoes differ in spacing?

Determinate (bush) varieties such as many paste and early slicing tomatoes grow to about 3 to 4 feet, set fruit over a few weeks and stop. They need less support and tolerate closer spacing, but should not be pruned heavily because the suckers carry fruit. Indeterminate (vining) varieties, including most heirlooms and cherry types, keep growing to 6 feet or more until frost.

For vining types, pruning to one or two main stems and tying them to a stake lets you plant at the tight end of the range. Without pruning, they fill the space quickly and need the wider end. Check the plant tag or seed packet for "determinate" or "indeterminate", often abbreviated "det" or "indet".

What mistakes cause tomato spacing problems?

  1. Planting by seedling size. A 6-inch transplant will be 3 feet wide by midsummer.
  2. Mixing sprawling and staked plants in the same row. Sprawlers shade and smother staked neighbors.
  3. Crowding to fit more plants. Eight crowded plants often yield less than five spaced ones.
  4. Ignoring the support's footprint. A round cage is 18 to 24 inches wide before the plant adds foliage.
  5. Planting where tomatoes grew last year. Rotate to a new spot (or a different bed) to reduce soil-borne diseases.
Plant tomatoes away from black walnut trees. Walnut roots release juglone, a compound that wilts tomatoes within the root zone, and walnut roots often reach well beyond the tree's drip line. Use a raised bed or containers with fresh soil if you have a walnut nearby.

Frequently asked questions

Can you plant tomatoes 12 inches apart?

Only dwarf or patio varieties do well at 12 inches. Standard determinate and indeterminate tomatoes need at least 18 to 24 inches, or they will compete for light and water and develop more leaf disease.

How far apart should determinate tomatoes be?

Plant determinate (bush) tomatoes 18 to 24 inches apart in rows 3 feet apart. They stay smaller than indeterminate types and ripen most of their fruit over a few weeks.

How far apart should indeterminate tomatoes be?

Indeterminate (vining) tomatoes need 24 to 36 inches between plants when trained on a stake, trellis or cage, and up to 48 inches if left to sprawl.

Can you plant two tomato plants in one hole?

It is not recommended. Two plants in one hole compete for the same roots, nutrients and water, and the dense foliage holds moisture and disease. Space them at least 18 to 24 inches apart instead.

How far apart should cherry tomatoes be planted?

Most indeterminate cherry tomatoes get large and vigorous, so give them 24 to 36 inches. Compact "patio" cherry types can be spaced 12 to 18 inches in pots or beds.

How far apart should tomato rows be?

Leave 3 to 4 feet between rows of staked or caged tomatoes, and 4 to 5 feet for sprawling plants. That gives you room to walk, prune and harvest without breaking stems.

The bottom line

Plant tomatoes 24 to 36 inches apart in rows 3 to 4 feet apart as a safe default, closer (18 to 24 inches) for staked and pruned plants, and farther (36 to 48 inches) if they'll sprawl. Bury the stem, support early and mulch. To fit the right number of plants in your bed, run your dimensions through the plant spacing calculator.
