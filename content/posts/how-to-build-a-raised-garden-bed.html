Quick answer

To build a 4×8 raised garden bed, screw three 8-foot 2×12 boards into a rectangle (two long sides, and two ends cut from the third board) with 4×4 corner posts and 3-inch exterior screws. It takes 1 to 2 hours, holds about 1 cubic yard of soil and costs roughly $140 to $290 with example prices, depending on the lumber.

A raised bed is one of the easiest woodworking projects you can do. It needs only four cuts, a drill and a tape measure, and a basic design lasts for years. The harder decisions come before you pick up a saw: where to put it, what wood to use, and how much soil to buy.

This guide gives you a complete cut list for a 4 × 8 bed, example costs, step-by-step assembly and tips to avoid common mistakes. When your frame is done, the soil calculator tells you how much soil to order for your bed's exact size and depth.

Key takeaways
  • Size: 4 ft wide (you can reach the middle) by 8 ft long is the standard.
  • Materials for one bed: three 8-ft 2×12s, one 8-ft 4×4, about 24 exterior screws.
  • Soil needed: about 27 cubic feet (1 cubic yard) for a bed filled 11¼ in deep.
  • Example cost: about $140–$190 with pine or fir, $240–$290 with cedar (including soil).
  • Check for square by measuring both diagonals; they should match.

What size should a raised garden bed be?

The standard raised bed is 4 feet wide, which lets you reach the middle from either side without stepping in. Length is flexible, but 8 feet is the easiest to build from standard lumber without waste. Narrower, 3-foot beds suit shorter gardeners or children.

For height, a single 2×12 board gives about 11¼ inches (a 2×12 is actually 1½ × 11¼ inches), which is deep enough for most vegetables. If you want deeper soil or an easier-to-reach bed, stack two boards for about 22½ inches. See the soil calculator for volumes at any height. Leave at least 2 to 3 feet between beds for paths.

How do you build a raised bed step by step?

  1. Choose and level the site. Pick a spot with 6 to 8 hours of sun, close to water. Mow or remove tall grass, and level the ground with a shovel.
  2. Cut the boards. Leave two 8-foot boards uncut. Cut the third in half to make two 48-inch pieces, then trim 3 inches off each so the ends fit between the long sides (45 inches).
  3. Cut the corner posts. Cut the 4×4 into four 12-inch pieces (or 11¼ inches to match the board height).
  4. Assemble the frame upside down on flat ground. Stand a post in each corner, pre-drill, then drive three 3-inch screws through each board into the post.
  5. Check that it is square. Measure the diagonals from corner to corner; they should be equal (about 107⅜ inches on the outside of a 4 × 8 frame). Adjust until they match.
  6. Move it into place and make sure it's level, using a level on the board edges. Shim low corners with soil, not wood.
  7. Line the bottom. Lay overlapping cardboard (remove tape) or landscape fabric inside the bed to smother grass. Add hardware cloth first if you have gophers.
  8. Fill with soil to within an inch of the top, water it thoroughly and let it settle before planting.

How much soil does a raised bed need?

A 4 × 8 bed built from 2×12s measures 45 × 93 inches on the inside. At an 11¼-inch fill, that is 45 × 93 × 11.25 ÷ 1,728 = 27.2 cubic feet, or about 1.0 cubic yard. That works out to 14 bags of 2 cubic feet. Allow another 10 percent for settling.

One common blend is roughly 60 percent topsoil, 30 percent compost and 10 percent aeration material such as perlite, though blends vary. Don't fill with plain topsoil; it compacts. The soil calculator converts any bed size into cubic yards and bags.

What mistakes should you avoid when building a raised bed?

  1. Making it too wide. Beds wider than 4 feet force you to step in and compact the soil.
  2. Skipping corner posts. Screws alone can pull out in wet soil, and the long sides can bow outward, especially when full.
  3. Using the wrong lumber. Avoid railroad ties and pre-2004 treated wood.
  4. Building on a slope without leveling. Soil and water slump to the low side.
  5. Forgetting the path. Allow 2 to 3 feet between beds and mulch with wood chips or gravel.
  6. Placing in shade. Fewer than 6 hours of sun means small harvests.
Bow-out warning: a long, deep bed full of wet soil pushes outward. If your bed is longer than 8 feet or taller than one board, add a cross-brace (a 2×4 or a length of cable) across the middle so the sides don't bow.

Frequently asked questions

How long does it take to build a raised bed?

A 4 × 8 raised bed takes about 1 to 2 hours to cut and assemble. Allow another hour or two to level the site, line the bottom and fill it with soil.

Is pressure-treated wood safe for a vegetable garden bed?

Modern pressure-treated lumber (made since 2004) uses copper-based preservatives, not arsenic, and many extension services consider it acceptable. If you'd rather avoid it, choose cedar or redwood instead.

Do raised beds need a bottom?

No. An open bottom lets roots and drainage reach the ground below. Line it with cardboard to smother grass, or hardware cloth if burrowing animals are a problem.

Can I put a raised bed on grass?

Yes. Mow the grass short, lay overlapping cardboard on top, build the frame on it and fill with soil. The grass dies from lack of light and decomposes.

How do I stop a raised bed from rotting?

Choose rot-resistant lumber like cedar, keep the bottom edge out of standing water and leave the soil side unsealed. Good drainage matters more than any sealant.

Is it cheaper to build or buy a raised bed?

Building is usually cheaper per bed, especially with standard lumber, and gives you any size you want. Kits can save time and cut boards for you but often cost more and use thinner wood.

The bottom line

A 4 × 8 raised garden bed needs three 8-foot 2×12 boards, a 4×4 post and some screws, takes an afternoon and holds about 1 cubic yard of soil.
