Quick answer

Bulk mulch is usually cheaper once you need more than about 2.5 to 3 cubic yards; below that, delivery fees make bagged mulch the better deal. One cubic yard equals 13.5 bags of 2 cu ft. With example prices of $4.50 per bag and $40 per yard plus $50 delivery, the break-even is 2.4 cubic yards.

Standing in the garden center aisle with 30 bags on a cart, it is easy to wonder whether a delivered pile would have been cheaper. Sometimes it would. Sometimes the delivery fee, the tarp and the shoveling make bags the smarter buy. The answer depends on the volume, the local prices and how you will move it.

Below is the break-even math with every number shown, a quality comparison, and a checklist for ordering bulk. For your own prices, the mulch calculator runs the bag-versus-bulk comparison automatically.

Key takeaways
  • Conversion: 1 cubic yard = 27 cubic feet = 13.5 bags of 2 cu ft or 9 bags of 3 cu ft.
  • Break-even: about 2.4–3.3 cubic yards in the examples below, depending on delivery fee and prices.
  • Small jobs: under about 1.5 cubic yards, bags are almost always cheaper once delivery is added.
  • Quality: bags are consistent; bulk varies by supplier, so ask what it is made from.
  • Always: compare cost per cubic foot, not per bag.

How do you compare bagged and bulk mulch prices?

Bags are priced per bag and bulk per cubic yard, so convert both to the same unit first. The simplest is cost per cubic yard. A 2 cu ft bag holds 2/27 of a yard, so you need 13.5 bags per cubic yard. A 3 cu ft bag needs 9 bags per yard, and a 1.5 cu ft bag needs 18.

Many bags show a volume that is nominal. If the label says 2 cu ft, trust it, but compare against the same volume on the competing brand. A bag labeled 1.5 cu ft at a lower price can cost more per cubic foot.

Formula: bag price × (27 ÷ bag size in cu ft) = price per cubic yard. At $4.50 for 2 cu ft: 4.50 × 13.5 = $60.75 per cubic yard.

What if you pick up bulk mulch yourself?

Many landscape suppliers let you load a truck or trailer yourself and charge only for the material. With no delivery fee, bulk wins from the first yard in our example: 1 yard at $40 against $60.75 in bags. The catch is hauling. A full-size pickup with a 6.5 ft bed holds about 2 cubic yards, and wood mulch weighs roughly 400–800 lb per cubic yard depending on moisture. Check your truck's payload rating, and never overload a small trailer.

Is bulk mulch lower quality than bagged mulch?

Not by definition, but the range is wider. Bagged mulch from major brands is screened and consistent, so you know the color and texture. Bulk mulch can be better or worse:

  • Good bulk: aged, screened hardwood or bark from a landscape supply yard. It is often darker and fresher than bags that sat on a pallet all winter.
  • Cheap bulk: mixed ground wood waste that can include pieces of treated lumber, pallets or construction debris. Ask what is in it, and avoid mulch from demolition sources.
  • Fresh unaged piles: can have a sour, vinegar or ammonia smell if they heated or went anaerobic. Extension guidance suggests spreading it thin and letting it air for a few days before it goes around plants.
  • Free arborist chips: excellent for paths, tree rings and large beds, but irregular in size and unlikely to be dyed or uniform.

Dyed mulch is available both ways. Bags list the colorant; for bulk, ask the supplier if it is dyed and with what.

Which is easier to spread: bags or a pile?

Bags are tidy and carry well, a bag at a time. Bulk dumps one big pile, then you shovel it into a wheelbarrow. A 3-yard pile is 81 cubic feet, which is about 14 loads in a 6 cu ft wheelbarrow or 20 loads in a 4 cu ft one. Either way, put a tarp under the pile to protect your driveway and to make it easy to scoop up the last of it.

How do you order bulk mulch step by step?

  1. Measure and calculate. Use square feet × depth in inches ÷ 324 for cubic yards, then add 10% for settling.
  2. Call two or three suppliers. Ask for the price per yard, delivery fee, minimum order and what the mulch is made from.
  3. Ask about the yard. Delivery trucks measure a "yard" loosely; confirm the stated quantity.
  4. Choose the drop spot. Close to the beds, on a tarp, off a street, and where the truck's weight will not crack the pavement.
  5. Spread within a few days. A big pile can heat up and the surface can crust.
Don't pay for yards you can't use. Large piles left sitting get weeds, can compact and kill the grass underneath. If an exact amount is hard to guess, order slightly under and top up with a few bags at the end.

What about waste, storage and timing?

Bags create plastic waste: a 10-yard job is 135 empty bags. Some garden centers have no take-back, so factor in the trash. Bags also stay clean and dry in the garage for weeks, while a pile outdoors should be spread within a few days, and quickly if rain is forecast. If a project will run over several weekends, bags let you work at your own pace, and bulk lets you finish in one push.

Seasonality matters too. Demand peaks in spring, when everyone mulches at once, so delivery slots book up. Ordering a week or two ahead, or buying in late fall or summer, can avoid the rush.

What is the best choice for your project?

  • Under about 1.5 cubic yards, no truck: bags.
  • A few beds, with a pickup: self-load bulk, if a yard is close by.
  • Over 3 cubic yards: delivered bulk is usually cheaper.
  • Lots of small, hard-to-reach beds: bags, because a pile has to be wheeled in anyway.
  • You want a specific color: compare directly; bags make color easy, bulk needs a sample.

Frequently asked questions

How many bags of mulch are in a cubic yard?

A cubic yard holds 27 cubic feet, so it equals 13.5 bags of 2 cu ft, 9 bags of 3 cu ft or 18 bags of 1.5 cu ft. Always round up when buying.

Is it cheaper to buy mulch in bags or bulk?

It depends on volume. With typical delivery fees, bulk tends to be cheaper above roughly 2.5–3.5 cubic yards, and bags are cheaper below that.

How much does a delivery of bulk mulch usually cost?

Delivery fees commonly run from about $40 to $100 depending on distance, and some suppliers waive them above a minimum order. Ask for the fee before you compare prices.

How much mulch fits in a pickup truck?

A full-size pickup with a 6.5 ft bed holds about 2 cubic yards level with the sides, but check the payload because wet mulch is heavy.

Can I get free mulch?

Many towns offer free wood chips or compost, and tree services sometimes drop chips for free. Quality varies, so check for weeds, plastic and treated wood.

Do mulch bags actually contain the volume on the label?

Reputable brands list a nominal volume, and settling can make a bag feel smaller. Compare cost per cubic foot across brands rather than per bag.

The bottom line

Convert everything to cubic yards, add 10% for settling, and compare. In most examples, bags win under about 2.5 cubic yards and delivered bulk wins above it. For your prices and bed sizes, the mulch calculator shows the break-even in seconds.
