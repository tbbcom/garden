--- title: How to Measure Your Lawn's Square Footage (Any Shape) labels: Lawn Care description: How to measure lawn square footage: multiply length by width, split odd shapes into rectangles, circles and triangles, then subtract the house and beds. ---
Quick answer

To measure lawn square footage, multiply length by width (in feet) for each rectangular section, then add the sections together. For triangles, use base × height ÷ 2; for circles, 3.14 × radius². Subtract the house, driveway, patio and planting beds. A 30 × 40 ft lawn is 1,200 sq ft.

Almost every lawn job starts with the same number: square feet. Fertilizer, grass seed, weed treatments, sod and even lawn-service quotes are all priced or dosed by the area. Guess it wrong by 20% and you buy too much or too little, or under- or over-apply a product.

The good news is that you don't need surveying tools. This guide shows five ways to measure your lawn, with worked examples for odd shapes, and how to handle the parts that aren't grass. Once you have the number, plug it into the garden calculator or the lawn fertilizer calculator.

Key takeaways
  • Rectangle: length × width. A 30 × 40 ft area is 1,200 sq ft.
  • Triangle: base × height ÷ 2. Circle: 3.14 × radius × radius.
  • Subtract the house, driveway, walks, patios and beds from the lot to get only grass.
  • One acre is 43,560 sq ft; a typical suburban lawn is 5,000–10,000 sq ft.
  • Round up slightly for seed or fertilizer, but never guess the area by eye.

How do you calculate the area of a lawn?

All lawns, however curvy, are made of a few simple shapes. Sketch your lawn on paper, divide it into rectangles, triangles and circles, measure each one in feet, and add up the areas. This table shows the formulas.

Measure in feet. If you only have inches, divide by 12. Always measure the grass itself, not the property line.

How do you measure an irregularly shaped lawn?

Real yards have curves, corners and islands. Here are three reliable methods.

Method 1: Split it into shapes

Draw the lawn, then divide it with straight lines into rectangles and triangles. An L-shaped lawn with one 60 × 40 ft rectangle and one 20 × 30 ft rectangle is 2,400 + 600 = 3,000 sq ft.

Method 2: Average the widths

For a curved or kidney-shaped lawn, measure the length, then measure the width at 3 or 4 points. A 40 ft long lawn with widths of 8, 10, 12 and 9 ft averages 9.75 ft wide, so about 40 × 9.75 = 390 sq ft. This is accurate to within about 10%, which is good enough for seed and fertilizer.

Method 3: Start from the lot and subtract

Take the lot area, subtract everything that isn't grass. Example: an 80 × 120 ft lot is 9,600 sq ft. Subtract a 40 × 30 ft house (1,200), a 12 × 40 ft driveway (480), a 3 × 30 ft walk (90) and 150 sq ft of beds. That leaves 7,680 sq ft of lawn, which is about 0.18 acre.

How do you pace a lawn accurately?

Measure out 30 ft with a tape and walk it normally, counting steps. If it takes 12 steps, your step is 30 ÷ 12 = 2.5 ft. A lawn edge of 48 steps is then 48 × 2.5 = 120 ft. Do this on level ground and average a few walks.

What should you subtract from your lawn area?

Only subtract things that won't get the product or seed. These are the usual ones:

  • Buildings, including porches and sheds.
  • Hard surfaces: driveways, sidewalks, patios, decks.
  • Planting beds, trees and mulched areas with no grass.
  • Pools, ponds and play areas without turf.

Don't subtract small areas like individual tree bases or the strip along a sidewalk; the amounts are tiny and fertilizer and seed need an edge.

What measuring mistakes should you avoid?

Don't use the lot size from your listing or deed as your lawn size. The house, driveway and beds usually take a third or more of a suburban lot, so using the lot number overstates your lawn and can lead to over-application of fertilizer or herbicide.
  1. Mixing units. Make sure everything is in feet before multiplying.
  2. Measuring along a slope. A tape laid along a slope reads slightly long; for home lawns the error is small, so ignore it.
  3. Forgetting the front, side and back yards if you're treating the whole property.
  4. Double-counting overlapping shapes when you split the lawn.
  5. Using a radius instead of a diameter for circles. Radius is half of the distance across.

What does your square footage tell you to buy?

Once you have the number, the shopping math is short. At 1 lb of nitrogen per 1,000 sq ft, a 7,680 sq ft lawn needs 7.68 lb of nitrogen, which is about 32 lb of a 24% product. Overseeding the same lawn with tall fescue at 3–4 lb per 1,000 sq ft takes roughly 23–31 lb of seed. Sod is sold by the square foot or by the pallet, usually covering around 450–500 sq ft, so always round up and add about 5–10% for cuts on curved edges.

Keep your number written down with the date. You will use it every year for fertilizer, seed, weed control and watering run times.

How do you account for slopes and tight corners?

For fertilizer and seed, measuring along a gentle slope as if it were flat is fine, since the difference is only a few percent. On a steep bank, measure along the ground surface with a tape, because the true area is larger than the flat map view. In tight corners, treat the corner as a small triangle or rectangle and add it to the total instead of rounding the whole shape up.

How often should you re-measure?

Measure once and keep the number unless the yard changes. Re-measure after you add a patio, expand a bed, remove a tree or install a shed, since each one changes how much grass you actually have. A quick sketch with the dimensions on it, stored with your fertilizer receipts or in a phone note, saves you from repeating the whole job next spring and keeps every product amount consistent from year to year.

Frequently asked questions

How big is the average lawn in square feet?

It varies widely, but many suburban lawns fall between about 5,000 and 10,000 sq ft. A quarter-acre lot is 10,890 sq ft before subtracting the house and driveway.

How many square feet are in an acre?

One acre is 43,560 sq ft. Half an acre is 21,780 sq ft and a quarter acre is 10,890 sq ft.

How do I convert square yards to square feet?

Multiply square yards by 9. A sod order of 100 square yards covers 900 sq ft.

How do I measure a lawn with a smartphone?

Use the area-measuring tool in a satellite map app: tap around the lawn's edges, and it displays the square footage. Check for tree shadows and compare to a tape measurement of one edge.

Why does lawn size matter for fertilizer and seed?

Rates are given per 1,000 sq ft, so an error in area leads directly to an error in how much you apply. Too much can burn the lawn; too little wastes the effort.

The bottom line

Break your lawn into rectangles, triangles and circles, measure each in feet, add the areas and subtract anything that isn't grass. Ten minutes with a tape measure saves money on every product you buy afterward. Take your number to the garden calculator and the lawn fertilizer calculator.
