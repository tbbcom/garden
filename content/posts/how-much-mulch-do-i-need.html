Quick answer

One cubic yard of mulch covers 108 square feet at 3 inches deep, the depth most garden beds need. To work out your own amount, multiply the bed's length × width in feet to get square feet, multiply by the depth in inches, then divide by 324. The answer is in cubic yards. For bags, multiply cubic yards by 13.5 to get the number of 2 cu ft bags.

Buying mulch is one of those jobs where guessing costs you twice. Buy too little and you're back at the garden center mid-project. Buy too much and you're left with half a yard of wood chips slowly composting in the driveway. The fix is a two-minute measurement and one simple formula.

This guide covers that formula, a ready-made coverage chart, how many bags common bed sizes need, the right depth for each type of mulch, and when bulk delivery beats bags. Key takeaways
  • Formula: square feet × depth (in) ÷ 324 = cubic yards.
  • Coverage: 1 cubic yard = 324 sq ft at 1 in, 162 at 2 in, 108 at 3 in, 81 at 4 in.
  • Bags: 1 cubic yard = 13.5 bags of 2 cu ft, or 9 bags of 3 cu ft.
  • Depth: 2–3 in for most wood mulches; never more than 4 in, and never piled against trunks.
  • Buy 10% extra: fresh mulch settles, and edges always take more than you expect.

How do you calculate how much mulch you need?

Every mulch calculation comes down to volume: area × depth. The only complication is that beds are measured in feet, depth in inches and bulk mulch is sold in cubic yards. Here's the whole process in four steps.

Step 1: Measure the area in square feet

For a rectangle, multiply length × width. A bed 20 ft long and 4 ft wide is 80 sq ft. Measure the inside of any edging, and subtract large areas you won't mulch, such as a patio or a big shrub base.

Step 2: Choose your depth in inches

Most beds need 3 inches of new mulch, or 2 inches if you're topping up an existing layer. See the depth chart below for each mulch type.

Step 3: Convert to cubic yards

Multiply square feet by depth in inches, then divide by 324. The 324 comes from 12 inches per foot × 27 cubic feet per cubic yard.

Example: 80 sq ft × 3 in ÷ 324 = 0.74 cubic yards.

Step 4: Convert to bags (if buying bagged mulch)

Multiply cubic yards by 27 to get cubic feet, then divide by your bag size and round up.

Example: 0.74 × 27 = 20 cu ft ÷ 2 cu ft bags = 10 bags. Add 10% for settling and buy 11.

How deep should mulch be?

Depth matters as much as quantity. Too thin and weeds push straight through. Too thick and the layer sheds water and starves shallow roots of oxygen. University extension services consistently recommend 2–4 inches, depending on how coarse the mulch is.

Don't build "mulch volcanoes." Piling mulch against tree trunks or shrub stems keeps the bark constantly wet, which invites rot, disease and gnawing rodents. Pull mulch back so there's a 3–6 inch bare ring around every trunk.

How to measure odd-shaped beds

Few beds are perfect rectangles. These shortcuts are accurate enough for buying mulch:

  • Circle: area = 3.14 × radius². A round bed 8 ft across has a 4 ft radius, so 3.14 × 16 = 50 sq ft.
  • Triangle or corner bed: area = base × height ÷ 2.
  • Ring around a tree: work out the area of the full circle, then subtract the small bare circle at the trunk.
  • Curvy or kidney-shaped bed: measure the length, then take three or four widths along it and average them. Length × average width gets you within about 10%.
  • Complicated layout: split it into rectangles and circles, work out each one, and add them together. The calculator lets you add as many beds as you need.

Topping up old mulch: how much to add

You rarely start from bare soil. Organic mulch breaks down over one to two years, so most beds only need a refresh. Push a trowel into the existing layer and measure how much is left, then add only the difference. If 1 inch remains and you want 3, add 2 inches.

Before topping up, rake the old layer to break up any crust that sheds water. If old mulch is matted, moldy or already deeper than 4 inches, remove some of it first.

Bagged vs bulk mulch: which is cheaper?

Bulk mulch is almost always cheaper per cubic yard, but the delivery fee changes the math for small jobs. Using example prices of $4.50 for a 2 cu ft bag, $40 per cubic yard in bulk and a $50 delivery fee:

  • One cubic yard in bags: 13.5 bags × $4.50 = $60.75
  • One cubic yard in bulk: $40 + $50 delivery = $90
  • Break-even: bulk becomes cheaper at about 2.4 cubic yards (roughly 33 bags).

Your local prices will differ, so plug them into the mulch calculator, which shows which option is cheaper for your exact project. Bulk has two other advantages for big jobs: no plastic bags to dispose of, and one delivery instead of several car trips.

Will it fit in my truck?

A full-size pickup with a 6.5 ft bed holds about 2 cubic yards of mulch level with the sides. Wood mulch weighs roughly 400–800 lb per cubic yard depending on moisture, so it's well within most half-ton trucks' payload. Rock and gravel are a different story: at roughly 1.4 tons per cubic yard, one yard can max out a light pickup.

5 mulching mistakes that waste money

  1. Measuring the outside of the edging. On narrow beds, a few inches of edging adds up to a lot of mulch you don't need.
  2. Forgetting settling. Fresh, fluffy mulch compacts 10–20%. Buy about 10% extra.
  3. Going too deep. More than 4 inches doesn't stop more weeds; it just costs more and can suffocate roots.
  4. Mulching over weeds. Pull or smother existing weeds first, or perennial weeds will come straight through.
  5. Mulching too early in spring. Wait until the soil has warmed, usually mid to late spring. Mulch insulates, and cold soil under mulch stays cold longer.

Frequently asked questions

How much mulch do I need for a 4×8 raised bed?

A 4 × 8 ft raised bed (32 sq ft) needs 8 cubic feet of mulch at 3 inches deep, which is 4 bags of 2 cu ft. At 2 inches it needs 5.3 cubic feet, or 3 bags. In vegetable beds, use straw, shredded leaves or compost instead of wood mulch.

How many yards of mulch do I need for 1,000 square feet?

At 3 inches deep, 1,000 sq ft needs 9.26 cubic yards; at 2 inches it needs 6.17 cubic yards. With 10% extra for settling, order about 10 yards or 7 yards respectively.

Is 2 inches of mulch enough?

Two inches is enough for fine mulches like shredded hardwood, and when topping up an existing layer. For new beds or coarse wood chips, 3 inches gives better weed control and moisture retention.

How much area does a bag of mulch cover?

A 2 cu ft bag covers 12 sq ft at 2 inches deep or 8 sq ft at 3 inches. A 3 cu ft bag covers 18 sq ft at 2 inches or 12 sq ft at 3 inches.

How much mulch do I need around a tree?

A mulch ring 6 ft across at 3 inches deep, with a 1 ft bare circle at the trunk, needs about 7 cubic feet, or 4 bags of 2 cu ft. Wider rings are better for trees, ideally out to the drip line, but keep the depth at 2–3 inches.

When is the best time to put down mulch?

Mid to late spring, after the soil has warmed and spring weeds have been pulled, is ideal. A second application in fall helps insulate perennials and shrub roots through winter.

The bottom line

Measure your beds, multiply square feet by depth in inches and divide by 324. That's your cubic yards. Aim for 2–3 inches of most wood mulches, add 10% for settling, and compare bag and bulk prices before you buy.
