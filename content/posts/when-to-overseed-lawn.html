Quick answer

Overseed cool-season lawns from late August to mid October, ideally about 45 days before your first fall frost. Overseed warm-season lawns in late spring to early summer, once soil stays above about 65–70°F. Fall is the best season because warm soil and cool air help seedlings root without heat stress or weed competition.

Overseeding means spreading new grass seed over an existing lawn without tearing it up. It fills thin spots, adds fresh and tougher varieties, and crowds out weeds. Done at the right time, one weekend of work can turn a patchy lawn thick by spring. Done at the wrong time, the seed gets baked, flooded or smothered by weeds.

This guide gives the best dates for each grass type, the steps in order, and the common mistakes to avoid. To work out how many pounds you need, use the garden calculator for the area and the rates below.

Key takeaways
  • Cool-season grass: late August–mid October (best), early spring (second choice).
  • Warm-season grass: late spring to early summer, when soil is 65–70°F or warmer.
  • Soil temperature: cool-season seed germinates best between about 50°F and 65°F.
  • Rate: about half the new-lawn rate; for example, 3–4 lb tall fescue per 1,000 sq ft.
  • Moisture: keep the seedbed damp until the seed sprouts, which takes 5–30 days by species.

What is the best time of year to overseed a lawn?

For the cool-season grasses that dominate the northern US, early fall is the best time. Soil is still warm from summer, which speeds germination, while air temperatures are mild so seedlings don't dry out. Weed pressure drops as summer annuals die, and seedlings get several months of cool growth before winter. Penn State and Michigan State Extension both emphasize fall as the best window.

Spring is a second choice. Spring-seeded grass has to establish before summer heat arrives, and crabgrass is germinating at the same time, so you can't use a normal crabgrass preventer.

The simplest approach: count back about 45 days from your average first fall frost. A first frost around October 20 makes September 1 a good date. Use a soil thermometer pushed 2 inches into the ground if you want precision.

How do you know if your lawn needs overseeding?

Overseed when bare spots total more than about 10–15% of the lawn, grass thins visibly under trees or on slopes, or weeds are taking space that grass used to hold. A lawn more than about 8–10 years old often benefits from newer disease-resistant varieties. If more than half the lawn is dead or weeds, starting over with a full renovation may be better.

How do you overseed a lawn step by step?

  1. Mow low. Cut to about 1.5–2 inches and bag the clippings so seed can reach soil.
  2. Remove thatch and debris. Dethatch or rake hard where thatch exceeds about 1/2 inch. Seed must touch soil.
  3. Aerate if the soil is hard. Core aeration leaves holes where seed can settle and improves rooting.
  4. Pick a good seed. Match the grass already growing, or choose newer improved cultivars for your region and shade level.
  5. Spread seed in two directions. Use a broadcast spreader at about half the new-lawn rate: roughly 3–4 lb per 1,000 sq ft for tall fescue or ryegrass, 1–2 lb for Kentucky bluegrass.
  6. Apply a starter fertilizer. A product with more phosphorus helps roots, unless a soil test or local rule says otherwise.
  7. Water lightly and often. Keep the top inch moist, usually 5–10 minutes two or three times a day, until seedlings emerge.
  8. Mow when new grass reaches about 3–4 inches, using a sharp blade and never removing more than a third of the height.

How long does overseeded grass take to sprout?

Germination speed depends on species and soil temperature. Perennial ryegrass is fastest; Kentucky bluegrass is slowest and can look like nothing is happening for weeks.

What should you do about weed killer and crabgrass preventer?

Most pre-emergent herbicides block grass seed too. Do not apply crabgrass preventer within about 6–12 weeks of seeding unless the label says it is safe for new grass. Many broadleaf weed killers also harm seedlings; wait until new grass has been mowed at least three or four times.

What overseeding mistakes should you avoid?

  1. Seeding on top of thatch. Seed that doesn't touch soil dries out.
  2. Letting the seedbed dry out. A few hot, dry days can kill sprouts.
  3. Too much seed. Crowded seedlings are weak and prone to disease.
  4. Mowing too soon or with a dull blade, which pulls out seedlings.
  5. Waiting too late. Seed that sprouts just before a hard freeze may not survive winter.

What seed should you choose for overseeding?

Match the species already in your lawn, or pick improved varieties of a grass that suits your sun and region. Look for the label's variety names and the National Turfgrass Evaluation Program (NTEP) performance ratings, which universities publish. Avoid cheap mixes heavy in annual ryegrass; they green up fast but die out.

Kentucky bluegrass spreads by rhizomes and can repair itself, but it is slow to germinate. Pairing it with a little perennial ryegrass gives quick cover while the bluegrass establishes.

What does the first month after overseeding look like?

  • Week 1: keep the top inch of soil damp with short, frequent watering. Do not mow.
  • Weeks 2–3: the first ryegrass and fescue sprouts appear. Keep watering; bluegrass has not started.
  • Weeks 3–5: first mowing at 3–4 inches. Start to water less often but deeper.
  • Weeks 6–8: apply a normal fall feeding once the new grass has been mowed about four times.

Keep pets and kids off the area where you can. Thin spots that did not take can be reseeded in the same season if there are still 4–6 weeks before hard frost.

Should you overseed in spring instead?

You can, if you missed the fall window or need to repair winter damage. Seed cool-season grass as soon as the soil is workable and above about 50°F, typically March to May depending on region. Expect more watering, because spring seedlings have only a few weeks of mild weather before heat arrives, and expect weed competition from crabgrass. If you plan to use a crabgrass preventer, skip spring seeding or use a seed-safe product, and mow new grass at 3 inches or higher all summer to help it survive.

Frequently asked questions

Is it too late to overseed my lawn in October?

In the northern US it is getting late, but it often still works if soil temperatures are above about 50°F and seed has a month or more to establish. In colder zones, consider a dormant seeding after the ground cools.

Can I overseed without aerating or raking?

You can, but results are usually poor. Seed needs soil contact, so at minimum mow low and rake the surface; slit seeding or core aeration gives much better germination.

Should I overseed before or after fertilizing?

Do both at the same time. A starter fertilizer applied at overseeding feeds seedlings, and a regular feeding can follow once the new grass has been mowed a few times.

What is dormant seeding?

It is spreading seed in late fall or winter after the soil is too cold for germination, so seed sprouts in spring. It works best in cold climates and is less reliable than early fall seeding.

Does overseeding really work?

Yes, when timed correctly and kept moist. Over time it thickens turf and reduces weeds, though it won't fix problems like heavy shade, compaction or poor drainage on its own.

The bottom line

Overseed cool-season lawns in late summer to early fall and warm-season lawns in late spring to early summer. Mow low, expose the soil, spread about half the new-lawn rate in two directions and keep it moist until it sprouts. Use the garden calculator to size the job.
