Quick answer

Plant watermelon 2–3 weeks after your average last frost, when the soil is at least 70°F and nights stay above 55°F. Most varieties need 80–100 days, so gardeners in zones 3–5 should start seeds indoors and transplant. Southern zones can direct sow in March or April.

Watermelon is the most heat-hungry crop in the vegetable garden. It needs a long, warm season, and it will sulk for weeks in cold soil. The goal is to give seedlings a head start where summers are short and to plant at the right moment where they are long.

This guide covers the soil-temperature rule, dates for USDA zones 3–10 and the tricks that make a northern watermelon crop possible. To get dates for your town, look up your frost dates by ZIP code, then find your exact dates with the planting calendar.

Key takeaways
  • Soil temperature: 70°F minimum for transplanting or sowing; 80–95°F is ideal for germination.
  • Timing: transplant 2–3 weeks after the last frost; direct sow 3–4 weeks after.
  • Season length: standard varieties need 80–100+ days; icebox types ripen in about 70–80.
  • Cold zones (3–5): start indoors 3–4 weeks before transplanting and warm the soil with black plastic.
  • Spacing: 2–3 ft apart in rows 6–8 ft apart, or one hill every 4–6 ft.

What soil temperature does watermelon need?

Watermelon germinates best at 80–95°F and will barely sprout below about 65°F. Seedlings stop growing in cold soil, and chilled roots can stunt a plant for the whole season. Use a soil thermometer pushed 2–4 inches deep.

How do you grow watermelon in a short northern summer?

Gardeners in zones 3–5 can succeed, but they need to stack the odds:

  • Pick short-season varieties. Look for icebox types that mature in 70–85 days instead of 90–100.
  • Start seed indoors 3–4 weeks before transplanting, in 3–4 inch pots, on a warm spot with strong light.
  • Warm the soil. Lay black plastic mulch 1–2 weeks before planting. It can raise soil temperature by several degrees.
  • Add row cover over transplants until flowers open, then remove it so bees can pollinate.
  • Choose the warmest site: south-facing, with shelter from cold wind.

How do you plant watermelon step by step?

  1. Prepare a sunny bed. Watermelon needs at least 8 hours of full sun and well-drained soil with a pH of about 6.0–6.8. Mix in 2–3 inches of compost.
  2. Warm the soil with black plastic in cool regions.
  3. Harden off seedlings for 5–7 days if you started them indoors.
  4. Plant when soil reaches 70°F. Set transplants at the same depth as in the pot, 2–3 feet apart in rows 6–8 feet apart.
  5. For direct sowing, plant 3–4 seeds per hill, 1 inch deep, then thin to the best 2 plants.
  6. Water deeply once or twice a week, about 1–2 inches in total, and mulch with straw once the soil is warm.

A single row 20 feet long at 3-foot spacing holds 7 plants, and each vine needs roughly 16–24 square feet to sprawl. That is why watermelon suits a large bed or a trellis with slings more than a small raised bed.

How do you know when watermelon is ripe?

Count days from planting, then look for physical signs: the curly tendril closest to the fruit turns brown and dry, the underside spot (field spot) changes from white to creamy yellow, and the rind loses its shine. A ripe melon also sounds dull when tapped, though that clue is less reliable. Most varieties are ready 35–45 days after the female flowers are pollinated.

Don't rush the planting date. A watermelon transplant set into 60°F soil often sits motionless for weeks and can end up behind seed planted a month later. If a cold snap is coming after you plant, cover plants overnight with row cover or buckets.

5 mistakes that cost you a watermelon harvest

  1. Planting on the calendar, not the thermometer. Cold soil stunts the whole plant.
  2. Choosing a long-season variety in a short climate. A 100-day melon in zone 3 may never ripen.
  3. Crowding vines. Poor airflow invites disease, and plants compete for water.
  4. Keeping row cover on through bloom. Bees must reach the flowers for fruit to form.
  5. Overwatering near harvest. Heavy watering late can dilute sweetness and split fruit; ease off once melons reach full size.

Frequently asked questions

Can you grow watermelon in zone 3 or 4?

Yes, with effort. Choose icebox varieties that mature in 70–80 days, start seeds indoors, use black plastic mulch and transplant 2–3 weeks after the last frost. Expect smaller fruit than in the South.

How long does watermelon take to grow?

Standard watermelons need 80–100 days from planting to harvest, while small icebox types often ripen in about 70–80 days. Cool summers add a week or more.

How far apart should watermelon be planted?

Plant watermelon 2–3 feet apart in rows 6–8 feet apart. In hills, space hills 4–6 feet apart with two plants per hill.

Is it too late to plant watermelon in July?

In zones 6 and warmer, a transplant in early July can still ripen before frost if the variety is 85 days or less. In zones 3–5 it is usually too late.

Do watermelon seeds need to be soaked before planting?

It is not required. Soaking for 4–8 hours can speed germination in warm soil, but in cold soil it raises the risk of rot, so only soak if you are planting right away into soil above 70°F.

The bottom line

Plant watermelon 2–3 weeks after your last frost, when the soil is 70°F or warmer. In zones 3–5, start seeds indoors, use black plastic and pick a variety that ripens in 70–85 days. Look up your frost dates by ZIP code and use the planting calendar to set your own schedule.
