Quick answer

One cubic yard of gravel covers 108 square feet at 3 inches deep and weighs about 1.4 tons. To find your amount, multiply length × width in feet, multiply by the depth in inches, divide by 324 to get cubic yards, then multiply by 1.4 for tons. Add about 10% for compaction.

Gravel is sold three ways: by the ton, by the cubic yard and in bags. That makes ordering confusing, and mistakes are costly. A single cubic yard weighs well over a ton, so ordering the wrong unit can leave you short or paying delivery on a pile you never needed.

This guide gives you the formula, a coverage chart by depth, a tons and bags table, the right depth for paths, driveways and drainage, and a bulk-versus-bag comparison. If you would rather skip the math, the topsoil and gravel calculator converts everything between square feet, yards and tons.

Key takeaways
  • Formula: square feet × depth (in) ÷ 324 = cubic yards.
  • Weight: gravel weighs about 1.2–1.5 tons per cubic yard; use 1.4 for a safe estimate.
  • Coverage: 1 cubic yard covers 162 sq ft at 2 in, 108 at 3 in, 81 at 4 in and 54 at 6 in.
  • Depth: 2–3 in for paths, 4–6 in for driveways, and a thicker bed for drainage trenches.
  • Order 10% extra: loose gravel compacts once it is raked and driven on.

How do you calculate how much gravel you need?

The method is the same for any shape: find the area, multiply by depth and convert to the unit your supplier sells. Here is the process in four steps.

Step 1: Measure the area in square feet

For a rectangle, multiply length × width. A path 3 ft wide and 30 ft long is 90 sq ft. For a circle, use 3.14 × radius². For irregular shapes, split the area into rectangles and add them up.

Step 2: Choose your depth in inches

Depth depends on use. See the depth chart below for the usual range for each project.

Step 3: Convert to cubic yards

Multiply square feet by depth in inches, then divide by 324.

Example: 90 sq ft × 3 in ÷ 324 = 0.83 cubic yards.

Step 4: Convert to tons and add the extra

Multiply cubic yards by 1.4 for tons. Then add 10% for compaction.

Example: 0.83 × 1.4 = 1.17 tons. With 10% extra, order about 1.3 tons (0.92 cubic yards).

How deep should gravel be for each project?

Depth is a function of what will happen on top of the gravel. Foot traffic needs far less than vehicles.

ProjectTypical depthCommon stone
Garden path / mulch substitute2–3 inPea gravel or 3/8 in crushed stone
Patio or walkway (decorative)2–3 in over a compacted baseFines-binding crushed stone
Driveway4–6 in (more over soft soil)Crushed stone, 3/4 in
Base under pavers4–6 in compacted (more in cold or wet climates)Crushed, angular base stone
Drainage trench / French drainFills the trench, often 12 in or moreWashed 3/4 to 1.5 in stone
Around foundation / drip line2–3 inWashed river rock

Round, smooth stone such as pea gravel rolls underfoot and shifts, so it is a poor choice for a driveway. Angular crushed stone locks together and compacts firmly.

Compacted base matters more than top depth. A driveway built with 6 inches of gravel directly on soft topsoil will rut. Dig out soft soil, compact the subgrade and consider landscape fabric under the stone for a stable surface.

How much gravel do I need for a driveway or French drain?

Two examples show how quickly quantities grow on bigger projects.

  • Driveway: 12 ft wide × 40 ft long = 480 sq ft. At 4 inches deep that is 480 × 4 ÷ 324 = 5.93 cubic yards, or about 8.3 tons. With 10% extra, order about 6.5 cubic yards (9.1 tons).
  • French drain: a trench 1 ft wide × 2 ft deep × 50 ft long holds 100 cubic feet, which is 3.7 cubic yards. Order about 4.1 cubic yards with 10% extra, then subtract any volume the pipe takes up.

For a driveway, build up in layers: larger base stone first, then a smaller top layer, compacting each one. Local suppliers can recommend the right mix for your soil.

Is bagged or bulk gravel cheaper?

Bulk gravel is almost always cheaper per ton or yard, but delivery fees mean bags can win for tiny jobs. Using purely example prices of $5 per 0.5 cu ft bag, $55 per ton in bulk and a $75 delivery fee:

  • One cubic yard in bags: 54 bags × $5 = $270
  • One cubic yard in bulk: 1.4 tons × $55 + $75 = $152

Even for one yard, bulk wins in this example, and the gap grows with volume. Bags make sense only for jobs under about a quarter of a yard, or when you cannot take a delivery. Check local prices, which vary widely by stone type and region.

Will it fit in my truck?

Two cubic yards of gravel weigh about 2.8 tons, which is more than the payload of many half-ton pickups. Trucks are often rated around 1,500–2,000 lb of payload. Fill the bed only part-way, or have it delivered.

5 gravel mistakes that waste money

  1. Mixing up tons and yards. Always confirm which unit the quote is in, and ask for the supplier's weight per yard for your stone.
  2. Skipping the compaction allowance. Loose stone settles 10–15% once raked and driven on.
  3. Using pea gravel for vehicles. Round stone slides and rutting is constant.
  4. No edging. Gravel migrates into lawns and beds without a border.
  5. Laying it over weeds. Remove vegetation and consider fabric underneath to slow regrowth.

Frequently asked questions

How many tons should I order for one cubic yard of gravel?

A cubic yard of gravel weighs roughly 1.2–1.5 tons, depending on stone type, size and moisture. Using 1.4 tons per yard is a safe estimate for ordering.

How much area will one ton of gravel cover?

At 1.4 tons per cubic yard, one ton covers about 116 sq ft at 2 inches, 77 sq ft at 3 inches or 58 sq ft at 4 inches.

How much gravel do I need for a 10×10 area?

A 100 sq ft area needs about 0.62 cubic yards (0.86 tons) at 2 inches and 0.93 cubic yards (1.3 tons) at 3 inches. Add about 10% extra for compaction.

Do I need landscape fabric under gravel?

Fabric helps keep stone from sinking into soft soil and slows weeds, so it is useful for paths and driveways. It is not required, and it can trap debris over time, so use a quality woven product and a decent depth of stone.

What size gravel is best for a driveway?

Angular crushed stone around 3/4 inch, often with fine particles that bind it, makes a stable surface. Many installers use larger base stone below and a smaller top layer.

Is gravel cheaper than mulch?

Per cubic yard, gravel often costs more upfront than bulk mulch, but it lasts far longer and does not need annual refreshing. Over several years the difference narrows.

The bottom line

Multiply square feet by depth in inches and divide by 324 for cubic yards, then multiply by 1.4 for tons and add 10% for compaction. Use 2–3 inches for paths and 4–6 inches for driveways, and buy in bulk for anything bigger than a small project.
