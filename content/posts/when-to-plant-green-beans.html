Quick answer

Plant green beans 1–2 weeks after your last spring frost, when the soil has warmed to at least 60°F. Sow seed directly in the garden, 1 inch deep, because beans resent transplanting. Bush beans are ready in about 50–60 days; sow again every 2–3 weeks for a steady harvest until about 8–10 weeks before fall frost.

Beans are one of the easiest vegetables to grow, but they punish impatience. Seed sown into cold, wet soil rots instead of sprouting, and a late frost kills seedlings outright. Planting on the right date, not the earliest possible one, is most of the battle.

This guide gives the soil temperature rule, planting dates for USDA zones 3–10, the difference between bush and pole timing, and how to sow in succession so you aren't buried in beans for one week and empty-handed the next. To find dates for your exact location, check your frost dates by ZIP code and then use the planting calendar.

Key takeaways
  • When: 1–2 weeks after the average last frost, never into soil below 60°F.
  • Method: direct sow only, 1 inch deep, 2–4 inches apart (thin to 3–4 inches).
  • Timing: bush beans take about 50–60 days; pole beans take about 60–70 days.
  • Succession: sow bush beans every 2–3 weeks; stop 8–10 weeks before the first fall frost.
  • Frost: beans are tender. A light frost kills the leaves, so they should never go out early.

What soil temperature do green beans need?

Green beans need soil of at least 60°F at planting depth, and germinate fastest at roughly 70–80°F. Below 60°F, seed can sit for weeks and rot, especially in heavy, wet ground. The seed leaf is also fragile, so cold snaps after sprouting set plants back hard.

Soil temperature (2 in deep)What to expect
Under 55°FSeed rots; do not plant
55–60°FVery slow and patchy germination
60–65°FAcceptable; sprouts in 10–14 days
70–80°FIdeal; sprouts in about 6–10 days
Over 90°FFlowers drop and pods fail to set

A soil thermometer costs only a few dollars. Check the soil at about 8 a.m. for three mornings in a row; if it's 60°F or higher, you're clear to plant. In cold-slow spots, covering a bed with clear plastic for a week warms it a few degrees.

Should you start green beans indoors?

No. Beans have a taproot-like root system that hates disturbance, and they grow so fast from seed that starting indoors saves little time. Direct sowing gives sturdier plants. The only exception is a short-season zone 3 or 4 garden where you want a head start, in which case sow into peat-free biodegradable pots only 2 weeks before transplanting and handle the roots gently.

If you want an early crop, protect the planting with a floating row cover instead. It adds a few degrees of frost protection and keeps soil warmer.

When do bush beans and pole beans differ?

Both types are planted at the same time in spring, but they differ in how long they take and how long they produce.

How do you plant green beans step by step?

  1. Pick a sunny spot. Beans need at least 6–8 hours of direct sun and well-drained soil.
  2. Skip heavy nitrogen. Beans fix their own nitrogen. Mix in a little compost, but avoid high-nitrogen fertilizer, which produces leaves instead of pods.
  3. Check the soil temperature. Wait for 60°F or more.
  4. Sow seeds 1 inch deep, 2–4 inches apart in rows 18–24 inches apart (1.5 inches deep in sandy soil). For pole beans, plant 4–6 seeds around each support.
  5. Water once after planting, then keep soil lightly moist. Soggy soil rots seed.
  6. Thin to 3–4 inches once seedlings have their first true leaves. Snip rather than pull, to protect neighbors.
  7. Mulch with straw once plants are about 4 inches tall.

How do you sow green beans in succession?

A single bush bean planting yields heavily for only two to three weeks. To harvest all summer, plant a new short row every 2–3 weeks from your first sowing date until the cutoff date for your zone. The cutoff is roughly 8–10 weeks before the first fall frost (bush beans need about 55–60 days plus a few weeks of picking).

For example, in zone 6 the first sowing falls around May 2. Planting every 3 weeks gives sowings on about May 2, May 23, Jun 13, Jul 4 and Jul 25, with the last one safe through about Aug 25. That's five harvest waves from a small footprint.

Beans are also useful for filling space after early crops. Once spring peas or lettuce are finished, bush beans can follow in the same bed. They enrich the soil as they grow.

What are the most common green bean planting mistakes?

Planting too early is the top mistake. One cold, wet week can rot every seed in the row, and a single frost kills seedlings that have already sprouted. If a late frost is forecast after you have planted, cover the row overnight with a sheet or row cover.
  1. Planting in cold soil. Wait for 60°F, even if the calendar says go.
  2. Seed too deep. Beyond 1.5 inches, the seedling struggles to push up, especially in clay.
  3. Overwatering after sowing. Water once, then let the top inch dry.
  4. Too much nitrogen. You'll get lush vines and few beans.
  5. Skipping succession sowing. One planting means one big flush, then little else.
  6. Working wet foliage. Picking or weeding while leaves are wet can spread bean rust and other diseases between plants.

Frequently asked questions

How late can you plant green beans?

Bush green beans can be sown until roughly 8–10 weeks before your first fall frost, because they need about 50–60 days to produce. In zone 6, that means late August; in zone 4, early August.

Can I soak bean seeds before planting?

It is best not to. Bean seed soaked for more than a few hours can crack and rot, and soaked seed is especially prone to damage in cool soil. Plant dry seed into moist, warm soil instead.

How long do green beans take to sprout?

Green beans usually sprout in 6–10 days in 70–80°F soil. In cooler soil near 60°F, expect closer to 10–14 days.

How many bean plants do I need per person?

For fresh eating, about 10–15 bush bean plants per person is a common planning figure. For freezing or canning, double it. Sowing in succession smooths the supply.

Do green beans come back every year?

No. Green beans are annuals, so they finish after one season and must be reseeded each year. Rotate them to a different bed every year or two to cut down on soil-borne disease.

Can green beans grow in partial shade?

They tolerate light shade but produce best with 6–8 hours of direct sun. In hot southern gardens, afternoon shade can help keep flowers from dropping in summer heat.

The bottom line

Wait until 1–2 weeks after your last frost and 60°F soil, sow beans straight into the garden 1 inch deep, and keep sowing every 2–3 weeks until about 8–10 weeks before your first fall frost. To get dates tailored to your ZIP code, open the planting calendar.
