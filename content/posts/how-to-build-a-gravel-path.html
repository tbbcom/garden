Quick answer

To build a gravel path, dig about 5 inches deep, lay landscape fabric, add 3 inches of compacted crushed stone and finish with 2 inches of gravel topping, held in by edging. A path 3 ft wide and 30 ft long (90 sq ft) takes about 1.4 tons of base stone and 0.8 tons of topping, around 2.2 tons in all.

A gravel path is one of the cheapest, most forgiving hardscape projects. It drains water, it isn't hard to curve around trees and beds, and you can finish it in a weekend with a shovel, a wheelbarrow and a hand tamper.

The difference between a path that stays crisp for years and one that turns into a weedy mud strip is the layers underneath. This guide gives the depths, the materials, a quantity chart and the steps. For your own length and width, use the topsoil and gravel calculator.

Key takeaways
  • Width: 3 ft for one person, 4 ft so two can walk side by side, 5 ft for a wheelbarrow.
  • Layers: 3 in compacted base + 2 in topping, so dig 5 in.
  • Material: angular crushed stone for the base and topping; rounded pea gravel shifts underfoot.
  • Edging: required on both sides, or the gravel migrates into the lawn and beds.
  • Quantity: a 3 × 30 ft path needs about 1.0 cu yd of base (loose) and 0.6 cu yd of topping.

What materials do you need for a gravel path?

  • Crushed stone for the base: 3/4 inch angular stone, sometimes sold as "3/4 minus" or road base, which includes fine particles that lock together when compacted.
  • Gravel for the top layer: 3/8 to 3/4 inch crushed stone in the color you like. Small, angular stone is more comfortable and stays put better than large rounded pieces.
  • Landscape fabric (woven or non-woven), to keep soil and weeds from mixing into the stone.
  • Edging: steel, aluminum, plastic, brick or stone. See our mulch and edging planning tool for length estimates around beds.
  • Tools: flat spade, rake, wheelbarrow, hand tamper (or a rented plate compactor for long paths), a level and string line.

How deep should a gravel path be?

A footpath that only carries foot traffic does well with 3 inches of compacted base and 1–2 inches of topping, so you dig roughly 4–5 inches. If the soil is clay or stays wet, go deeper on the base, up to 4–6 inches, so water can drain through. Paths that carry carts or light vehicles need a much thicker base, which is a different project.

How do you build a gravel path, step by step?

Step 1: Call 811 and mark the route

In the US, call 811 a few days before you dig so utility lines can be marked for free. Then lay a garden hose or rope along the route and adjust the curves until it looks right from the house and from the path's far end.

Step 2: Excavate 5 inches

Cut along the marked line with a flat spade and remove the sod and soil to a depth of about 5 inches (more if you're using a deeper base). Keep the bottom level across the width, and slope it very slightly so water runs off: about 1/8 to 1/4 inch per foot across the path, or along it, toward a lower area.

Step 3: Compact the soil and install the edging

Tamp the bare soil until it's firm. Install the edging along both sides with the top at your finished gravel level, 1/2 inch or so below the lawn if the path is next to turf so the mower can pass.

Step 4: Lay landscape fabric

Roll the fabric over the soil, overlapping seams by about 6 inches and tucking the edges against the edging. With a 3 ft path, a 30 ft run needs about 99 sq ft with a 10% allowance.

Step 5: Add and compact the base

Spread 3 inches of crushed stone in two lifts, wetting lightly and tamping each one until it's firm and doesn't shift underfoot. A hand tamper works for short runs; rent a plate compactor for anything over about 30 ft.

Step 6: Add the topping

Rake 1–2 inches of topping gravel over the base to the finished level. Light compaction with the tamper settles it. Gravel is meant to move a little, so keep a bucket of spare stone to top up the low spots.

Gravel path edging and weeds: what actually works?

Edging does two jobs: it holds the gravel in and keeps lawn and bed edges out. Steel and aluminum give crisp lines and flex around curves. Brick or stone looks traditional and lasts. Plastic is the cheapest and the first to heave or bow.

No fabric is weed-proof forever, because wind-blown seeds sprout in the gravel itself. Pull seedlings when they're small, rake the topping every season or two and don't put soil or mulch on the path. A shallow layer of coarse stone (1–2 inches) stays drier and grows fewer weeds than a deep one.

Skip the pea gravel for a walking surface. Rounded stones act like ball bearings: they roll underfoot, migrate off the path and make wheelbarrows and strollers hard to push. Angular crushed stone locks together and stays put.

What are the most common gravel path mistakes?

  1. No base. Gravel poured straight on soil sinks within a season and mixes with the dirt.
  2. No edging. The gravel creeps into the lawn and the mower throws stones.
  3. Flat grade in a wet spot. Water pools and the base turns soft. Give it a slight slope or add drainage.
  4. Too deep a topping. More than 2 inches of loose stone is tiring to walk on.
  5. Underordering. Loose stone compacts. Order about 20% extra for the base layer.

How much does a gravel path cost to build?

Material cost depends on your local stone prices, so plug your own numbers into the quantities above. Using assumed example prices of $40 per ton for the base stone and $55 per ton for decorative topping, a 3 × 30 ft path needs 1.4 tons of base ($56) and 0.8 tons of topping ($44), about $100 in stone. Fabric for 99 sq ft at $0.25 per sq ft adds about $25, and 60 ft of edging at $2 to $6 per foot adds $120 to $360.

These are example prices, not quotes. Delivery fees, tool rental and disposal of the soil you dig out (about 1.4 cubic yards for this path) come on top. Even so, a gravel path usually costs far less per square foot than pavers or concrete.

Frequently asked questions

How wide should a garden gravel path be?

Three feet suits one person, four feet allows two people to walk together and five feet fits a wheelbarrow comfortably. Narrower than 2 feet feels cramped.

Do you need landscape fabric under a gravel path?

It's recommended. Fabric keeps the base stone from sinking into the soil and slows weeds from below, though weeds can still sprout from seeds that land in the gravel.

What is the best gravel for a walking path?

Angular crushed stone in small sizes, around 3/8 to 3/4 inch. It compacts and locks together, so it's more stable underfoot than round pea gravel.

How much does a ton of gravel cover on a path?

A ton of gravel is about 0.7 cubic yards, which covers roughly 115 sq ft at 2 inches deep. That is based on the planning figure of 1.4 tons per cubic yard, so confirm weight with your supplier.

How do you keep a gravel path from washing out on a slope?

Use a thicker, well-compacted base, choose angular stone, and add cross-slope or small check dams. Very steep paths need steps or a different surface.

The bottom line

Dig about 5 inches, lay fabric, compact 3 inches of crushed stone, finish with 1–2 inches of topping and edge both sides. Order about 2.2 tons for a 3 × 30 ft path and use the topsoil and gravel calculator to size your own.
