Quick answer

To level ground for a patio or shed base, stake out the area, run level string lines, cut the high spots and fill the low ones with compacted gravel. Patios should slope about 1/4 inch per foot away from the house, so a 10 ft patio drops 2.5 inches. A shed pad should be level, on 4–6 inches of compacted gravel.

Almost no backyard is flat. A patio or shed set on uneven ground will rock, crack or collect water, and once it's built, the only fix is to tear it up. A few hours with stakes and string at the start saves that.

This guide shows how to find out how far off level you are, how to cut and fill without causing settling, and how much gravel the base needs. For quantities, use the topsoil and gravel calculator.

Key takeaways
  • Patio slope: 1/4 inch of fall per foot, away from buildings (a 12 ft run drops 3 in).
  • Shed pad: as level as you can get it, with a 4–6 inch compacted gravel base extending about a foot past the footprint.
  • Cut rather than fill: dig down into firm soil on the high side where you can; fill settles.
  • Compact in lifts: spread fill in layers of about 4 inches and tamp each one.
  • Quantity: a 10 × 12 ft shed pad at 6 inches deep needs about 3.7 tons of crushed stone.

How do you check whether your ground is level?

Don't trust your eyes. A yard that looks flat can have a 6 inch drop across 12 feet. Use one of these methods.

  • String and line level: drive a stake at each end of the site, tie a mason string at the height of the highest ground and hang a line level on it. Pull it taut and measure from the string down to the ground at several points.
  • Straight board and level: lay a long straight 2 × 4 on the ground, set a 4 ft level on top and shim until the bubble centers. The gap under the board shows the dip.
  • Water level or laser level: faster across big areas. Rental stores and hardware stores carry rotary and line lasers.

Measure at the corners and the middle, write the numbers down and you have a map of the high and low spots.

How much slope should a patio have?

A patio needs a slight slope so rain runs off instead of pooling or heading toward the foundation. The usual rule is about 1/4 inch of fall per foot (roughly 2%), sloping away from the house. Shed pads and the areas beneath them should be as close to level as you can manage, since the shed floor must sit flat.

How do you level ground, step by step?

  1. Call 811. Have utilities marked before digging. It's free in the US.
  2. Outline the site. Stake the corners, 6–12 inches wider than the finished patio or shed footprint, and run string.
  3. Set the finished height. Choose the top of the finished surface, mark it on the stakes and run level or sloped string lines at that height.
  4. Remove sod and topsoil. Strip the grass and the soft top 3–4 inches, since organic soil settles and holds water.
  5. Cut the high side. Dig down to the required depth, measuring from the string. Work down to firm soil, not loose fill.
  6. Fill the low side with gravel. Add crushed stone in layers of about 4 inches and compact each with a hand tamper or plate compactor. Don't fill with topsoil, which keeps settling.
  7. Screed the surface. Lay two parallel pipes at the right height, fill between them and drag a straight board across to smooth the base. Compact again.
  8. Recheck. Measure from the strings and use the level. Fix any hump or dip now, because it won't be fixable later.

What if the slope is steep?

If the ground drops more than about a foot across the site, filling the low side gets expensive and unstable. Consider cutting a terrace into the hill and holding the uphill side with a small retaining wall, or choosing a smaller or elevated deck instead. Taller walls and big grade changes can require permits or an engineer, so check local rules.

Don't level with loose topsoil. Topsoil and fill dirt settle over the first year or two, leaving dips and tilted pavers. Use compacted crushed stone for any fill under a patio or shed.

What are the most common leveling mistakes?

  1. Leveling by eye. Always use a string line and level, and recheck after each step.
  2. Making the patio perfectly flat. It needs about 1/4 inch per foot of fall away from the house.
  3. Leaving organic soil. Roots and topsoil rot and settle, so strip them before building up.
  4. Compacting one thick layer. Do it in lifts of about 4 inches.
  5. Forgetting drainage. Pools of water near the foundation or under the pad cause the problems you were trying to avoid.

What tools make leveling easier?

You can level a small site with basic tools, and a few rentals make bigger jobs faster. Here's a practical kit.

  • Flat spade and a square-point shovel for cutting and moving soil.
  • Mason string, stakes and a line level to set the finished height.
  • A 4 ft level and a long straight 2 × 4 for checking and screeding.
  • Hand tamper for small pads; a plate compactor, rented by the day, for anything bigger.
  • Wheelbarrow and rake to move and spread gravel evenly.
  • Marking paint or flags to show the high and low spots you measured.

Mark every spot where you measured and write the depth of cut or fill beside it. Working from a simple map prevents over-digging, which is the most common reason a base ends up needing extra gravel.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best way to level a yard for a shed?

Stake the footprint, strip the sod, cut the high side down and fill the low side with compacted crushed stone. Finish with a screeded 4–6 inch gravel pad about a foot larger than the shed.

Can I level ground with sand?

Sand works only as a thin 1 inch bedding layer under pavers. As the main fill it washes out and shifts, so use compacted crushed stone for anything thicker.

How do I level ground without a laser level?

Use stakes, mason string and a line level, or a long straight board with a 4 ft level on top. Both are accurate enough for a patio or shed base.

Should a patio be perfectly level?

No. A patio should slope about 1/4 inch per foot away from the house so water drains. A shed pad, which has to support a floor, should be level.

How far past the shed should the gravel pad extend?

About 6–12 inches on each side keeps water from pooling at the edges and gives you room to work. Always check your shed maker's instructions for base size.

The bottom line

Stake and string the site, cut the high side, fill the low with compacted crushed stone in lifts and slope patios 1/4 inch per foot away from the house. Aim for a flat, 4–6 inch gravel pad under a shed. Get your quantities from the topsoil and gravel calculator before you order.
