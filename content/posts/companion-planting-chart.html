Quick answer

Companion planting works best when the pairing has a clear reason: flowers that feed beneficial insects, plants that share space without competing, or crops that shouldn't share diseases. Good bets include tomatoes with basil and marigolds, carrots with onions, and corn with beans and squash. Bad neighbors include fennel, black walnut trees and potatoes next to tomatoes.

Most companion planting charts read as fact when much of the advice is garden folklore. Some pairings have solid science behind them, some have a plausible mechanism but little testing, and some are simply habit. Knowing which is which saves you from rearranging a bed for no benefit.

This chart rates each pairing by how strong the evidence is, explains the reasoning in plain English, and shows which combinations to avoid. When you're ready to place plants, the plant spacing calculator will tell you how many of each fit in your bed.

Key takeaways
  • Strongest evidence: flowers that attract beneficial insects, three sisters planting and keeping tomatoes away from black walnut.
  • Mixed evidence: carrot and onion pairs, nasturtium as an aphid trap, marigolds against nematodes (works best as a full-season cover crop).
  • Mostly folklore: many "tomatoes taste better beside basil" style claims.
  • Always safe: space plants to their mature size, and rotate crop families each year.

What is companion planting and does it really work?

Companion planting is growing different plants close together so they help each other. The help can take several forms: attracting predators and pollinators, masking or confusing pests, shading soil, supporting climbers, or taking different nutrients from different soil depths. A few of these mechanisms are well documented. Many specific pairs have never been tested in controlled trials.

University extension services generally give a measured answer: diverse plantings tend to have fewer pest outbreaks than a single crop, and flowers that feed beneficial insects help. But no pairing replaces good spacing, watering, rotation and scouting. Treat companion planting as a bonus, not a pest-control plan.

Juglone is real. Black walnut roots, and to a lesser extent butternut and hickory, release juglone into the soil, and tomatoes, peppers, potatoes and eggplant are among the most sensitive plants. If you have a walnut, grow these crops in raised beds with imported soil or in containers well away from the root zone.

What are the three sisters and how do you plant them?

The most famous companion planting system is the three sisters of corn, climbing beans and squash, developed by Native American peoples and still grown today. Corn gives the beans something to climb, beans add nitrogen to the soil through their root nodules over time, and big squash leaves shade the soil and reduce weeds.

  1. Prepare a mound about 12 inches across and 6 inches high, spaced 3 to 4 feet from the next mound, after soil reaches 60°F or higher.
  2. Plant 4 to 6 corn seeds in the mound and let them grow to about 6 inches tall.
  3. Add 4 pole bean seeds around the corn once the stalks are 6 inches tall.
  4. Add 2 squash seeds at the edge of the mound a week or two later, letting the vines spread outward.

The system needs space and doesn't fit a small raised bed. In small gardens, use a trellis for climbing beans or cucumbers and let compact squash sprawl at the base.

Do marigolds really keep pests away?

This is one of the most repeated claims in gardening, and the truth is narrower than the folklore. Research on French marigolds (Tagetes patula) shows they can reduce root-knot nematodes in soil when grown as a dense cover crop for a full season and then tilled in. A few marigolds at the end of a tomato row don't do the same job. Marigolds do attract pollinators and some predators, which is a useful, if more modest, benefit.

What really helps pest control in a garden?

If you want the results people claim from companion planting, these practices are better supported than most plant pairings:

  • Grow a patch of small flowers (sweet alyssum, dill, cilantro, yarrow, calendula) so beneficial insects have nectar all season.
  • Rotate crop families each year, which interrupts disease and pest cycles.
  • Space plants properly for airflow and light.
  • Use row cover over brassicas and squash early in the season to block egg-laying insects.
  • Scout regularly and remove pests by hand when numbers are low.

How do you use companion planting in a small bed?

Start with a main crop, then fit companions into the gaps rather than crowding the main plant. In a 4 × 8 bed, you might plant staked tomatoes on the north side with basil at their feet, onions or carrots in the next row and a border of alyssum and marigolds around the outside. Use the same spacing you would for each crop on its own, because companions do not make plants immune to crowding.

Keep a simple garden map each year. It makes crop rotation easy and shows which combinations did well in your conditions. Match planting dates with the planting calendar so companions are ready at the same time.

Frequently asked questions

What should not be planted next to tomatoes?

Keep tomatoes away from potatoes, which share blight, and from black walnut trees, whose roots release juglone. Fennel and brassicas such as cabbage and broccoli are also commonly advised against as neighbors.

Does basil really improve tomatoes?

There is little scientific evidence that basil improves tomato flavor. Basil is a good neighbor mainly because it is compact, enjoys the same sun and watering, and its flowers attract pollinators.

What are the best companion plants for vegetables in general?

Small-flowered plants like sweet alyssum, dill, cilantro, calendula and yarrow are the best all-round companions because they feed hoverflies and parasitic wasps, which eat aphids and caterpillar pests.

Can you plant cucumbers and tomatoes together?

You can, as long as each has enough space and support. Tomatoes and cucumbers like similar sun and water, but cucumbers sprawl, so grow them on a trellis to keep them from shading the tomatoes.

Do onions repel carrot flies?

Some trials suggest that interplanting onions or leeks with carrots can reduce carrot rust fly damage, but results are mixed. Floating row cover is a more reliable control.

Is companion planting an organic pest control method?

It is one organic tool, best used together with crop rotation, row covers and healthy soil. It rarely controls a heavy infestation on its own.

The bottom line

The best companion plants are the ones with a clear job: flowers for beneficial insects, climbers that use their neighbors for support, and crops kept apart from disease and black walnut roots.
