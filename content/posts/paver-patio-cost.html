Quick answer

A DIY 12 × 12 ft paver patio costs roughly $965 in materials and tool rental, about $6.70 per square foot, using the example prices in this guide. Professional installation costs more because it adds labor, excavation and overhead. Your own prices will differ by region, so check local quotes.

If you're pricing a paver patio, you'll find wildly different numbers online. The reason is simple: cost depends on paver choice, base depth, how much soil has to be dug out and hauled, and whether you or a crew do the work. Instead of quoting a number you can't check, this guide shows how to build your own estimate line by line.

The worked example uses clearly labeled assumed prices for a 12 × 12 ft patio. Swap in prices from your local supplier, or let the paver patio calculator handle the quantities.

How much does a professional paver patio cost?

A contractor's price covers everything above plus labor, equipment, excavation and haul-away, overhead and profit. Quotes vary widely by region, patio size, design complexity and the paver you pick, and they change with the season and local demand, so there is no single figure that fits everyone.

The reliable way to compare is per square foot, with the scope spelled out. As a purely hypothetical illustration, a quote of $18 per square foot for 144 sq ft would be $2,592, nearly three times the DIY material cost in the example. That gap pays for crew labor, equipment and a workmanship guarantee. It isn't automatically a rip-off, and the cheapest quote isn't automatically the best.

What to make every contractor itemize

  • Base depth and material: 4 inches versus 6 inches of compacted stone is a big quality difference.
  • The paver product: brand, size, thickness and cost per square foot.
  • Excavation and disposal: whether hauling away roughly 4 cubic yards of soil is included.
  • Edge restraint and jointing sand type.
  • Drainage and slope work, if the yard is uneven or wet.
  • Warranty terms, in writing.

Is DIY or professional installation cheaper?

DIY is cheaper in cash, but only if you value your time at close to nothing and own or can rent the tools. A first-time 12 × 12 ft patio takes most people a long weekend or two, because digging, hauling and compacting are hard work.

A common middle path: hire a pro for excavation and base, then lay the pavers yourself, or the reverse. The base is the part most likely to fail and the heaviest to move, so paying for it is where hiring pays off most.

How can you lower the cost of a paver patio?

  1. Size it to the paver. A patio that's a multiple of the paver dimensions needs few cuts. A 12 × 12 ft patio in 12 × 12 in pavers needs exactly 144 and almost no waste.
  2. Choose a simple pattern. Running bond wastes less than herringbone at 45°.
  3. Compare bulk to bagged materials. Buying stone and sand by the ton from a landscape yard is usually much cheaper per pound than bagged material, though delivery adds a fee.
  4. Reuse excavated soil. Spread it to fix low spots in the lawn or use it in a raised bed, if it's good soil, to avoid disposal fees.
  5. Get three itemized quotes. Prices for the same scope can differ a lot between contractors.
Don't cut costs on the base. Thinner base, skipped compaction or no edge restraint all save money on day one and cost far more when the patio sinks. Save on the paver choice or the pattern instead.

When does a paver patio cost more than you expect?

Surprises usually come from the site, not the pavers. Roots, rock or buried debris slow digging. Clay soil that holds water may call for a deeper base. A steep yard may need a small retaining wall, and a long carry from the driveway to the backyard adds labor if you're paying a crew or wheeling stone yourself.

Delivery is the other common add-on. Bulk stone and sand arrive by truck and are dumped in one pile, often on your driveway, and many suppliers charge a separate delivery fee. A tarp, plywood to protect the driveway and a plan for moving 5 tons of material in a day or two are part of the real project cost. Build 10–15% contingency into the budget so a surprise doesn't stall the job halfway.

Frequently asked questions

How much does a 12x12 paver patio cost?

With the example prices in this guide, a DIY 12 × 12 ft patio is about $965 in materials and rental. A professional installation will cost more because it includes labor, excavation and overhead; get itemized local quotes.

Is a paver patio cheaper than concrete?

It depends on the finish. Basic poured concrete is often cheaper to install than pavers, but pavers can be repaired by lifting individual units and are less likely to crack as a single slab.

How much do pavers cost per square foot?

Pavers alone range from basic concrete at a few dollars per square foot to premium or natural stone at several times that. This guide's example uses $3 per square foot; check your local supplier.

What is the cheapest way to build a patio?

A small, rectangular patio of basic concrete pavers in a simple pattern, built yourself on a well-drained site, is typically the lowest-cost paver option. Gravel patios cost less still but are a different surface.

Does the cost include digging out the soil?

The DIY table doesn't include a soil disposal fee, although it assumes you do the digging. A 12 × 12 ft patio excavated 9 3/8 in deep produces about 4.2 cubic yards of soil, so ask whether contractors include haul-away.

The bottom line

A small DIY paver patio can come in under $1,000 in materials and rental at the example prices, with the paver choice and base depth driving most of the cost. Itemize every quote, never skimp on the base and check the quantities with the paver patio calculator before you buy.
