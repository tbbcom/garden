Quick answer

Cubic yards = length (ft) × width (ft) × depth (ft) ÷ 27. If depth is in inches, use square feet × depth in inches ÷ 324. For example, a 10 × 12 ft area covered 4 inches deep is 120 × 4 ÷ 324 = 1.48 cubic yards. A cubic yard of soil, mulch or gravel is 27 cubic feet.

Bulk soil, mulch, sand and gravel are all sold by the cubic yard, yet we measure garden space in feet and depth in inches. That mismatch is where orders go wrong. One conversion, 27, and one shortcut, 324, solve it.

This page walks through the formulas with worked examples for rectangles, circles, triangles and trenches, then gives a quick-reference chart and the conversion to tons for gravel. For fast answers on any project, use the topsoil and gravel calculator.

Key takeaways
  • Feet formula: length × width × depth (all in feet) ÷ 27 = cubic yards.
  • Inches shortcut: square feet × depth in inches ÷ 324 = cubic yards.
  • One cubic yard: 27 cubic feet, which covers 324 sq ft at 1 inch deep or 108 sq ft at 3 inches.

What is a cubic yard?

A cubic yard is a cube that measures 3 feet on each side: 3 × 3 × 3 = 27 cubic feet. In visual terms, it is about the size of a washing machine or a small fridge lying down. It is also roughly 4.5 wheelbarrow loads (a 6 cu ft barrow) or 6.75 loads in a 4 cu ft barrow.

Cubic yards are the standard unit for bulk landscape materials in the US, including topsoil, compost, mulch, sand and gravel. Bagged products are sold in cubic feet, so you need to convert between the two.

How do you calculate cubic yards step by step?

  1. Measure length and width of the area in feet. For irregular shapes, see below.
  2. Choose a depth and convert to feet by dividing inches by 12 (3 inches = 0.25 ft; 4 inches = 0.33 ft).
  3. Multiply length × width × depth to get cubic feet.
  4. Divide by 27 to get cubic yards.
  5. Add 5–10% and round up.

Example: a 10 × 12 ft area at 4 inches deep. 10 × 12 × 0.333 = 40 cubic feet. 40 ÷ 27 = 1.48 cubic yards. With 10% extra, 1.63, so order 1.75 or 2 cubic yards, depending on the supplier's increments.

What is the quick formula using square feet and inches?

Because 12 inches per foot × 27 cubic feet per yard = 324, you can skip the depth conversion: square feet × inches ÷ 324 = cubic yards. The same 10 × 12 ft area at 4 inches: 120 × 4 ÷ 324 = 1.48. The same answer, one fewer step.

How do you measure irregular shapes?

  • Circle: area = 3.14 × radius × radius. A circle 12 ft across has a 6 ft radius: 3.14 × 36 = 113 sq ft. At 3 inches deep: 113 × 3 ÷ 324 = 1.05 cubic yards.
  • Triangle: area = base × height ÷ 2.
  • L-shape or complex layout: split into rectangles and add. A 12 × 10 ft section plus an 8 × 6 ft section = 120 + 48 = 168 sq ft.
  • Curved bed: take three or four widths along its length, average them and multiply by the length.
  • Trench or footing: length × width × depth. A 30 ft trench 1 ft wide and 1 ft deep is 30 cubic feet = 1.11 cubic yards.
  • Sloped or uneven ground: measure the depth at several points and use the average.

How do you convert cubic yards to tons?

Gravel, crushed stone and sand are often sold by the ton, so you need weight. A common rule of thumb is about 1.4 tons per cubic yard for crushed stone and gravel, which is where you get the 1 ton ≈ 0.71 cubic yards figure. Actual density varies with stone size, moisture and type (roughly 1.2–1.5 tons per cubic yard), so always ask the supplier for their conversion.

Example: a gravel path 20 ft long, 3 ft wide and 3 inches deep: 60 sq ft × 3 ÷ 324 = 0.56 cubic yards. × 1.4 = 0.78 tons. In practice, round up to 1 ton.

How many bags equal a cubic yard?

  • 1 cubic foot bags: 27 bags
  • 1.5 cubic foot bags: 18 bags
  • 2 cubic foot bags: 13.5 bags
  • 3 cubic foot bags: 9 bags

Bags of gravel or sand are often sold by weight (40 or 50 lb) rather than volume, so use tons instead: a ton is 2,000 lb, or 40–50 of those bags.

Common mistakes when calculating cubic yards

The most expensive mistake is the depth unit. Mixing feet and inches turns a 4 inch layer into a 4 foot one, a 12-fold error. Convert inches to feet (divide by 12) or use the 324 shortcut, and never mix the two.
  1. Forgetting 27. Dividing cubic feet by 3 (feet in a yard) instead of 27 gives a result 9 times too high.
  2. Rounding down. Always round up; running short means a second delivery fee.
  3. Ignoring compaction. Gravel and road base compact 10–20% when tamped, soil settles, and mulch fluffs.
  4. Measuring the wrong depth. Use the finished depth, not the depth of a layer that is already there.
  5. Ignoring obstacles. Subtract areas you will not fill, like a tree trunk circle or a paved patch.

Frequently asked questions

How many cubic feet are in a cubic yard?

There are 27 cubic feet in a cubic yard, because a cubic yard is 3 ft × 3 ft × 3 ft. Divide cubic feet by 27 to convert to cubic yards.

How do I convert square feet to cubic yards?

Multiply the square footage by the depth in inches, then divide by 324. For 500 sq ft at 3 inches deep: 500 × 3 ÷ 324 = 4.63 cubic yards.

How many wheelbarrows are in a cubic yard?

A standard 6 cubic foot wheelbarrow holds about 0.22 cubic yards, so a yard takes 4.5 full loads. A 4 cubic foot barrow needs nearly 7.

How much does a cubic yard of gravel weigh?

Roughly 1.4 tons, or about 2,800 pounds, for typical crushed stone or gravel. It varies with stone size and moisture, so confirm with your supplier.

How many square feet does a cubic yard cover?

A cubic yard covers 324 sq ft at 1 inch deep, 162 at 2 inches, 108 at 3 inches and 81 at 4 inches.

How much does a pickup truck hold in cubic yards?

A full-size pickup with a 6.5 ft bed holds about 2 cubic yards of light material like mulch, but far less of heavy material. A yard of gravel can approach the payload of a half-ton truck.
