Quick answer

For a typical patio, lay 4–6 inches of compacted gravel base topped with 1 inch of bedding sand. Use 6 inches or more for driveways, soft or clay soil, and freeze-thaw climates. A 12 × 12 ft patio with a 6 inch base needs about 3.2 cubic yards (4.5 tons) of crushed stone and 0.6 tons of sand.

The base is the part of a paver patio nobody sees and the part that decides whether it lasts. Pavers don't fail on their own; they sink, tilt and heave when the base underneath was too thin, too wet or not compacted. Get the depth right and a patio stays flat for decades.

Below you'll find the recommended depth for each use, how much gravel and sand to order by patio size, and how deep to dig. For your own dimensions, the paver patio calculator returns the quantities directly.

Key takeaways
  • Walkways and patios: 4–6 in of compacted crushed stone; add depth on clay soil or in cold climates.
  • Driveways: 8–12 in of base, since they carry vehicle loads.
  • Bedding sand: 1 in, screeded and never compacted before the pavers go on.
  • Dig depth: base + 1 in sand + paver thickness. A 6 in base with 2 3/8 in pavers means digging about 9 3/8 in.
  • Order extra: loose stone compacts by roughly 20%, so buy about 1.2 times the finished volume.

How deep should a paver base be?

Paver base depth depends on what the surface carries and what's underneath it. The ranges below reflect typical guidance from the paver industry. Always follow the installation instructions for the product you use, and check any local frost or soil conditions.

If soil is very soft or stays wet, a thicker base won't help on its own. Fix drainage first, and consider asking a local landscape supplier or a contractor about a geotextile layer between soil and gravel.

What kind of gravel goes under pavers?

Use an angular, crushed aggregate that locks together when compacted, sold under names like "crushed stone," "road base," "dense-graded aggregate" or "3/4 inch minus." The fine particles mixed in among the larger pieces are what make it compact into a solid layer.

Avoid rounded pea gravel or river rock for a base. The pieces roll over each other and never lock, so the surface keeps shifting. Ask your supplier for the material they recommend for paver bases in your region, since names vary.

How much sand goes under pavers?

Bedding sand is a thin, even leveling layer, about 1 inch thick. It's not the structure; the gravel is. Use coarse, washed concrete sand rather than fine play sand or stone dust, which hold water and drain poorly.

Screed the sand flat, then lay the pavers on top without walking on the sand. Joint sand, which is swept into the gaps afterward, is a separate (and much smaller) quantity.

How deep do you dig for a paver patio?

Dig deep enough for the base, the sand and the paver itself, so the finished surface ends up where you want it, usually 1 inch or so above surrounding soil in your lawn so water runs off.

Dig depth = base depth + 1 in sand + paver thickness. Typical residential pavers are about 2 3/8 inches (60 mm) thick, so:

  • 4 in base: 4 + 1 + 2.375 = 7 3/8 in
  • 6 in base: 6 + 1 + 2.375 = 9 3/8 in
  • 8 in base: 8 + 1 + 2.375 = 11 3/8 in

Excavate 6–12 inches beyond the patio edge on each side so the base extends under the edge restraint. For a 12 × 12 ft patio dug 9 3/8 inches deep, expect about 4.2 cubic yards of soil to move or haul away.

How do you build the base, step by step?

  1. Call 811 first. Utility locates are free in the US and required before digging in most states.
  2. Mark and excavate. Stake the outline, run string lines and dig to your calculated depth, sloping the subgrade about 1/4 inch per foot away from the house.
  3. Compact the soil. Run a plate compactor over the bare subgrade until it feels firm, and replace any soft spots with gravel.
  4. Add gravel in 2–3 inch layers (lifts). Spread, wet lightly and compact each lift before adding the next. Compacting a single deep layer leaves loose stone underneath.
  5. Check the slope. Use a level and string line to confirm the base slopes the way you intended before any sand goes down.
  6. Screed the bedding sand. Lay two parallel pipes on the base, fill between them with 1 inch of sand and drag a straight board across. Pull the pipes out and fill the grooves.
  7. Lay pavers and edge them. Install the edge restraint, compact the pavers, sweep in joint sand and compact again.
Don't use the sand as a thick leveling fix. Sand layers deeper than about 1 inch settle unevenly and show up as dips. If the base is uneven, correct it with gravel and recompact, not with extra sand.

What are the most common paver base mistakes?

  1. Skipping the plate compactor. Hand tamping can't compact a gravel base to the density a paver surface needs. Rent a plate compactor for a day.
  2. Using the wrong material. Pea gravel, stone dust alone or topsoil can't be compacted into a stable layer.
  3. Building too shallow for the climate. A 4 inch base on clay soil in a freeze-thaw region invites heaving. Go deeper where winters are cold or soil is soft.
  4. No slope. A perfectly flat patio holds water. Aim for about 1/4 inch of fall per foot, directed away from buildings.
  5. No edge restraint. Without it, the paver edges spread and the joints open up.

Frequently asked questions

Can I put pavers directly on dirt?

You can, but it isn't recommended. Pavers on bare soil sink, shift and fill with weeds within a season or two, because soil doesn't drain or support a load the way compacted gravel does.

Is 4 inches of gravel enough for a paver patio?

Four inches is generally acceptable for walkways and patios on firm, well-drained soil in mild climates. On clay, soft ground or in cold-winter regions, 6 inches or more is safer.

Do I need landscape fabric under a paver base?

Fabric isn't needed over firm soil. A geotextile layer between soil and gravel can help over soft, clay-heavy soil by keeping the layers from mixing. Don't put fabric between the base and the bedding sand.

How many tons of gravel do I need for a 10x10 paver patio?

For a 100 sq ft patio, a 4 inch base needs about 2.1 tons and a 6 inch base about 3.1 tons, including a 20% allowance for compaction. Ask your supplier whether they sell by the ton or the yard.

Can I use stone dust instead of sand under pavers?

Coarse concrete sand is the standard bedding layer because it drains well. Stone dust holds water, packs hard and can cause pavers to settle unevenly, so most installers avoid it for bedding.

The bottom line

Plan on 4–6 inches of compacted crushed stone and 1 inch of coarse bedding sand for a patio, and go deeper on soft soil, in cold climates or for driveways. Dig to base + sand + paver thickness, compact in lifts and order about 20% more stone than the finished volume. The paver patio calculator turns your dimensions into exact quantities.
