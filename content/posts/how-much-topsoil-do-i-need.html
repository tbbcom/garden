Quick answer

One cubic yard of topsoil covers 108 square feet at 3 inches deep. To find your amount, multiply the area in square feet by the depth in inches and divide by 324. A 1,000 sq ft lawn at 4 inches needs 12.3 cubic yards, so order about 14 yards with a 10% allowance for settling.

Topsoil is sold by the cubic yard, weighed by the ton or bagged by the cubic foot, and none of those match how you measure a yard. The fix is one formula and a depth that suits the job, because a new lawn and a leveled low spot need very different amounts.

This guide gives the formula, a chart for common lawn and garden sizes, the right depth for each use and how to buy. For your own measurements, use the topsoil and gravel calculator.

Key takeaways
  • Formula: square feet × depth (in) ÷ 324 = cubic yards.
  • Coverage: 1 cubic yard = 324 sq ft at 1 in, 162 at 2 in, 108 at 3 in, 81 at 4 in, 54 at 6 in.
  • Depth: 4–6 inches for a new lawn or bed, 1/2 inch or less at a time for leveling, 1/4–1/2 inch for top-dressing.
  • Extra: order 10–15% more for settling and uneven ground.
  • Weight: a cubic yard is roughly 1–1.3 tons, so check your truck's payload.

How do you calculate how much topsoil you need?

Topsoil follows the same volume math as mulch: area × depth, converted to cubic yards. Four steps cover it.

Step 1: Measure the area in square feet

Multiply length × width in feet. For circles, use 3.14 × radius squared, and split irregular areas into rectangles and circles.

Step 2: Choose the depth for your job

See the depth table below. If you're adding to existing soil, measure what's already there.

Step 3: Multiply and divide by 324

Square feet × depth in inches ÷ 324 = cubic yards. The 324 is 12 inches per foot × 27 cubic feet per yard.

Example: 1,000 sq ft × 4 in ÷ 324 = 12.35 cubic yards.

Step 4: Add 10–15% and round up

Loose topsoil compacts as it's spread and settles after watering, and ground is never perfectly flat. 12.35 × 1.10 = 13.6, so order about 14 cubic yards.

How deep should topsoil be?

Depth depends on the job and on how good the soil underneath is. These are typical ranges; if your project is large, check with your local extension office for your soil and grass.

UseTypical topsoil depth
New lawn on compacted or subsoil-only ground4–6 in, blended into the top layer if possible
New flower or shrub bed4–6 in
Vegetable garden on poor ground6–8 in of good soil, often mixed with compost
Filling low spots in an existing lawnUp to 1/2 in at a time, repeating once grass grows through
Top-dressing a lawn1/4–1/2 in
Filling around a foundation or raising a bedAs needed, graded away from the house

How many cubic yards for a lawn or a garden bed?

For a new lawn of 5,000 sq ft at 4 inches, that's 61.7 cubic yards (about 68 with 10% extra), which is a large delivery, so confirm access and a place to dump the load. For a garden bed 4 × 25 ft (100 sq ft) at 6 inches, it's 100 × 6 ÷ 324 = 1.85 cubic yards, about 2 with extra.

When leveling small low spots, it's more efficient to calculate just the dip rather than the whole lawn. A 100 sq ft dip filled 1/2 inch deep needs only 0.15 cubic yards, about 4 cubic feet.

How much does topsoil weigh?

A cubic yard of topsoil weighs roughly 2,000–2,600 lb (about 1–1.3 tons), depending on moisture, sand content and organic matter. Wet topsoil is much heavier than dry. A full-size pickup can legally carry about a cubic yard or two of topsoil before it hits payload limits, so for bigger orders, have it delivered.

Ask your supplier for their own weight per yard if you're buying by the ton, because it varies by pit and by season.

Bagged or bulk topsoil: how do you buy it?

Bagged topsoil, sold by the cubic foot (0.75 and 1 cu ft bags are common), makes sense for small jobs. A cubic yard is 27 cubic feet, so it equals 27 bags of 1 cu ft or 36 bags of 0.75 cu ft. Bulk topsoil, delivered by the yard, is nearly always the better buy past a couple of yards. Check bag sizes carefully when comparing price per cubic foot.

What to ask a topsoil supplier

  • Is it screened? Screened topsoil has been sifted to remove rocks, roots and large clods.
  • What's in it? Ask about organic matter and the source. Avoid soil with a strong chemical smell or lots of debris.
  • Does it contain weed seed or clay? Cheap fill dirt often does.
  • How is it sold? By the cubic yard or by the ton, and the weight per yard.
Test unfamiliar soil before a big order. Ask for a small sample or buy a few bags first, and send a sample to your state's soil-testing lab through your county extension office if you plan to grow food. A load of poor soil is expensive to remove.

What mistakes lead to ordering the wrong amount?

  1. Forgetting to subtract. Don't count patios or sheds in your measurement, but do count steps and dips.
  2. Burying grass under thick fill. More than about an inch of soil over turf can smother it; for large raises, strip the turf first.
  3. Mixing up units. Cubic yards, cubic feet and tons are different; use consistent units.
  4. Skipping settling. Fresh topsoil sinks. Order 10–15% more.
  5. Grading toward the house. Slope fill away from foundations so water drains away.

Where does topsoil go wrong, and what are the alternatives?

Topsoil isn't always the best fill. Under a patio or shed, use compacted gravel instead, because soil settles. For a vegetable bed, a mix of topsoil and compost generally grows better than topsoil alone, since topsoil by itself can be dense and low in organic matter. For leveling, compost-and-sand blends are often used to top-dress established lawns without smothering the grass.

If you only need a modest amount, compare delivered bulk with bagged by cubic foot, and consider whether you can improve the soil you already have with compost. Adding 1–2 inches of compost and mixing it into the top 6 inches is often cheaper than replacing the soil, and it improves structure at the same time.

Frequently asked questions

How many cubic yards of topsoil do I need for 1,000 square feet?

At 4 inches deep you need 12.3 cubic yards, and with 10% extra order about 14. At 1 inch deep it's 3.1 cubic yards, and at 6 inches it's 18.5 cubic yards.

How many bags of topsoil are in a cubic yard?

A cubic yard is 27 cubic feet, so it equals 27 bags of 1 cu ft or 36 bags of 0.75 cu ft. Check the bag size on the label before comparing prices.

How much does a cubic yard of topsoil weigh?

About 2,000–2,600 lb, or roughly 1–1.3 tons, depending on moisture and the mix. Ask your supplier for their exact weight per yard.

How deep should topsoil be for a new lawn?

Aim for 4–6 inches of topsoil, ideally tilled into the layer beneath so there's no sharp boundary. If the existing soil is already decent, a thinner layer can be enough.

What is screened topsoil?

It's topsoil sifted through a mesh to remove rocks, roots and big clumps, leaving a finer, more even material that's easier to spread and better for lawns and beds.

The bottom line

Multiply square feet by depth in inches and divide by 324 to get cubic yards, then add 10–15% for settling. Use 4–6 inches for new lawns and beds and only 1/2 inch at a time for leveling. The topsoil and gravel calculator converts the result to tons, bags and cost.
