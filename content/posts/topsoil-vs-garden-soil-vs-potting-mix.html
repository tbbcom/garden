Quick answer

Use topsoil to fill and level the ground, garden soil to improve in-ground and raised beds, and potting mix only in containers. Topsoil is screened native soil with little organic matter; garden soil is topsoil blended with compost; potting mix is soilless, made of peat or coir, bark and perlite. Do not swap them 1-for-1.

The soil aisle looks simple until you stand in it. Topsoil, garden soil, potting soil, raised bed mix, fill dirt: the bags look similar and the prices vary by a factor of five. Buy the wrong one and plants struggle, or you spend more than you needed to.

This guide explains what each product is, where it belongs and how much to buy for common jobs. Once you know which, the soil calculator converts your dimensions into cubic yards and bags.

Key takeaways
  • Topsoil: cheapest, for filling holes, leveling and lawn bases.
  • Garden soil: topsoil plus compost, for in-ground flower and vegetable beds.
  • Potting mix: soilless, light and sterile-ish, only for pots and containers.
  • Raised beds: a blend of topsoil and compost, or a bagged raised-bed mix; never pure topsoil or potting mix.
  • Quantity: square feet × depth in inches ÷ 324 = cubic yards for any of them.

"Topsoil" has no single legal standard in the US, so quality varies enormously between suppliers. One bag or yard can be dark and crumbly, another pale and full of clay or stones. Always look at a sample before you order a load.

When should you use topsoil?

Topsoil is the budget workhorse. It is right when you need volume more than fertility:

  • Leveling low spots in a lawn with thin layers (up to about 1/2 inch at a time, so grass grows through).
  • Backfilling around a new foundation, patio or retaining wall.
  • Building a base for a new lawn where native soil is poor: roughly 4–6 inches.
  • Ingredient in a raised bed mix (about 60%, with compost and a coarse aggregate).

Do not use plain topsoil as the only fill in a raised bed. It compacts and drains poorly in a confined space, and it is rarely rich enough to feed vegetables.

When is garden soil the right choice?

Garden soil is topsoil that has already been blended with compost. Use it when you are planting in the ground or topping up a bed:

  • Filling a planting hole for shrubs or perennials (mix with native soil, do not replace it).
  • Adding 1–2 inches over a bed and working it into the top few inches.
  • Raising a low bed by a few inches.

Beware of products labeled "garden soil" that are mostly bark fines or composted forest products. Squeeze a handful: it should hold its shape loosely and break apart, not stay soggy or crumble into dust.

Why should potting mix stay in containers?

Potting mix is engineered for pots. It is light, drains fast, and holds air, because roots in a small container would suffocate in heavy soil. That is exactly why it is wrong for beds: it dries out quickly, shrinks as the peat or bark breaks down, and costs several times more per cubic foot than topsoil or garden soil.

Conversely, garden soil in a pot becomes dense and waterlogged. Even "potting soil" sold in bags is normally soilless; read the label's ingredient list. One cubic foot of potting mix fills about 7.5 gallons, so a 2 cubic foot bag fills roughly three 5-gallon pots.

How much topsoil or garden soil do you need?

The formula is the same for all three: square feet × depth in inches ÷ 324 = cubic yards. For potting mix in containers, work in cubic feet instead.

How can you tell good topsoil from bad?

  1. Look and feel. Dark, crumbly and slightly moist is good. Pale, sticky clay or sandy and dusty is not.
  2. Check for debris. Rocks, roots, construction waste and weeds mean low-grade fill.
  3. Smell. It should smell earthy. A sour, rotten smell means anaerobic, poorly composted material.
  4. Ask the source. Screened topsoil from a reputable landscape supplier is better than "fill" from a construction site, which can carry weed seed and contamination.
  5. Consider a lab test for pH and nutrients, especially if you plan to grow vegetables on new soil, since urban fill can have contaminants.
Don't use soil from unknown sources for edible gardens. Construction-site topsoil and old industrial or roadside soil can contain lead or other contaminants. If you are unsure, use a certified raised-bed mix or test the soil first through your state extension service.

Should you buy soil in bags or in bulk?

Bags suit small jobs: a 40 lb bag of topsoil or 1 to 2 cubic feet of garden soil is easy to carry and store. Bulk is far cheaper once you pass roughly 1 cubic yard, which is 13.5 bags of 2 cubic feet. For a lawn base or several beds, a delivered load is the practical choice. Put a tarp under the pile and spread it within a few days, because wet soil piled on a lawn kills the grass beneath.

Which should you buy for common jobs?

  • Fixing low spots in the lawn: screened topsoil, or a topsoil-sand-compost blend.
  • Planting trees and shrubs: backfill with the native soil you dug out, amended lightly with compost.
  • New vegetable or flower bed in the ground: garden soil or compost worked into native soil.
  • Raised beds: a mix of topsoil and compost, or bagged raised-bed mix.
  • Pots and hanging baskets: potting mix.
  • Seed starting: a fine seed-starting mix.

Frequently asked questions

Can I use garden soil in pots?

Not ideally. Garden soil is too dense for containers and can drain badly and compact. Use a potting mix, which is formulated to stay light and aerated in pots.

Is potting soil the same as potting mix?

In practice, yes: both are mostly soilless blends of peat or coir, bark and perlite. The term "soil" on the bag does not mean it contains real soil.

Is topsoil good for growing vegetables?

It can be, if it is dark, crumbly and screened, and blended with compost. Straight topsoil is rarely rich enough on its own and can compact in raised beds.

What is the cheapest way to fill a large garden bed?

Buy bulk topsoil and compost by the cubic yard and mix them 2:1, rather than buying bagged garden soil. Bulk costs far less per cubic foot for beds over about 50 sq ft.

Can I put topsoil over grass to level my lawn?

Yes, in layers no deeper than about 1/2 inch, so grass can grow through. Deeper fills smother the grass and are better done by stripping the turf first.

What is fill dirt used for?

Fill dirt is subsoil used to raise grade or fill holes under hardscapes. It has little organic matter and is not suitable for plants without a layer of topsoil on top.

The bottom line
