Quick answer

Plant cucumbers 1–2 weeks after your average last frost, once the soil is at least 65°F (70°F is ideal) and nights stay above 50°F. For most of the US that means late April to early June. Warm Southern zones can sow again in late summer for a fall crop.

Cucumbers are among the most frost-tender vegetables in the garden. A single night near 32°F kills the vines, and cold, wet soil rots seed before it sprouts. Planting a week too early costs you more than planting a week late, because cucumbers sown into warm soil catch up fast.

This guide gives you soil-temperature rules, a dates table for USDA zones 3–10, and a plan for succession sowing. To get dates for your own town, check your frost dates by ZIP code, then find your exact dates with the planting calendar.

Key takeaways
  • Frost: cucumbers have no frost tolerance. Wait until 1–2 weeks after the last spring frost.
  • Soil: seed germinates slowly below 60°F; aim for 70°F or warmer, with 65°F as a practical minimum.
  • Method: direct sow is best. Start indoors only 3 weeks ahead, in peat-free pots that won't disturb roots.
  • Timing to harvest: most slicers take 50–70 days from sowing; pickling types are often 50–55.
  • Succession: sow a second batch 3 weeks later, and in zones 7–10 a fall crop 10–12 weeks before first frost.

What soil temperature do cucumbers need?

Cucumbers are a warm-season crop from tropical roots. Extension guidance generally puts the germination minimum near 60°F, with fast, reliable sprouting at 70–95°F. Seed in 55°F soil can sit for weeks and rot. The table below shows what to expect.

Soil temperatureWhat happens to cucumber seed
Below 55°FSeed rots or does not sprout; seedlings stall
55–60°FVery slow, uneven germination
65–70°FGood germination in roughly 7–10 days
75–90°FFastest sprouting, often in 3–5 days

Check with a soil thermometer pushed 2 inches deep, at mid-morning, for three days in a row. Air temperature alone misleads you, because soil warms more slowly than the air, especially after a wet spring.

Should you start cucumbers indoors or sow them outside?

Direct sowing wins for most gardeners. Cucumbers dislike root disturbance, and seed sown into warm soil often overtakes transplants within weeks. Start indoors only if your season is short (zones 3–5) or you want a head start.

  • Direct sow: best from zone 5 south. Seed goes in 1 inch deep, once the soil is 65°F or warmer.
  • Start indoors: sow 3 weeks before your planned transplant date, in 3-inch pots filled with seed-starting mix. Older seedlings with root-bound roots establish poorly.
  • Harden off: give seedlings 5–7 days of gradual outdoor exposure before planting.

For zone 3, a seed-start date of about May 11 (3 weeks before the June 1 outdoor date) lets you transplant on schedule; for zone 5, about April 21 for May 12 planting.

How do you plant cucumbers step by step?

  1. Pick a sunny spot. Cucumbers need at least 6–8 hours of direct sun and a bed that drains well.
  2. Enrich the soil. Mix in 2–3 inches of compost. Cucumbers are heavy feeders, and ideal soil pH is about 6.0–7.0.
  3. Wait for warmth. Confirm the soil is at least 65°F and the 10-day forecast has no nights below 50°F.
  4. Sow seed 1 inch deep. Plant 2–3 seeds per spot. For vining types on a trellis, space spots 12 inches apart; for sprawling hills, 3 feet apart.
  5. Water gently. Keep the soil evenly moist until sprouts appear in about a week.
  6. Thin to the strongest seedling per spot by snipping the extras at the soil line.
  7. Mulch with 2 inches of straw once plants are established and the soil is warm.

A 4 × 8 ft bed (32 sq ft) with a trellis along one long side can hold about 8 vines at 12 inches apart along the 8 ft length, leaving the other rows free for other crops.

How do you plant cucumbers in succession?

Cucumber vines produce heavily for four to six weeks and then slow down, often from disease or bitter fruit in summer heat. Sowing a second batch about 3 weeks after the first spreads the harvest. Zone 6 gardeners, for example, can sow on May 2 and again on May 23, then pull the first planting when production fades.

Count back from your first fall frost to set a last sowing date. A 60-day variety planted 70 days before frost leaves 10 days of buffer for slow growth as days shorten. This is why zone 7 can sow as late as the third week of August, while zone 5 should be done by the first of August.

Can you grow cucumbers as a fall crop?

In zones 7–10 the answer is yes. Late summer soil is warm and seed sprouts in 3–4 days. The challenge is heat stress and pests, so water deeply and choose disease-resistant varieties. In zones 9 and 10, fall cucumbers sown in mid-September often produce until the first cool nights in December. Fall crops usually need less trellis support because vines are smaller.

Frost warning: if frost is forecast after seedlings emerge, cover plants with a floating row cover or an upturned bucket overnight. Even light frost damages cucumber leaves, and a hard freeze kills vines outright. Remove the cover each morning once temperatures rise.

5 mistakes that hurt your cucumber crop

  1. Planting before the soil warms. The seed rots. Use a thermometer, not the calendar.
  2. Letting the bed dry out. Irregular watering causes bitter, misshapen fruit. Aim for about 1 inch of water per week, more in heat.
  3. Skipping the trellis. Climbing vines hold fruit off the ground, improve airflow and make picking easier.
  4. Letting cucumbers overripen. Pick often. Fruit left on the vine tells the plant to stop producing.
  5. Planting in the same spot every year. Rotate away from cucumbers, squash and melons for 2–3 years to reduce soil-borne disease.

Frequently asked questions

How long do cucumbers take to grow from seed?

Most cucumbers produce fruit 50–70 days after sowing. Pickling types are often ready in 50–55 days, while larger slicers and specialty types can take 60–70 days. Cooler weather adds days.

Is it too late to plant cucumbers in July?

It depends on your zone. In zones 6–10 July sowing works well for a late harvest, because a 55-day variety will mature before frost. In zones 3–4, July is the last practical month and only for fast pickling types.

Can I plant cucumber seeds directly in the ground in April?

Only in zones 7 and warmer, and only after the soil reaches 65°F. In zones 3–6, April soil is usually too cold, and seed will rot in the ground.

Should I soak cucumber seeds before planting?

It is not necessary. Soaking for a few hours can speed sprouting slightly, but in cool, wet soil it can encourage rot, so plant dry seed into warm, moist soil.

How many cucumber plants do I need per person?

Two to three healthy vines per person is enough for fresh eating. Plan on 5–8 vines per household if you also want to make pickles.

The bottom line

Wait until 1–2 weeks after the last frost and until the soil is 65–70°F, then sow cucumber seed 1 inch deep. Add a second sowing 3 weeks later, and in warm zones a fall crop 10–12 weeks before the first frost.
