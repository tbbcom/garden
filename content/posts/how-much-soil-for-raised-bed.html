Quick answer

Multiply length × width × depth in feet to get cubic feet of soil. A 4 × 8 ft raised bed filled 12 inches deep needs 32 cubic feet, or 1.19 cubic yards. Divide cubic feet by 27 for yards, or by the bag size (usually 1.5–2 cu ft) for bags. Add about 10% because fresh soil settles.

Filling a raised bed is the biggest single cost of getting started, and the most common place to miscalculate. Order too little and you are stuck with a half-filled bed and a second trip. Order too much and you are paying for soil you do not need. Both are avoidable with one multiplication.

Below are the formula, charts for common sizes in cubic feet, cubic yards and bags, and tips for cutting the cost. For beds that are not rectangles, or to compare bagged and bulk soil, use the soil calculator.

Key takeaways
  • Formula: length (ft) × width (ft) × depth (ft) = cubic feet. Depth in inches ÷ 12.
  • 4 × 8 ft at 12 in: 32 cu ft = 1.19 cu yd = 16 bags of 2 cu ft (22 bags of 1.5 cu ft).
  • Settling: add about 10%, because fresh soil and compost compact.
  • Depth: 6–8 in works for greens; 10–12 in suits most vegetables; 18 in or more helps root crops and poor native soil.
  • Cost trick: bulk soil is far cheaper per cubic foot than bags once you need over about 1 cubic yard.

How do you calculate soil for a raised bed?

Raised-bed volume is a box: length × width × depth. Use feet for all three, so a depth of 12 inches is 1 foot.

  1. Measure the inside of the bed (not the outside of the frame).
  2. Convert depth to feet by dividing inches by 12. Eight inches is 0.67 ft.
  3. Multiply length × width × depth for cubic feet.
  4. Convert if needed. Cubic feet ÷ 27 = cubic yards. Cubic feet ÷ bag size = bags.
  5. Add 10% for settling and round up.

Example: a 4 × 8 ft bed filled 12 inches: 4 × 8 × 1 = 32 cubic feet. ÷ 27 = 1.19 cubic yards. With 10% extra, 35.2 cubic feet, which is 17.6 bags of 2 cu ft, so buy 18.

How deep should the soil be?

Depth drives both cost and results. Roots can grow into the ground below if the bed sits on bare soil, so the fill only needs to be as deep as the roots will use.

  • 6–8 inches: lettuce, spinach, radishes, herbs and other shallow roots. Good for budget beds set directly on loosened ground.
  • 10–12 inches: most vegetables, including tomatoes, peppers, beans, cucumbers and carrots (short types).
  • 18 inches or more: deep-rooted or long crops, beds over concrete or compacted soil, and accessible tall beds.

If the bed sits on soil, loosen the ground under it with a fork first, and roots will reach well below the frame.

How can you use less soil?

For tall beds, you can fill the bottom with wood or other bulky organic material instead of all soil. This is the idea behind "hugelkultur" and lasagna filling. If the bottom third of a 4 × 8 ft bed at 12 inches is branches and logs, soil need drops from 32 to about 21 cubic feet (0.79 cubic yards). The trade-off is that the organic fill breaks down and the bed settles over several years, so top it up with compost. Use only untreated wood, and avoid black walnut.

Do not use all topsoil or all compost. Straight topsoil compacts and drains poorly in a bed, while pure compost can be too rich and too fluffy and shrink quickly. See our raised bed soil mix guide for blends that work.

5 mistakes to avoid when ordering raised bed soil

  1. Using outside dimensions. Frames are 1.5 inches thick, which on a 4 × 8 ft bed overstates volume by roughly 10%.
  2. Forgetting settling. Fresh mixes with lots of compost shrink 10–20% in the first season.
  3. Mixing up cubic feet and bags. Bags labeled in quarts or liters are smaller than they sound: a 40-quart bag is only about 1.5 cubic feet, since 1 cubic foot is roughly 26 dry quarts.
  4. Buying "fill dirt". It is subsoil with little organic matter; use topsoil, garden soil or a raised-bed mix.
  5. Overfilling. Leave an inch or two below the rim so water does not wash soil out.

Frequently asked questions

How many cubic feet of soil are in a 4×8 raised bed?

A 4 × 8 ft raised bed filled to 12 inches holds 32 cubic feet. At 8 inches it holds 21.3 cubic feet and at 6 inches 16 cubic feet.

How many bags of soil for a 4x4 raised bed?

A 4 × 4 ft bed filled 12 inches deep needs 16 cubic feet, which is 8 bags of 2 cu ft or 11 bags of 1.5 cu ft, rounded up. Add a bag or two for settling.

How many cubic yards of soil do I need for a 3×6 raised bed?

A 3 × 6 ft bed at 12 inches deep needs 18 cubic feet, which is 0.67 cubic yards. At 8 inches deep it needs 12 cubic feet, or 0.44 cubic yards.

How do I convert cubic feet to cubic yards for soil?

Divide cubic feet by 27, because a cubic yard is 3 ft × 3 ft × 3 ft = 27 cubic feet. For example, 54 cubic feet is 2 cubic yards.

Is it cheaper to buy soil in bags or bulk for raised beds?

Bulk is usually cheaper per cubic foot once you need around a cubic yard or more, but delivery fees matter. Bags win for one small bed or a top-up.

Can I put cardboard or landscape fabric under a raised bed?

Cardboard under the soil suppresses grass and rots away within a season. Avoid plastic sheets, which block drainage, and fabric that restricts roots from reaching deeper soil.

The bottom line

Multiply length × width × depth in feet, convert to cubic yards or bags, then add 10% for settling. A 4 × 8 ft bed at 12 inches needs 32 cubic feet (1.19 cubic yards). For several beds or odd shapes, the soil calculator gives you the totals in seconds.
