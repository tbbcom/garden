Quick answer

To harvest pumpkins by Halloween, plant them between late May and early July, depending on your climate and the variety's days to maturity. Count back 90–120 days from October 31, and sow only after frost has passed and soil is at least 70°F. A 100-day pumpkin sown around July 9 is ready on time in the warm South.

Pumpkins ask for two things you cannot hurry: warm soil to sprout and a long stretch of heat to ripen. Sow too early and the fruit is ripe in August, then sits in the sun and rots before Halloween. Sow too late and frost kills the vines with green pumpkins still on them.

This guide shows how to count back from Halloween, gives sowing dates for USDA zones 3–10, and explains how to match the variety to your season. Look up your frost dates by ZIP code and use the planting calendar for your exact dates.

Key takeaways
  • Count-back rule: sow date = October 31 minus days to maturity. A 100-day pumpkin: July 23; add a 2-week buffer and aim for around July 9.
  • Soil temperature: pumpkin seeds sprout best in soil at or above 70°F; wait until at least 65°F.
  • Frost: pumpkin vines are frost-tender, so sow about 2 weeks after your last frost.
  • Cold zones (3–5): frost limits you; choose 85–95-day varieties and sow in late May to mid June.
  • Maturity ranges: mini and pie types roughly 90–105 days; large field pumpkins 110–120+ days.

How do you count back from Halloween to find your pumpkin planting date?

Start with October 31 and subtract your variety's days to maturity, found on the seed packet or plant tag. Days to maturity count from sowing for direct-seeded plants. Then subtract about 14 more days as a cushion so the pumpkins are ripe, hardened and ready before Halloween rather than just barely turning orange.

What soil temperature do pumpkins need?

Pumpkin seeds rot rather than sprout in cold, wet soil. Extension guidance generally says to wait until the soil reaches at least 65°F, with 70°F or warmer ideal. Check with a soil thermometer pushed 2 inches deep, in the morning, for three days in a row. Air temperatures should stay above 50°F at night.

Black plastic mulch laid over the bed for a week or two before sowing can warm soil in cool springs, which helps in zones 3–5.

Should you start pumpkins indoors or sow them directly?

In most climates, direct sowing is best because pumpkins dislike having their roots disturbed. In short-season zones (3–4), start seeds indoors 2–4 weeks before transplanting, ideally in biodegradable pots that can go straight into the ground, and harden plants off for a few days first.

Zone 3 example: transplant date is two weeks after last frost (June 8), so start seeds around May 18 (3 weeks earlier). That gains about three weeks of growing time compared with sowing outside.

How do you plant pumpkins step by step?

  1. Choose a sunny, roomy spot. Pumpkins need 6–8 hours of sun and plenty of space. Vining types can cover 50–100 square feet or more per plant.
  2. Prepare rich soil. Pumpkins are heavy feeders. Mix in several inches of compost; a pH of about 6.0–6.8 is ideal.
  3. Make hills or rows. Build low mounds about 4–6 feet apart (up to 8 feet for large vines) with rows 8–10 feet apart. Mounds warm faster and drain well.
  4. Sow 4–5 seeds per hill, 1 inch deep. Keep the soil evenly moist until they sprout in 5–10 days.
  5. Thin to the 2 strongest plants per hill once they have a couple of true leaves, cutting the extras at the soil line rather than pulling them.
  6. Mulch and water deeply. Give about 1–2 inches of water per week, applied at the base to keep leaves dry.
Do not sow on the calendar alone. A warm April followed by a cold snap kills seedlings fast. If frost threatens after sprouting, cover young plants overnight with a bucket, row cover or frost blanket.

How do you know when pumpkins are ready to harvest?

Harvest when the rind is hard enough that you cannot dent it with a fingernail, the color is deep and even, and the stem is turning brown and woody. The vine often starts to die back. Cut the stem leaving 3–4 inches attached, and never carry a pumpkin by its stem.

Harvest before a hard freeze. Light frost kills leaves but usually not the fruit; a hard freeze can damage it. For storage, many extension sources recommend curing for about 10 days at 80–85°F, then keeping pumpkins in a cool, dry place around 50–55°F.

What are the most common pumpkin timing mistakes?

  1. Sowing in cold soil. Seeds rot before germinating; wait for 65–70°F.
  2. Sowing too early for Halloween. A pumpkin ripe in August may rot or soften by late October. Delay sowing in warm zones.
  3. Choosing a long-season variety in a short-season zone. A 120-day pumpkin in zone 3 will not ripen. Pick by days to maturity first, looks second.
  4. Crowding vines. Too little space means disease, because powdery mildew spreads in humid, crowded foliage.
  5. Skipping pollinators. Plants make male flowers first and female flowers days later. Do not worry if early flowers do not set fruit; avoid spraying insecticides while flowers are open.

Frequently asked questions

How many days does it take to grow a pumpkin?

Most pumpkins take about 90–120 days from sowing to harvest, with mini and pie types at the shorter end and large field and giant types at the longer end. Check your seed packet for the exact days to maturity.

Is July too late to plant pumpkins?

Not in warm climates. In zones 7–10, planting a 90–100-day variety in early to mid July gives ripe pumpkins by Halloween. In zones 3–5, July is too late because frost arrives before the fruit matures.

How many pumpkins does one plant produce?

It depends on the variety. Small and pie types often set several fruits per vine, while large types are usually best at one to three. Thinning extra fruit helps the remaining pumpkins grow larger.

Do pumpkins need full sun?

Yes. Pumpkins need at least 6–8 hours of direct sun daily for good growth and fruit set. Shade slows ripening and encourages mildew.

How far apart should pumpkin hills be?

Space hills 4–6 feet apart for most varieties and up to 8 feet for large vining types. Keep rows 8–10 feet apart, and allow 2 plants per hill after thinning.

Can you grow pumpkins in a raised bed or container?

Mini and bush varieties can grow in a large raised bed or a 20-gallon or larger container if you train vines onto a trellis. Full-size vining pumpkins need much more ground space than most raised beds offer.

The bottom line

Take October 31, subtract your pumpkin's days to maturity (90–120), then add a two-week cushion. Sow only after the last frost has passed and the soil is at least 70°F, and in zones 3–5 let the first fall frost, not Halloween, set your deadline.
