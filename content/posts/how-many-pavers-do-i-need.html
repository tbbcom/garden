Quick answer

To work out how many pavers you need, divide your patio's area by the area of one paver, then add 10% for cuts and breakage. A 12 × 16 ft patio (192 sq ft) in 6 × 9 inch pavers needs 512 whole pavers, so order about 564. Pavers measuring 12 × 12 inches need 1 per square foot.

Ordering pavers is a one-shot decision. Run short and the next pallet may come from a different batch with a slightly different color, which shows in a finished patio. Order far too many and you're paying for stone you'll stack behind the shed.

This guide gives you the formula, a pavers-per-square-foot chart for the common sizes, worked examples for rectangles and curves, and the right amount of waste for each pattern. If you'd rather not do the math, the free paver patio calculator works out pavers, base gravel and sand together.

Key takeaways
  • Formula: patio area (sq ft) ÷ one paver's area (sq ft) × 1.10 = pavers to order.
  • Per square foot: 4 × 8 in = 4.5 · 6 × 6 in = 4 · 6 × 9 in = 2.67 · 12 × 12 in = 1 · 16 × 16 in = 0.56 · 12 × 24 in = 0.5.
  • Waste: about 5–10% for straight patterns, 10–15% for diagonal, herringbone or curved edges.
  • Joints: the 1/8 to 3/8 inch joints shift your count by only a few percent; the waste allowance covers it.
  • Same batch: buy everything in one order so colors match, plus a few spares for future repairs.

How do you calculate how many pavers you need?

The method is the same for any paver shape: find the patio's area, find one paver's area, divide, then add waste. It takes four steps.

Step 1: Measure the patio area

For a rectangle, multiply length × width in feet. A patio 12 ft by 16 ft is 192 sq ft. For other shapes, split the patio into rectangles, circles and triangles, work out each, and add them together.

Step 2: Work out the area of one paver

Multiply the paver's length by its width in inches, then divide by 144 (the number of square inches in a square foot). A 6 × 9 inch paver is 54 sq in ÷ 144 = 0.375 sq ft.

Step 3: Divide and round up

Patio area ÷ paver area gives the number of whole pavers. For the example, 192 ÷ 0.375 = 512 pavers.

Step 4: Add the waste allowance

Multiply by 1.10 for a 10% allowance and round up: 512 × 1.10 = 563.2, so order 564. With a simple running-bond pattern and few cuts, 5% (538 pavers) can be enough.

How much waste should you allow for?

The waste allowance depends on the pattern and the shape of the patio, because every pattern that doesn't line up with your edges creates cut pieces. Most of those offcuts end up in the bin.

Pattern or shapeTypical allowanceWhy
Stack bond or running bond, rectangular patio sized to the pavers5%Few or no cuts; only breakage
Running bond, patio not a multiple of paver size10%Cuts along two edges
Basket weave or herringbone (90°)10%Edge cuts on all sides
Herringbone at 45°, diagonal layouts12–15%Triangular cuts along every edge
Curves, circles, many corners15%Lots of small cut pieces

Because most of the waste is edge cuts, a patio sized to a whole number of pavers wastes very little. A 12 × 12 ft patio in 12 × 12 inch pavers needs exactly 144, with no cutting at all.

What about the joints between pavers?

Pavers are laid with narrow joints, usually 1/8 to 3/8 inch depending on the product, and filled with jointing sand. Joints make each paver effectively a little larger than its face, so the true count for a given area can be a few percent lower than the simple formula says.

The 10% allowance more than absorbs that difference, so there's no need to adjust for it. Your measurements, cuts and any broken pieces matter far more than a 1/8 inch joint.

How to calculate pavers for a circular or curved patio

For a round patio, area = 3.14 × radius squared. A circle 10 ft across has a 5 ft radius, so 3.14 × 25 = 78.5 sq ft. With 12 × 12 inch pavers and a 15% allowance, that's 78.5 × 1.15 = 90.3, so order 91.

For a patio with one curved side, work out the full rectangle and then subtract roughly half the area of the curve you remove. Add the 15% allowance regardless, because curved edges are where most cuts happen. Smaller pavers such as 4 × 8 or 6 × 6 follow curves with less waste than big square slabs.

Common mistakes when ordering pavers

Don't order by area alone. Pavers are sold both by the piece and by the square foot or pallet, and the coverage printed on a pallet is for the face area only. Always confirm whether the quote is per paver or per square foot before you compare prices.
  1. Forgetting the border. A soldier-course border in a contrasting paver counts as its own area. Subtract it from the field, then calculate it separately by length.
  2. Using nominal sizes without checking. A "12 × 12" paver may measure 11 3/4 inches. On a big patio that's a few extra pavers.
  3. Skipping the spares. Keep 5–10 extras from the same batch. Matching a color years later is often impossible.
  4. Mixing pallets. Pavers vary slightly between production runs. Lay from several pallets at once so any shade differences blend instead of forming patches.
  5. Ordering before the base is planned. The paver thickness drives your excavation depth. Decide on the product first, then dig.

Frequently asked questions

How many 12x12 pavers do I need for a 10x10 patio?

A 10 × 10 ft patio is 100 sq ft, which takes 100 whole 12 × 12 inch pavers. With a 10% allowance, order 110. If the patio size matches the paver size and you use a straight pattern, 105 is usually enough.

How many 6x9 pavers cover 100 square feet?

It takes about 267 pavers of 6 × 9 inches to cover 100 sq ft with no waste. Adding 10% for cuts and breakage brings the order to 294.

How many pavers are on a pallet?

It depends on the product, so read the label or ask the supplier. Pallets are usually sold by their coverage in square feet, and the number of pieces on them changes with the paver's size and thickness.

Do I need extra pavers for cuts?

Yes. Add 5–10% for straight layouts and 10–15% for diagonal, herringbone or curved patios. Offcuts are only reusable if they happen to fit another edge.

How many pavers do I need for a walkway 3 feet wide?

Each foot of a 3 ft wide walkway is 3 sq ft. With 12 × 12 inch pavers, that's 3 pavers per foot of path, so a 20 ft walkway needs 60 plus about 10% extra, which is 66.
