Quick answer

To find how much flooring you need, multiply room length × width to get square feet, then add 5–10% for waste (10–15% for diagonal or herringbone). A 12 × 15 ft room is 180 sq ft, so buy about 198 sq ft at 10% waste. Divide by the coverage per box, usually around 20 sq ft, and round up.

Flooring is sold in full boxes, can't be returned once it's opened in many stores and often changes color between production lots. A good measurement is what keeps you from a half-finished room and a hunt for a matching box.

This guide shows the formula, a waste-factor chart, box counts for common room sizes, and how to handle closets, L-shaped rooms and stairs. For several rooms at once, use the flooring calculator.

Key takeaways
  • Formula: length × width × (1 + waste %) ÷ box coverage = boxes to buy, rounded up.
  • Waste: 5–10% for straight layouts, 10–15% for diagonal or herringbone, and more for tricky rooms.
  • Boxes: a 12 × 15 ft room needs 10 boxes at 20 sq ft each with 5–10% waste; 11 with 15%.
  • Same lot: buy all boxes together and check the lot number so colors match.
  • Keep a spare: save at least one unopened box for future repairs.

How do you calculate how much flooring you need?

You only need a tape measure, a notepad and about ten minutes. Here are the steps.

Step 1: Measure each room's length and width

Measure wall to wall at the widest points, in feet and inches. Convert inches to decimals by dividing by 12: 10 ft 6 in is 10.5 ft. For a plain rectangle, length × width gives square feet. A 12 ft × 15 ft room is 180 sq ft.

Step 2: Add spaces that need flooring

Measure closets, alcoves, hall sections and doorways separately, then add them to the total. A 3 × 4 ft closet adds 12 sq ft, which takes the room above to 192 sq ft.

Step 3: Subtract only permanent fixtures

Kitchen islands and built-in cabinets don't need flooring under them. Furniture, appliances you'll replace and removable fixtures do. Many installers don't subtract small areas at all, since leftover material is useful for cuts.

Step 4: Add the waste factor

Multiply the area by 1.05 to 1.15 depending on the layout (see the next section). For 192 sq ft at 10%: 192 × 1.10 = 211.2 sq ft.

Step 5: Divide by the coverage per box and round up

The box label shows square feet per carton. At 20 sq ft per box, 211.2 ÷ 20 = 10.56, so buy 11 boxes.

How much waste factor should you add for flooring?

Waste covers cuts at walls and doorways, damaged or defective planks and the occasional mistake. The right percentage depends on the layout and the room's shape, so the numbers below are ranges, not rules; your installer or the manufacturer may recommend a specific figure.

SituationTypical waste factor
Simple rectangular room, planks laid straight, experienced installer5%
Typical DIY job, standard rectangular room7–10%
Rooms with many corners, closets, doorways or an L shape10%
Diagonal layouts10–15%
Herringbone or chevron15% or more
Long, narrow planks in a small, cut-up room10–15%

How do you measure an L-shaped or irregular room?

Break any odd room into rectangles, measure each one, then add the areas together.

  • L-shaped room: split it into two rectangles. A 12 × 10 ft main space plus an 8 × 6 ft nook is 120 + 48 = 168 sq ft.
  • Bay window or alcove: measure its width and depth as a rectangle and add it.
  • Room with a diagonal wall: measure the full rectangle, then subtract half the area of the cut-off triangle.
  • Open floor plan: decide where the flooring will end, usually at a doorway or threshold, and measure each zone separately.

How much flooring do you need for stairs?

Stairs are measured by the step, not square feet. Each step has a tread (the flat part) and a riser (the vertical part). Measure the tread depth and width plus the riser height, then add the two for each step. Stair-nosing pieces are sold separately by length.

For a quick estimate, multiply the number of steps by the width of the staircase times roughly 1.5 ft to cover tread and riser, then add 10–15% because stair cuts waste more. Most stair covers are sold as pre-cut treads, so check your product's packaging instead of relying on a general rule.

What mistakes lead to buying the wrong amount?

Check the box size before you pay. Square feet per carton varies by brand and product, from under 15 to more than 25. Compare price per square foot, not price per box, and recalculate the box count for the product you actually choose.
  1. Forgetting the closets and hallways. Adjacent spaces often add up to a full extra box or two.
  2. Using too little waste for the layout. A diagonal pattern needs about twice the allowance of a straight one.
  3. Measuring only the middle of the room. Walls aren't always square. Take the longest length and widest width.
  4. Mixing lots. Production lots can vary in color. Buy everything at once and check the lot numbers.
  5. Buying exactly what you need. One unopened leftover box saves you from sourcing a discontinued product when a plank is damaged later.

Do you need underlayment, trim and extras?

Flooring boxes are only part of the order. Many floating floors need underlayment unless a pad is attached, which is sold in rolls by the square foot, so buy the same area as the floor plus a few percent. Transition strips are needed at doorways and where the floor meets carpet or tile, and quarter-round or baseboard covers the expansion gap at the walls.

Measure the linear feet of wall for trim and the number of doorways for transitions. A 12 × 15 ft room has 54 ft of perimeter, minus doorway openings. Moisture barrier is needed over concrete slabs for many products. Check the installation instructions for the product you choose, since requirements differ and affect the warranty.

Frequently asked questions

How much flooring do I need for a 12x12 room?

A 12 × 12 ft room is 144 sq ft. With 10% waste, buy about 158 sq ft, which is 8 boxes at 20 sq ft each. For diagonal or herringbone layouts with 15% waste, plan on 166 sq ft or 9 boxes.

How many square feet is a typical box of flooring?

It varies by product, but many laminate and vinyl plank cartons cover roughly 18–25 sq ft. The label lists the exact coverage, so use that figure rather than an average.

Should I subtract closets and cabinets from the flooring total?

Add closets, since they need flooring. Don't subtract fixed cabinets or a kitchen island unless the area is large, and treat the leftover as extra cutting material.

Do I need to buy extra flooring for future repairs?

Yes, if possible. Keeping one or two unopened boxes from the same lot lets you replace a damaged plank later even if the style is discontinued.

How do I convert square feet to square yards for flooring?

Divide square feet by 9. A 180 sq ft room is 20 square yards. Carpet is often priced per square yard, while plank flooring is sold by the square foot.

The bottom line

Measure each space, add them together, then add 5–10% for straight layouts or 10–15% for diagonal ones, and divide by the box coverage. Round up, buy from a single lot and keep a spare box. The flooring calculator does the multi-room math instantly.
