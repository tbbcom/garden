Quick answer

Sow carrots outdoors 2–3 weeks before your last spring frost, once the soil is at least 50°F (ideal 55–75°F). Sow again for fall about 10–12 weeks before your first fall frost. Seeds take 1–3 weeks to sprout, and most varieties are ready 60–80 days later.

Carrots are easy to grow and fussy to start. The seeds are tiny, slow to sprout and picky about soil, so a few days of timing can decide whether you get a full row or a bare strip. Planting too early in cold soil or too late in summer heat are the two most common ways to lose a carrot crop.

This guide gives you the soil-temperature rule, a spring and fall sowing table for USDA zones 3–10, and what to do after sowing. For dates matched to your own ZIP code, use the planting calendar or look up your frost dates by ZIP code.

Key takeaways
  • Spring sowing: 2–3 weeks before the last frost, with soil at 50°F or warmer.
  • Germination: works in soil from about 45°F to 85°F, but it is fastest at 70–75°F and can take 1–3 weeks.
  • Fall sowing: 10–12 weeks before the first fall frost; fall carrots are often sweeter.
  • Depth and spacing: sow 1/4–1/2 inch deep, then thin to 2–3 inches apart.
  • Maturity: most varieties take 60–80 days; some storage types take longer.

When is the best time to plant carrots in spring?

Carrot seedlings and young plants tolerate light frost, so you don't need to wait until the danger of frost has passed. Extension guidance is generally to sow 2–3 weeks before the average last spring frost, as soon as the soil can be worked and has begun to warm. That puts the carrots in early so they mature before summer heat, which makes roots tough and strong-flavored.

Seeds will germinate in soil as cool as about 45°F, but very slowly, and many gardeners treat 50°F as the practical minimum. At 50°F expect close to three weeks; at 70–75°F, about one to two weeks. A cheap soil thermometer pushed 2 inches into the bed removes the guesswork.

You can keep sowing through spring. Make a new sowing every 2–3 weeks, called succession sowing, until about 10–12 weeks before the weather turns hot (daytime highs regularly above 85°F). That spreads out the harvest instead of giving you 100 carrots on one weekend.

What soil temperature do carrots need to germinate?

Soil temperatureWhat to expect
Below 45°FSeeds sit dormant or rot; wait
45–50°FPossible, but very slow (about 3 weeks or more)
55–75°FBest range; sprouts in roughly 1–2 weeks
75–85°FFast but only if the bed stays moist; crusting is a risk
Above 85°FPoor, patchy germination; wait for fall

The challenge in late spring and summer is not the temperature itself but the surface drying out, because carrot seeds sit in the top half inch of soil.

When should you plant carrots for a fall harvest?

Count back from your first fall frost: most carrots need 60–80 days to mature, and growth slows as days shorten and soil cools, so add a few weeks. That is why the rule is 10–12 weeks before the first fall frost. Carrots do not mind a frost, and cold converts starch to sugar, so frosted carrots taste noticeably sweeter. In zones 6 and colder, a thick layer of straw mulch lets you keep harvesting well into winter.

The difficulty is that fall sowing often falls in the hottest weeks of summer. Keep the seedbed cool and damp by covering it with a board or burlap until sprouts appear, then remove it right away. Shade cloth also helps.

How do you plant carrots step by step?

  1. Prepare deep, loose soil. Loosen the bed 10–12 inches deep and remove stones, sticks and clods, which cause forked or stubby roots. A sandy loam is ideal; heavy clay should be amended with compost.
  2. Skip fresh manure and high-nitrogen fertilizer. Too much nitrogen gives lush tops and hairy, forked roots.
  3. Sow thinly. Scatter seed in a shallow furrow 1/4–1/2 inch deep, in rows 12 inches apart, or broadcast in a raised bed. Cover lightly with fine soil or compost.
  4. Keep the surface moist. Water gently every day or two (not heavy blasts) until seedlings are up, which can take up to three weeks in cool soil.
  5. Thin the seedlings. When they are 2 inches tall, thin to 1–2 inches apart, then thin again to 2–3 inches when the roots are about finger-width. Pinch rather than pull, since pulling can damage neighbors and the scent of crushed carrot tops attracts carrot rust flies.
  6. Water evenly at about 1 inch per week and mulch lightly once plants are 3–4 inches tall.
Why carrots fail to come up: sowing into soil colder than 50°F, letting the surface crust or dry out before the seedlings emerge, sowing too deep (more than 1/2 inch), and old seed. Carrot seed is only reliably viable for about 1–3 years, so replace any packet that has been in a hot garage.

What are the common carrot planting mistakes?

  1. Sowing too thick and never thinning. Crowded carrots stay skinny and twisted.
  2. Planting in rocky or compacted soil. The root forks around anything it hits.
  3. Planting in summer heat. Roots are woody and bitter when matured in hot weather; time them for spring or fall.
  4. Inconsistent watering. Dry spells followed by a soak make roots split.
  5. Leaving shoulders exposed. Soil or mulch around the root tops prevents green, bitter shoulders.

Frequently asked questions

Why aren't my carrot seeds germinating?

The usual causes are cold soil (below 50°F), a surface that dried out, seed sown too deep or old seed. Keep the top half inch evenly damp and be patient; cool soil can take three weeks.

How long does it take carrots to sprout?

Carrots sprout in about 1–3 weeks: roughly 7–14 days in 70–75°F soil, and up to three weeks at 50°F. They are one of the slowest vegetables to emerge.

Can carrots survive a frost after planting?

Yes. Carrot seedlings tolerate light frost, and mature roots handle freezes into the low 20s°F, especially under mulch. That is why you can sow 2–3 weeks before the last frost.

Should you soak carrot seeds before sowing?

Soaking is optional. A 12–24 hour soak in water can speed up sprouting a little, but the seeds must not dry out afterwards, so it only helps if you can keep the bed moist.

Can you plant carrots in summer?

Only for a fall harvest, and only if the seedbed is kept cool and damp. Sow 10–12 weeks before the first fall frost; in zones 9–10, wait until fall because summer soil is too hot.

How far apart should carrots be planted?

Thin carrots to 2–3 inches apart, or 1–2 inches for baby carrots. Rows go 12 inches apart.

The bottom line
