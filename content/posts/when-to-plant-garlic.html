Quick answer

Plant garlic in fall, from about the average first frost date to roughly 4 weeks later, and at least 2–4 weeks before the ground freezes solid. That means mid-September to early October in zones 3–5, October to mid-November in zones 6–7, and November to January in zones 8–10. Plant cloves 2 inches deep, 6 inches apart.

Garlic is the one crop most gardeners plant when everything else is winding down. Cloves planted in autumn grow roots, sit through winter, then shoot up in spring and are ready to harvest the following summer. Plant too early and the cloves send up tall green tops that freeze; plant too late and roots have no time to anchor before winter.

Dates depend on where you live. Use the table below for a first estimate, then find your exact dates by ZIP code in the planting calendar.

Key takeaways
  • Season: fall planting, harvest the next June–August (about 8–9 months).
  • Timing rule: after soil cools to roughly 60°F or below, and a few weeks before the ground freezes.
  • Depth and spacing: 2 inches deep, 6 inches apart, pointed end up.
  • Type by zone: hardneck for zones 3–7, softneck for zones 6–10.
  • Mulch: 4–6 inches of straw or shredded leaves in cold-winter areas.

When is the best time to plant garlic?

Garlic needs a stretch of cold weather (vernalization) to form a bulb with multiple cloves. Fall planting provides it. The goal is for cloves to grow roots in cool soil without growing much above ground. Extension services in cold states such as Cornell and Minnesota advise planting 4–6 weeks before the ground freezes, which in most of the North is late September through October.

A practical trigger is soil temperature: plant when the soil at 4 inches has cooled below about 60°F and is trending down. Garlic is very frost-tolerant. A few inches of green growth before winter will usually survive, and roots keep growing in soil above freezing.

Can you plant garlic in warm climates?

Yes, but there is a catch. In zones 9–10, winters may be too mild to vernalize cloves, and bulbs may form without distinct cloves. The fix is to pre-chill seed garlic in the refrigerator for 4–8 weeks (keep it away from ripening fruit), then plant in late fall or winter. Choose softneck varieties adapted to the South, which need less cold than hardneck.

How do you plant garlic step by step?

  1. Buy seed garlic. Use bulbs from a nursery or garlic grower, not supermarket garlic, which may be treated to prevent sprouting or be a variety unsuited to your climate.
  2. Prepare the bed. Choose a sunny spot with loose, well-drained soil. Work in 2–3 inches of compost. Garlic rots in soggy ground.
  3. Break the bulbs into cloves 1–2 days before planting, keeping the papery skin on each clove. Plant the largest cloves.
  4. Plant pointed end up, 2 inches deep (3 inches in cold zones 3–5), 6 inches apart, in rows 12 inches apart.
  5. Cover with soil and water well to settle it.
  6. Mulch. Add 4–6 inches of straw or shredded leaves after the soil cools, or immediately in zones 3–5.

A 4 × 8 ft raised bed planted on a 6-inch grid holds 8 × 16 = 128 cloves.

How long does garlic take to grow?

Expect about 8–9 months from planting to harvest. Shoots appear in late fall or early spring. Hardneck garlic sends up a curling scape in late spring to early summer; snip it so the plant puts energy into the bulb. Harvest when the lower third to half of the leaves have turned brown, typically between June and August depending on zone and variety. Cure the bulbs in a shady, airy place for 2–4 weeks.

What are common garlic planting mistakes?

Don't plant garlic in wet, heavy soil without improving drainage. Cloves sitting in saturated soil over winter rot, and fungal disease can persist in the bed. Raised beds or ridges help in clay.
  1. Planting too early. Warm fall soil makes tall tops that may be damaged by hard freezes.
  2. Planting too late. Cloves without roots can heave out of the soil in a freeze-thaw cycle.
  3. Planting upside down. The pointed end must face up, or shoots waste energy turning around.
  4. Skipping mulch in zones 3–6, where winter freeze-thaw can push cloves up.
  5. Replanting garlic in the same bed every year, which builds disease pressure. Rotate with a 3–4 year gap from other alliums.
  6. Over-fertilizing with nitrogen in spring after mid-season, which delays bulbing. Feed early, then stop around scape time.

What soil and care does garlic need through the season?

Garlic prefers fertile, loose, well-drained soil with a pH of about 6.0 to 7.0. A soil test tells you whether to add lime or sulfur. Work in compost before planting rather than fresh manure, which can burn cloves and introduce weeds.

  • Fall: plant and mulch; no further fertilizer needed.
  • Early spring: when shoots are 4–6 inches, pull back mulch if it is thick and apply a nitrogen feeding (blood meal or a balanced fertilizer at the label rate).
  • Spring to early summer: give about 1 inch of water per week if rain is short, and keep beds weeded because garlic competes poorly.
  • 2–3 weeks before harvest: stop watering so bulb wrappers dry and cure well.

How much seed garlic should you buy?

A 4 × 8 ft raised bed on a 6-inch grid holds 128 cloves. Hardneck bulbs average roughly 6 usable cloves, so you need about 21 bulbs. Softneck bulbs have around 12 or more cloves, so about 11 bulbs fill the same bed. Buy a few extras because small inner cloves are better eaten than planted. Larger cloves produce larger bulbs the following summer.

Frequently asked questions

Can you plant garlic in the spring?

Yes, as soon as the soil can be worked, but bulbs are usually smaller because garlic lacks the full cold period. For best size, plant in fall.

Is it too late to plant garlic in November?

Not in zones 6–9, where November is prime time. In zones 3–5 the ground may be frozen already; plant as soon as you can dig and mulch heavily, or wait until spring.

Do you need to soak garlic cloves before planting?

It's optional. Some growers soak cloves in water for a few hours or in a mild solution, but healthy seed garlic planted in good soil does well without it.

How many garlic cloves do I plant per square foot?

On a 6-inch grid you plant 4 cloves per square foot. A 4 × 8 ft bed (32 sq ft) holds 128 cloves.

Can I plant garlic from the grocery store?

It may sprout, but it is often treated to inhibit sprouting and is usually not adapted to your climate. Seed garlic from a nursery is more reliable.

When do I harvest garlic planted in fall?

Harvest the following summer, usually June to August, when the lower leaves are brown but 5–6 green leaves remain.

The bottom line

Plant garlic in fall, from about your first frost date to four weeks later, and a few weeks before the ground freezes. Zones 3–5 plant from mid-September to October, zones 6–7 from late October to November, and zones 8–10 from November into January with pre-chilled cloves.
