Our promise

Every guide on Home Garden Lot is researched from authoritative sources, reviewed by a person before it's published, and corrected openly when we get something wrong. No advertiser or affiliate partner influences what we write.

Where our information comes from

Gardening advice varies by climate, soil and season, so we rely on sources that test their recommendations:

  • Land-grant university extension services for planting dates, fertilizer rates, pest management and soil science.
  • U.S. government data: NOAA climate normals for frost dates, the USDA Plant Hardiness Zone Map, and EPA guidance on chemicals and water.
  • Product labels and manufacturer instructions for anything you spray, spread or install. The label is the law for pesticides and fertilizers.
  • Peer-reviewed research where extension guidance is thin.

Each guide ends with a Sources section naming the institutions we used. We don't cite other blogs as evidence.

How a guide is made

  1. Pick a real question readers ask, one guide per question, so answers stay focused.
  2. Research and outline from the sources above.
  3. Calculate, don't guess. Quantities, conversions, coverage rates and costs are computed and double-checked. Prices are labeled as typical U.S. ranges, never quotes.
  4. Human review. An editor reads every draft before publishing, checks each number and claim against its source, and adds first-hand experience and photos where we have them.
  5. Publish with a date. Every guide shows when it was last reviewed.

How we use AI tools

We use AI writing tools to help with research summaries, outlines and first drafts, much like a research assistant. AI never publishes anything on its own. A person checks every fact, number and recommendation against the sources listed in the guide, rewrites as needed, and is responsible for what goes live. AI-generated images, when we use them, are illustrations, not photos of real results, and we don't present them as our own garden.

First-hand experience

When a guide includes something we did ourselves (a measurement, a cost, a mistake), it's real. We never invent personal experiences, test results, reviews or quotes. If we haven't tried something, the guide relies on the research and says so.

Calculators and data tools

Our calculators use standard geometry and coverage rates published by extension services and manufacturers, and each page explains its formula. Frost dates at tool.homegardenlot.com come straight from NOAA's 1991–2020 U.S. Climate Normals; the method and limits are published, and the build code is open source.

Independence, ads and affiliate links

  • Advertisers don't see or approve content, and ads are clearly separated from articles.
  • Affiliate links, where used, are disclosed and never change our recommendations. We don't accept free products in exchange for coverage, and we don't sell links or sponsored posts.
  • Full details: Disclaimer & Affiliate Disclosure.

Safety-first advice

For pesticides, power tools, electrical work and structural projects, we point you to the product label, local building codes or a licensed professional. We don't give medical, legal or pesticide-dosage advice beyond the label.

Updates

We review seasonal guides before their season (for example, spring planting guides each January and February) and update prices, dates and recommendations when sources change. The "Last reviewed" date on each guide shows when it was last checked.

Corrections policy

If you find an error, please tell us with the page and what looks wrong. We check every report against the original source. Confirmed factual errors are fixed promptly, and significant corrections are noted at the end of the guide with the date. Typos and wording changes are fixed without a note.
