Quick answer

Divide the patio area by the area of one paver, then add 5–10% for cuts and breakage (10–15% for herringbone or curves). Standard 4 × 8 in pavers come to 4.5 per sq ft, so a 12 × 12 ft (144 sq ft) patio needs about 648 pavers, or roughly 715 with 10% extra. In metric, 200 × 100 mm blocks come to 50 per m².

A paver patio has three parts to buy: the pavers, the compacted gravel base and the sand. This guide covers all three. The paver calculator, paver base calculator and polymeric sand calculator do the math for any size.

Key takeaways
  • Pavers: area (sq ft) × pavers per sq ft, plus 5–15% waste.
  • 4 × 8 in pavers: 4.5 per sq ft. 12 × 12 in: 1 per sq ft.
  • Base: 4–6 in (100–150 mm) of compacted crushed stone for patios and paths; 8–12 in for driveways.
  • Bedding sand: 1 in (25 mm), about 1 cubic yard per 324 sq ft.

How do you calculate how many pavers you need?

  1. Find the area in square feet (length × width; split odd shapes into rectangles).
  2. Find one paver's area: length (in) × width (in) ÷ 144.
  3. Divide the patio area by the paver area.
  4. Add waste: 5% for straight running bond, 10% for basketweave and borders, 10–15% for herringbone, circles and curves.

Example: a 10 × 14 ft patio (140 sq ft) in 6 × 9 in pavers: 6 × 9 ÷ 144 = 0.375 sq ft each, so 140 ÷ 0.375 = 374 pavers. With 10% extra, buy about 411.

How much base gravel and sand do you need?

Base: area × base depth (ft) ÷ 27 = cubic yards of crushed stone (often sold as paver base or crusher run). For a 144 sq ft patio with a 4 in base: 144 × 0.333 ÷ 27 = 1.78 cu yd, or about 2.5 tons. Compact it in 2 in layers. See how much gravel do I need for tons and coverage.

Bedding sand: 1 in of coarse concrete sand. 144 sq ft × 0.083 ft ÷ 27 = 0.44 cu yd, or about 10–11 bags of 0.5 cu ft. The sand calculator converts it to tons.

Joint sand: polymeric sand coverage depends on the paver size and joint width, so check the bag's coverage chart. Wider joints and smaller pavers use more.

How deep should you dig for pavers?

Add the paver thickness (usually 2⅜ in / 60 mm), 1 in of sand and the base depth. For a patio with a 4 in base, that's about 7½ in (190 mm) below the finished surface. Use a 6 in base on clay or in freeze-thaw climates, and slope the surface about ¼ in per foot (2%) away from the house so water drains off.

Frequently asked questions

How many pavers do I need for a 10×10 patio?

About 450 standard 4 × 8 in pavers for 100 sq ft, or about 495 with 10% extra for cuts. With 12 × 12 in pavers you need 100, or about 110 with extra.

How many 4×8 pavers are in a square foot?

4.5, because each 4 × 8 in paver covers 32 square inches and a square foot is 144 square inches.

How much extra pavers should I buy?

Buy 5% extra for straight patterns, 10% for most patios and 10–15% for herringbone, circular designs or lots of curves.

How thick should the base be under pavers?

About 4–6 inches of compacted crushed stone for patios and walkways, 8–12 inches for driveways, plus 1 inch of bedding sand.

The bottom line

Pavers needed = area ÷ area of one paver + 5–15% for waste. Budget too for about 4–6 inches of compacted gravel base and 1 inch of sand, since the base is what keeps a patio flat for decades.
