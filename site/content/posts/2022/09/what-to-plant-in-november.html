Quick answer

In November, plant garlic, spring-flowering bulbs, and trees, shrubs and roses while the ground can still be dug. In mild climates (USDA zones 8–10), November is also prime time to sow or transplant cool-season vegetables such as lettuce, spinach, kale, broccoli, carrots, radishes and peas. In cold zones (3–6), finish bulbs and garlic before the ground freezes, then grow herbs and microgreens indoors.

What you can plant in November depends almost entirely on your climate. In the North it is the last call for anything that goes into the ground; in the South and on the West Coast it is the start of the best vegetable season of the year. Use the table below, then check your average first frost date by ZIP code to fine-tune the timing.

Key takeaways
  • Everywhere the ground isn't frozen: garlic, spring bulbs, trees, shrubs and bare-root roses.
  • Zones 3–6: plant bulbs and garlic at least 2–4 weeks before the soil freezes, then mulch.
  • Zones 7–8: garlic, onion sets, spinach and hardy greens, plus pansies and violas.
  • Zones 9–10: a full cool-season vegetable garden, from lettuce and broccoli to carrots and peas.
  • Indoors anywhere: microgreens, herbs on a sunny windowsill, and paperwhite bulbs for winter blooms.

Is it too late to plant garlic in November?

Usually not. Garlic is planted in fall so the cloves grow roots before winter. In zones 6–9 November is the main planting window; in zones 3–5 plant as soon as you can dig, as long as the ground isn't frozen, and mulch heavily. See our guide to garlic planting dates by zone.

Can you still plant spring bulbs in November?

Yes. Tulips, daffodils, hyacinths, crocus and alliums need a cold period to bloom, so fall planting is ideal. Plant them once the soil has cooled, and at least a few weeks before it freezes so roots can form. Set bulbs pointed end up at a depth of about 2–3 times their height, water them in, and add mulch in cold areas. In zones 8–10, winters are often too warm for tulips and hyacinths: buy pre-chilled bulbs or chill them in the refrigerator for 10–12 weeks (away from fruit) before planting.

Should you plant trees and shrubs in November?

Fall is one of the best times to plant hardy trees and shrubs. The air is cool, so plants lose less water, while the soil is still warm enough for roots to grow. Plant until the ground freezes, water deeply after planting and every week or two without rain until freeze-up, and spread 2–3 inches of mulch over the root zone, keeping it a few inches away from the trunk. Bare-root roses and fruit trees can go in now in mild climates; in cold zones wait for early spring.

Which vegetables grow best in a November garden?

In zones 8–10, choose quick, cold-tolerant crops and sow in succession every 2–3 weeks:

  • Leafy greens: lettuce, spinach, arugula, kale, Swiss chard and collards.
  • Roots: radishes (about a month to harvest), carrots, beets and turnips.
  • Brassicas: broccoli, cabbage and cauliflower from transplants.
  • Peas and fava beans for a late-winter harvest.
  • Herbs: cilantro, parsley and dill grow better in cool weather than in summer.

What can you grow indoors in November?

  • Microgreens: ready in 1–3 weeks on a windowsill or under a grow light.
  • Herbs: parsley, chives, mint and basil in a bright south- or west-facing window.
  • Paperwhites and amaryllis: plant now for flowers in December and January.

What other garden jobs should you do in November?

  • Spread compost or shredded leaves over empty beds to protect the soil over winter.
  • Mulch perennials, strawberries and garlic after the ground starts to cool.
  • Clean, sharpen and oil tools before storing them.
  • Keep watering new plantings until the ground freezes.

Frequently asked questions

Is November too late to plant vegetables?

In cold zones, yes for most crops outdoors, though garlic can still go in if the soil isn't frozen. In zones 8–10, November is one of the best months to plant cool-season vegetables.

Can I plant tulip bulbs in November?

Yes, as long as the ground isn't frozen. In warm zones, chill the bulbs for 10–12 weeks before planting.

What flowers can you plant in November?

Pansies, violas and snapdragons in mild climates, and spring bulbs anywhere the soil can still be dug.

Can you plant grass seed in November?

It's usually too late for cool-season grass seed in the North, where early fall is the best time. You can dormant-seed in late fall so seed sprouts in spring, or wait until spring. See when to overseed your lawn.

The bottom line

November is for garlic, spring bulbs, trees and shrubs almost everywhere the ground can be dug, and for a full cool-season vegetable garden in zones 8–10. Check your frost dates and your hardiness zone, then plant before the soil freezes.
