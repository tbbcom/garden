Quick answer

To get rid of ants for good, kill the colony, not just the ants you see. Put ant bait stations next to the trail, leave the trail alone so ants carry the bait home, remove food and water they're after, and seal the cracks they use to get in. Most indoor infestations clear up within 1–2 weeks. Call a professional for carpenter ants, fire ants, or ants that keep returning after baiting.

Spraying the ants you can see feels satisfying, but it rarely solves the problem: the queen and most of the colony stay safe in the nest, and new workers keep coming. The methods that work target the nest through the workers, then make your home less attractive so ants don't come back. Here is what to do, in order.

Key takeaways
  • Bait, don't spray: slow-acting bait reaches the queen; sprays kill only foragers.
  • Be patient: expect more ants for a day or two, then fewer within 1–2 weeks.
  • Remove the reason they came: crumbs, sticky spills, pet food and leaks.
  • Seal entry points with caulk once the colony is gone.
  • Safety: use enclosed bait stations and follow the product label, especially with children and pets.

Why are ants coming into my house?

Ants come indoors looking for food, water and shelter. Scouts find a source, then lay a scent trail that other workers follow, which is why one ant quickly becomes hundreds. Common attractions are sugary spills, crumbs, pet food, garbage, and damp areas around sinks, dishwashers and windows. Infestations often start in spring and after heavy rain, when outdoor nests flood or food outside runs short.

What is the most effective way to get rid of ants?

Baiting works best for most household ants because workers share the bait with the rest of the colony, including the queen. Compare the main options:

How do you use ant bait correctly?

  1. Find the trail and, if you can, the spot where ants enter, such as a window frame, baseboard gap or outlet.
  2. Place bait stations right beside the trail, not on top of it, out of reach of children and pets. Read and follow the product label.
  3. Leave the ants alone. Don't spray or wipe the trail; you want workers to keep carrying bait home.
  4. Remove competing food so the bait is the most attractive thing around.
  5. Check after a few days. If ants ignore the bait, try a different type: some ants prefer sugar-based baits, others protein or grease-based baits.
  6. Clean up once activity stops, usually within 1–2 weeks, then seal entry points.

Do vinegar and essential oils get rid of ants?

They help, but they don't eliminate a colony. Wiping counters and trails with soapy water or a 1:1 mix of white vinegar and water removes the scent trail, so fewer ants find their way back. Essential-oil sprays such as peppermint can repel ants briefly. Use these for cleanup and prevention, not as your only treatment. Vinegar can damage natural stone like marble and granite, so use soapy water there.

Be careful with homemade borax baits. Borax and boric acid are toxic to children and pets if eaten, and mixes that are too strong kill ants before they reach the nest. Enclosed, child-resistant commercial bait stations are the safer choice. Always follow the label, which is the legal guide for any pest product.

How do you keep ants from coming back?

  • Store food in sealed containers, including sugar, cereal and pet food.
  • Wipe counters and sweep floors daily in the kitchen, and rinse recycling.
  • Fix leaks and dry damp areas under sinks and around appliances.
  • Seal cracks around windows, doors, pipes and baseboards with caulk.
  • Trim branches and plants so they don't touch the house, and keep mulch a few inches away from the foundation.
  • Empty the trash often and keep outdoor bins closed.

When should you call an exterminator?

Call a licensed pest control professional if:

  • You see carpenter ants (large, usually black ants) or piles of fine sawdust near wood. They nest in damp wood and can damage your home.
  • You have fire ants around the house; their stings can be dangerous, especially for children and people with allergies.
  • Ants return within weeks after careful baiting, which may mean several colonies or a nest inside a wall.
  • You have tiny pale ants (often pharaoh ants) that keep spreading. Sprays can make these colonies split into several new ones, so they need a bait-based plan.

Ask for a written quote, what products they use and how they treat around pets and children.

Frequently asked questions

How long does it take to get rid of ants with bait?

You may see more ants for the first day or two as they collect the bait. Activity usually drops within a week and stops within about 2 weeks once the colony is gone.

What kills ants instantly?

Soapy water or a contact spray kills the ants it touches, but not the colony. For a lasting fix, use bait so workers carry it back to the nest.

Why do I suddenly have ants in my kitchen?

Usually because scouts found food or water, often after rain or a change in weather. Clean up spills, store food in sealed containers and bait the trail.

Is ant bait safe around pets?

Enclosed bait stations limit access, but no bait should be eaten by pets. Place stations where pets can't reach them and follow the label. Call your vet if a pet chews one.

Does cinnamon or coffee grounds keep ants away?

Strong scents can disrupt a trail briefly, but they don't kill the colony. Use them as a short-term deterrent at most.

The bottom line

Ants keep coming back when only the workers are killed. Bait beside the trail and leave it alone, remove food and water, and seal the gaps once activity stops. Call a professional for carpenter ants, fire ants or infestations that return after baiting.
