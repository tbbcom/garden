Quick answer

Lizards come indoors to hunt insects, so the lasting fix is to remove their food and their way in: cut down insects attracted to lights, seal gaps around doors, windows and pipes, keep rooms dry and clutter-free, and gently catch and release any lizard already inside. Common house lizards (house geckos) are harmless to people and actually eat pests, so humane removal is all you need.

House geckos and small lizards are common in warm climates, including much of Southeast Asia and the southern United States. They aren't dangerous, but they leave droppings and many people would rather not share a bedroom with them. Repellent sprays and old tricks rarely work for long, because lizards keep coming back as long as there are insects to eat and gaps to get in through. Here is what works.

Key takeaways
  • No insects, no lizards: control moths, flies and ants first.
  • Block the way in: screens, door sweeps and sealed gaps.
  • Make rooms less inviting: less clutter, fewer hiding spots, less moisture.
  • Catch and release with a container and card; avoid glue traps and poisons.

Why are lizards coming into my house?

  • Food: insects gather around indoor and porch lights at night, and lizards follow them.
  • Warmth and shelter: gaps behind furniture, cabinets, picture frames and air-conditioner units are ideal hiding places.
  • Moisture: damp bathrooms, kitchens and leaky pipes attract both insects and lizards.
  • Easy access: open windows without screens, gaps under doors and holes around pipes and vents.

How do you get rid of lizards in the house?

  1. Reduce insects. Fix fly, ant and moth problems, keep food covered and take out the trash daily. See how to get rid of flies and how to get rid of ants.
  2. Change your lighting. Switch off unnecessary lights at night, keep curtains closed, and use warm-colored or yellow outdoor bulbs, which attract fewer insects than bright white ones.
  3. Seal entry points. Fit fine mesh screens on windows and vents, add door sweeps, and fill gaps around pipes and cables with sealant.
  4. Declutter. Move furniture slightly away from walls, clear piles of boxes and newspapers, and check behind frames and appliances.
  5. Keep it cool and dry. Lizards prefer warm spots; air conditioning, fans and fixing leaks all make a room less appealing.
  6. Catch and release. Place a container over the lizard, slide a piece of card underneath, and release it outside away from doors.

Do eggshells, onions or naphthalene balls repel lizards?

There's little evidence that eggshells, onion, garlic or peacock feathers keep lizards away. Naphthalene (mothballs) is toxic to people and pets, especially children, so don't scatter it around the house. Pepper sprays and chemical sprays can harm the animal and irritate your own eyes and lungs, and they don't stop new lizards coming in. Glue traps are cruel and also catch other wildlife; avoid them.

Are house lizards harmful?

Common house geckos don't bite unless handled roughly and aren't venomous. Like many reptiles, they can carry Salmonella, so wipe up droppings with disinfectant, keep food covered, and wash your hands after handling anything a lizard has touched. In many gardens they are welcome guests, because they eat mosquitoes, moths and cockroaches.

How do you keep lizards out of the garden and porch?

  • Trim dense shrubs and climbers away from walls and windows.
  • Clear piles of wood, stones and debris near the house.
  • Keep outdoor lights off when not needed, or use motion-sensor lights.

Frequently asked questions

What smell do lizards hate?

Strong smells such as pepper, garlic or coffee are often suggested, but they work briefly at best. Removing insects and sealing gaps is far more effective.

Is it bad to have lizards in the house?

Not dangerous: house lizards eat insects and rarely cause harm. The main concerns are droppings and possible Salmonella, so keep food covered and clean up droppings.

How do I get a lizard out of my room at night?

Turn off the lights, open a door to a lit hallway or outside, and the lizard will usually head toward the light and insects. Or gently catch it with a container and card.

How do I stop lizards from coming back?

Keep insects down, use window and vent screens, seal gaps, and reduce clutter and moisture. Without food and entry points, lizards move on.

The bottom line

Lizards come for insects, warmth and shelter. Cut the insects, seal the gaps, keep rooms dry and tidy, and release any lizard you find outside. Skip toxic mothballs, sprays and glue traps; they're unsafe and don't solve the problem.
