Quick answer

Grow ginger from a fresh, plump rhizome (root) with visible growth buds. Plant pieces 1–2 inches deep, buds up, in a wide pot of loose, well-drained potting mix, and keep it warm (about 70–90°F, 21–32°C), lightly shaded and evenly moist. Shoots appear in a few weeks; harvest young ginger after about 4–6 months, or mature ginger at 8–10 months when the leaves turn yellow.

Ginger (Zingiber officinale) is a tropical plant grown for its spicy rhizome. It is easy to grow in a pot, even from ginger bought at the store, and it grows year-round in warm climates such as Malaysia, Florida and Hawaii. In cooler areas, grow it in a container outdoors in summer and bring it indoors before nights drop below about 50°F (10°C).

Key takeaways
  • Start with a rhizome: ginger is grown from pieces of root, not seed.
  • Warmth matters most: it grows best between 70 and 90°F (21–32°C).
  • Shade and moisture: bright, indirect light or morning sun; moist but never waterlogged soil.
  • Wide, shallow container: rhizomes spread sideways, so width beats depth.

How do you choose ginger to plant?

  • Pick firm, plump rhizomes with smooth skin and visible "eyes" (small buds), ideally ones that are already starting to sprout.
  • Organic ginger is a good choice; conventional ginger is sometimes treated to slow sprouting. Soaking the rhizome in water overnight before planting helps either kind.
  • Cut large rhizomes into pieces about 1–2 inches long, each with at least one or two buds, and let the cuts dry for a day.

How do you plant ginger?

  1. Choose a wide pot at least 12 inches across and 8–12 inches deep, with drainage holes.
  2. Fill with loose, rich potting mix, or mix potting soil with compost. Ginger rots in heavy, soggy soil.
  3. Plant pieces 1–2 inches deep, buds facing up, about 6–8 inches apart.
  4. Water lightly and keep the pot somewhere warm. Shoots usually appear in 2–8 weeks; be patient and don't overwater while you wait.

How do you care for ginger plants?

  • Light: bright, indirect light or 2–5 hours of morning sun. Hot afternoon sun can scorch the leaves.
  • Water: keep the soil evenly moist but not soggy; water when the top inch feels dry.
  • Humidity: ginger likes humid air; mist the leaves or group pots together indoors.
  • Feeding: add compost at planting and feed with a balanced liquid fertilizer every 3–4 weeks during active growth.
  • Hilling: if rhizomes push up to the surface, cover them with a little more soil or mulch.

When and how do you harvest ginger?

StageWhenWhat you get
Young gingerAbout 4–6 months after plantingTender, mild, thin-skinned rhizomes; no need to peel
Mature gingerAbout 8–10 months, when the leaves yellow and die backFull-flavored, fibrous rhizomes that store well

You can harvest a little at a time by gently digging at the edge of the pot and snapping off a piece, leaving the rest to keep growing. Keep a few plump pieces with buds to replant for your next crop. Store fresh ginger in the refrigerator, unpeeled, for a few weeks, or freeze it.

Can you grow ginger in the ground?

Yes, in warm, frost-free climates (roughly USDA zones 9–12 and the tropics). Choose a lightly shaded spot with loose, well-drained soil rich in compost, and plant at the start of the rainy or warm season. Raised beds help in heavy clay.

Frequently asked questions

Can you grow ginger from store-bought ginger?

Usually, yes. Choose plump pieces with visible buds, soak them overnight, and plant 1–2 inches deep. Organic ginger tends to sprout more reliably.

How long does ginger take to sprout?

Typically 2–8 weeks, depending on warmth. Keep the soil lightly moist and warm while you wait.

Can you grow ginger from seed?

Not practically. Cultivated ginger rarely produces seed and is grown from rhizome pieces.

Does ginger need full sun?

No. It prefers partial shade or bright, indirect light; strong afternoon sun can burn the leaves.

Can ginger grow indoors?

Yes, in a warm, bright room away from cold drafts. Keep humidity up and water evenly.

The bottom line

Plant a plump ginger rhizome 1–2 inches deep in a wide pot of loose soil, keep it warm, lightly shaded and evenly moist, and harvest young ginger at 4–6 months or mature ginger at 8–10 months. Save a few pieces to replant.
