Quick answer

Grow Brussels sprouts as a long, cool-season crop: start seeds indoors, transplant in spring for a fall harvest (or in late summer for a winter harvest in mild climates), space plants 18–24 inches apart in full sun and rich soil, and keep them evenly watered and fed. Pick sprouts from the bottom of the stalk when they are firm and 1–2 inches across; a light frost makes them sweeter.

Brussels sprouts need patience: most varieties take about 90–110 days from transplanting to harvest. The three things that make the biggest difference are timing the crop so the sprouts mature in cool weather, keeping water and feeding steady, and letting a few frosts sweeten them before you pick.

Key takeaways
  • Secret 1, timing: plan for sprouts to mature in cool fall or winter weather.
  • Secret 2, steady care: rich soil, about 1–1.5 inches of water a week, and a mid-season feeding.
  • Secret 3, frost and topping: light frosts improve flavor; pinching the top helps sprouts size up.
  • Spacing: 18–24 inches between plants; stake tall stalks in windy spots.

How do you plant Brussels sprouts?

  1. Start seeds ¼–½ inch deep in seed trays and grow them under bright light for 4–6 weeks.
  2. Choose the spot: full sun (6+ hours a day) and fertile, well-drained soil with plenty of compost. Avoid beds where cabbage-family crops grew in the last 3 years.
  3. Transplant seedlings 18–24 inches apart, in rows about 30–36 inches apart, setting them slightly deeper than they grew in the tray.
  4. Water in well and mulch to keep the soil cool and moist.

How do you care for Brussels sprouts?

  • Water: about 1–1.5 inches a week. Uneven watering leads to loose, small sprouts.
  • Feeding: they are heavy feeders. Side-dress with compost or a nitrogen fertilizer, following the label, when plants are about a foot tall.
  • Support: stalks can grow 2–3 feet tall; stake them in windy gardens and firm soil around the base.
  • Lower leaves: remove yellowing leaves at the bottom of the stalk to improve airflow.
  • Pests: cabbage worms and aphids are the main problems. Cover young plants with fine insect netting, check leaf undersides, and hose off aphids.

What is "topping" Brussels sprouts?

About 3–4 weeks before you want to harvest, pinch or cut off the top growing tip of the stalk. The plant then puts its energy into sizing up the sprouts already formed, so more of them mature at the same time. Skip topping if you prefer a long, gradual harvest from the bottom up.

When and how do you harvest Brussels sprouts?

  • Pick sprouts when they are firm, tightly closed and about 1–2 inches across, starting at the bottom of the stalk.
  • Twist them off or cut with a knife; more sprouts keep forming higher up.
  • Light frosts convert some starches to sugars, so sprouts picked after a few cold nights taste sweeter. Plants tolerate temperatures into the low 20s °F (around −5°C).
  • Before a hard freeze, cut the whole stalk and store it in a cool place, or keep sprouts in the refrigerator for a few weeks.

Frequently asked questions

How long do Brussels sprouts take to grow?

About 90–110 days from transplanting for most varieties, plus 4–6 weeks for seedlings, so plan for a long season.

Why are my Brussels sprouts loose and leafy?

Usually because they matured in hot weather or the plants were short of water or nutrients. Time the crop for cool weather and keep watering steady.

Can you grow Brussels sprouts in containers?

Yes, one plant per large pot of at least 5 gallons, with rich potting mix and regular watering and feeding.

Do Brussels sprouts come back every year?

No. They are biennial but grown as annuals; plant new seedlings each year.

The bottom line

Time Brussels sprouts to mature in cool weather, give them rich soil, steady water and a mid-season feeding, and let a few light frosts sweeten them before harvesting from the bottom up.
