If you're looking for a delicious and healthy fruit to add to your diet, strawberries are a great option! What Type of Strawberries to Grow



When you're deciding what type of strawberry to grow, you'll want to consider the climate you live in as well as the amount of space you have. If you live in an area with a mild climate, you can grow strawberries all year round. However, if you live in a colder climate, you'll want to choose a variety that is tolerant of cold weather.

If you have limited space, consider growing strawberries in containers. This way, you can move them around to get the best sunlight and protection from the elements.

Once you've decided on the type of strawberry to grow, it's time to get started! Check out our tips on how to grow strawberries for a successful crop.

How to Plant Strawberries



Looking to add some strawberries to your garden? Here's a quick guide on how to plant them!

Strawberries are a relatively easy fruit to grow and make for a delicious, healthy snack. They can be grown in most regions with little effort and will provide you with an abundance of berries over the course of the season.

When planting strawberries, it is important to choose a location that receives full sun and has well-drained soil. You can either grow them in raised beds or in the ground, but make sure the area is free of any weeds or other vegetation that might compete for resources.

To plant, simply dig a hole that is large enough to accommodate the roots of the strawberry plant. Gently loosen the roots and place the plant in the hole, making sure that the crown (where the leaves meet the stem) is level with the soil surface. Backfill the hole with soil and water thoroughly.

If you are growing strawberries in an area where they are not native, you will need to provide some support for the plants as they grow. This can be done by placing straw or mulch around them, or by using cages or trellises.

With proper care, your strawberries will thrive and produce an abundance of fruit for you to enjoy!

How to Plant Strawberries in the Ground

2. Prepare the soil. Loosen the topsoil with a spade or tiller to a depth of 12 inches, then mix in some organic matter such as compost or manure.

3. Plant the strawberries. Place your plants 18 inches apart in rows that are 3 to 4 feet apart. You can gently loosen the roots before planting, and be sure to plant them at the same depth they were growing in the container. Well water after planting.

4. Mulch around the plants. This will help keep the soil moist and prevent weeds from competing with your strawberries for water and nutrients.

5. Fertilize frequently. Apply a balanced fertiliser every few weeks during the growing season. You can also side-dress with compost or manure in early spring and again in late summer.

6. Keep an eye out for pests and diseases. Check your plants regularly for signs of pests or disease, and take action immediately if you see anything.

Growing Strawberries From Bare Root Runners



One of the easiest and most rewarding ways to grow your own food is by starting with bare-root runners. These are young strawberry plants that have been grown from seed and are ready to be transplanted into your garden.

Here are a few tips on how to get started:
  • Choose a sunny spot in your garden that has well-drained soil.
  • Dig a hole large enough to accommodate the root ball of the runner.
  • Gently backfill the hole, making sure not to damage the roots.
  • Water the plant well and keep the soil moist until it is established.
  • Enjoy your delicious homegrown strawberries!

Growing Strawberries in Pots



If you live in an apartment or don't have a lot of space for gardening, you can still enjoy fresh strawberries by growing them in pots! All you need is a sunny spot and some basic supplies.

To get started, choose a pot that is at least 12 inches wide and has drainage holes. Fill the pot with a quality potting mix, and then water it well.

Next, select healthy strawberry plants from your local nursery or garden center. Be sure to pick varieties that are bred for container growing, such as 'Alpine' or 'Tristar'.

When you're ready to plant, dig a hole in the potting mix that is large enough to accommodate the roots of your strawberry plant. Take the plant out of its pot carefully and put it in the hole. Backfill the hole with potting mix, and then water your plant well.

For best results, place your pot in a sunny spot and water it regularly. Use a balanced fertiliser and apply it every few weeks. And finally, enjoy those delicious strawberries when they're ripe!

How to Care for Strawberry Plants



If you're lucky enough to have your own strawberry patch, here are some tips on how to keep your plants healthy and productive.

First, strawberries are shallow-rooted plants, so they need consistent moisture. Water them deeply and regularly, especially during dry periods.

Second, add organic matter to the soil each year to help retain moisture and nutrients. You can do this by spreading compost around the base of the plants.

Third, mulch heavily around the plants to further help with moisture retention and weed control.

Fourth, remove any runners (side shoots) that appear, as these will take away energy from the main plant. However, you can root them if you want to create new plants.

Finally, give the plants a boost of nitrogen-rich fertilizer early in the season to encourage growth. Just be sure not to overdo it, as too much fertilizer can actually harm the plants.

With a little care, your strawberry plants will be thriving in no time!

When to Harvest Strawberries



The best time to harvest strawberries is in the morning, after the dew has evaporated. You should also wait until the berries are fully red - this usually takes about three weeks after the flowers have bloomed. Gently twist the berry to see if it comes off easily; if it doesn't, it's not quite ready yet.
