Quick answer

Measure the total rise (floor to floor), divide by about 7.5 inches (190 mm) and round up to get the number of risers; then divide the total rise by that number for the exact riser height. You need one fewer tread than risers. In the US, residential code (IRC) allows risers up to 7¾ in and treads of at least 10 in; a comfortable stair satisfies roughly 2 × rise + run = 24–25 in (610–635 mm).

Every step in a staircase must be the same height. Even a small difference trips people. So you start from the total height, divide it evenly, and then check the result against code and comfort. The stair calculator and stair stringer calculator do the math and the stringer layout.

Key takeaways
  • Risers: total rise ÷ 7.5 in, rounded up.
  • Exact riser height: total rise ÷ number of risers.
  • Treads: number of risers − 1 (when the top step is the upper floor).
  • US code (IRC): riser max 7¾ in (197 mm), tread min 10 in (254 mm), headroom min 6 ft 8 in (2.03 m).
  • UK (private stairs): rise max 220 mm, going min 220 mm, pitch max 42°.

How do you calculate stair rise and run step by step?

  1. Measure the total rise: from the finished lower floor to the finished upper floor, in inches or mm.
  2. Find the number of risers: divide by 7.5 in (190 mm) and round up.
  3. Calculate the exact riser height: total rise ÷ number of risers.
  4. Count the treads: risers − 1.
  5. Choose the tread depth (run): usually 10–11 in (254–280 mm).
  6. Total run: treads × tread depth. This is how far the stair extends horizontally.

Example: total rise 105 in. 105 ÷ 7.5 = 14 risers, each exactly 7.5 in. Treads = 13. With 10.5 in treads, total run = 13 × 10.5 = 136.5 in (about 11 ft 4 in).

What is the most comfortable stair rise and run?

A long-standing rule of thumb is 2 × rise + run = 24–25 inches (about 610–635 mm; the UK rule is 550–700 mm). For example, a 7-inch rise with an 11-inch run gives 25 inches. Lower rises with deeper treads feel easier; steep stairs save space but are harder to climb.

How do you lay out a stair stringer?

  1. Use a framing square with stair gauges set to your riser height and tread depth.
  2. Mark each step along a straight 2×12 (about 38 × 285 mm) board.
  3. Reduce the bottom riser by the thickness of one tread so all finished steps end up equal.
  4. Cut, test-fit and use it as a template for the other stringers. Space stringers to suit your tread material.

Frequently asked questions

What is the standard stair rise and run?

Typically about 7–7¾ inches of rise and 10–11 inches of run in US homes, within the IRC limits of 7¾-inch maximum rise and 10-inch minimum tread.

How many steps do I need for a 9-foot ceiling?

Measure floor to floor, not ceiling height: with a 9 ft ceiling and about 12 in of floor framing, total rise is about 120 in, so 16 risers of 7.5 in and 15 treads.

Is the number of treads the same as risers?

No. There is usually one fewer tread than risers, because the top step is the upper floor.

What is the 2R + T rule?

Twice the riser height plus the tread depth should be about 24–25 inches for a comfortable stair.

The bottom line

Risers = total rise ÷ 7.5 in, rounded up; riser height = total rise ÷ risers; treads = risers − 1. Keep every step identical, stay within your local code, and aim for 2R + T of about 24–25 inches.
