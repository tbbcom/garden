Quick answer

Pounds of salt = pool gallons × the ppm increase you need × 8.34 ÷ 1,000,000. To bring a new 10,000-gallon pool from 0 to 3,200 ppm you need about 267 lb, or roughly seven 40 lb bags. In metric it's simpler: kg = litres × ppm ÷ 1,000,000, so 40,000 L to 3,200 ppm takes 128 kg. Always check your salt cell's target range first.

A salt chlorine generator makes chlorine from dissolved salt, but only within the range its maker specifies. With too little salt it stops producing, and with too much you get corrosion and error codes. You can't remove salt except by draining, so add it in stages. The pool salt calculator works out the amount, and the pool volume calculator finds your gallons.

Key takeaways
  • Formula: lb = gallons × (target ppm − current ppm) × 8.34 ÷ 1,000,000.
  • Rule of thumb: about 8.3 lb per 1,000 gallons raises salt by 1,000 ppm.
  • Target: usually 2,700–3,400 ppm, often 3,200; your cell's manual is the authority.
  • Salt type: 99%+ pure sodium chloride with no iodine or anti-caking agents.
  • Add in stages: add about ¾ of the amount, circulate for 24 hours, retest, then top up.

How do you calculate pool salt?

  1. Find the volume. Rectangle: length × width × average depth (ft) × 7.48. Round: diameter² × average depth × 5.9. Oval: length × width × average depth × 5.9.
  2. Test the current salt level with strips or a digital meter. Refilled water is not zero if it was topped up or rained into.
  3. Subtract the current level from the target ppm.
  4. Multiply gallons × ppm increase × 8.34, then divide by 1,000,000.

Example: a 16 × 32 ft pool with an average depth of 5 ft holds 16 × 32 × 5 × 7.48 = 19,149 gallons. It tests at 2,400 ppm and the target is 3,200 ppm, so the increase is 800 ppm. 19,149 × 800 × 8.34 ÷ 1,000,000 = 128 lb, about three 40 lb bags plus a bit.

How do you add salt to a pool?

  1. Turn the salt cell off (or set it to 0%) and run the pump.
  2. Pour the salt around the shallow end, away from the skimmer, and brush it toward the deep end to help it dissolve.
  3. Circulate for about 24 hours, then retest before adding more.
  4. Turn the cell back on once the reading is in range.

Salt doesn't evaporate. You only lose it through splash-out, backwashing and draining after heavy rain, so top-ups are small after the first fill. Keep your other chemistry in range too. Stabilizer (cyanuric acid), pH and free chlorine still matter, and you'll still need shock occasionally (see the pool shock calculator).

Frequently asked questions

How much salt do I need for a 10,000-gallon pool?

About 267 lb to raise it from 0 to 3,200 ppm, which is roughly seven 40 lb bags. If it already has some salt, use only the difference.

How many bags of salt for a 20,000-gallon pool?

About 13–14 bags of 40 lb (534 lb) from zero to 3,200 ppm.

Can I use table salt or water softener salt in a pool?

Only if it is 99%+ pure sodium chloride without iodine, anti-caking agents or rust-removal additives; buy salt labelled for pools to be safe.

What happens if a pool has too much salt?

Most cells show a high-salt error and the water tastes salty, and very high levels can corrode metal parts. The only fix is to partially drain and refill with fresh water.

The bottom line

Salt (lb) = gallons × ppm increase × 8.34 ÷ 1,000,000, which is about 267 lb per 10,000 gallons from zero to 3,200 ppm. Confirm your cell's target, add salt in stages with the pump running and retest after 24 hours.
