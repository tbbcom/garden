Quick answer

One gallon of paint covers about 350–400 sq ft per coat (roughly 9–10 m² per litre). Measure the wall area, subtract about 20 sq ft per door and 15 sq ft per window, multiply by the number of coats (usually two), and divide by 350. A typical 12 × 12 ft bedroom with 8 ft ceilings needs about 2 gallons for two coats on the walls.

Buying the right amount of paint saves a second trip mid-job and avoids leftover cans. All you need is a tape measure and the coverage figure on the can. For outside walls, the exterior paint calculator does the math for you.

Key takeaways
  • Coverage: about 350–400 sq ft per gallon (9–10 m² per litre) per coat on smooth walls.
  • Two coats for most jobs; three for big color changes.
  • Subtract doors (~20 sq ft / 1.9 m²) and windows (~15 sq ft / 1.4 m²).
  • Rough or porous surfaces (new drywall, brick, stucco) use 20–50% more.
  • Buy all the paint at once so the color matches.

How do you calculate how much paint you need for a room?

  1. Measure the perimeter: add the lengths of all walls.
  2. Multiply by wall height to get the total wall area.
  3. Subtract about 20 sq ft for each door and 15 sq ft for each window.
  4. Multiply by the number of coats.
  5. Divide by the coverage on the can (use 350 sq ft per gallon if unsure) and round up.

Example: a 12 × 12 ft room with 8 ft walls: perimeter 48 ft × 8 ft = 384 sq ft. Minus one door and one window (35 sq ft) = 349 sq ft. Two coats = 698 sq ft ÷ 350 = 2 gallons.

How much paint for the outside of a house?

Measure each wall's width and height (for gable ends, add half the base × height of the triangle), subtract doors and windows, multiply by two coats, and divide by the coverage on the can. Rough siding, brick and stucco absorb more, so allow 20–50% extra. The exterior paint calculator handles gables, openings and coats.

Do you need primer?

  • Yes for new drywall, bare wood, patched areas, stains, glossy surfaces and big color changes (dark to light).
  • Usually no when repainting a similar color on clean, sound paint, especially with a paint-and-primer product.
  • Primer covers about 200–300 sq ft per gallon on porous surfaces.

What affects how far paint goes?

  • Surface: textured and porous surfaces soak up more paint.
  • Color change: covering dark colors may need a third coat or a tinted primer.
  • Application: rollers and brushes are more efficient than spraying, which loses some paint to overspray.

Frequently asked questions

How many square feet does a gallon of paint cover?

About 350–400 sq ft per coat on smooth, primed walls; less on rough or porous surfaces.

How much paint do I need for a 12×12 room?

About 2 gallons for two coats on 8 ft walls, plus about half a gallon for the ceiling.

Is one coat of paint enough?

Rarely. Two coats give even color and better durability, especially with color changes.

How much paint is that in litres?

One US gallon is about 3.8 litres. Most UK and European paints cover about 10–12 m² per litre per coat; check the tin.

The bottom line

Paint needed = (wall area − doors − windows) × coats ÷ coverage. Plan on about 350 sq ft per gallon, two coats, and extra for rough surfaces. Buy it all at once and keep a little for touch-ups.
