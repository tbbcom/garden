Quick answer

Divide the fence length by the post spacing (usually 8 ft / 2.4 m for wood, 6 ft / 1.8 m for many vinyl panels) and add 1, then add an extra post for each corner and two for each gate. A straight 100 ft wood fence with posts every 8 ft needs 100 ÷ 8 = 12.5, so 13 sections and 14 posts. Set posts about one-third of their total length into the ground, and at least 24 inches (600 mm) deep.

Posts carry the whole fence, so getting the count, spacing and depth right is what keeps it straight for years. Measure each straight run separately, because every corner and gate needs its own post. Key takeaways
  • Posts per run: (run length ÷ spacing), rounded up, + 1.
  • Spacing: 6–8 ft (1.8–2.4 m); closer in windy areas or for solid privacy fences.
  • Gates: two posts each, often heavier 6×6 in (150 mm) posts.
  • Depth: about ⅓ of the post's length, at least 24 in (600 mm), and below the frost line in cold climates.
  • Call 811 before you dig (US) so buried utilities are marked; in the UK, check for cables and pipes.

How do you calculate fence posts?

  1. Measure each straight run of fence separately.
  2. Divide each run by the post spacing and round up to get sections.
  3. Posts per run = sections + 1. Where two runs meet at a corner, they share that post.
  4. Add gate posts: two per gate, sized for the gate's weight.

Example: an L-shaped fence of 60 ft and 40 ft, posts every 8 ft: 60 ÷ 8 = 7.5 → 8 sections (9 posts); 40 ÷ 8 = 5 sections (6 posts); they share the corner post, so 9 + 6 − 1 = 14 posts. Add a gate: +2 posts, 16 total.

How deep should fence posts be?

  • Rule of thumb: bury about one-third of the post's total length. For a 6 ft fence, use 8 ft posts with about 2–3 ft (600–900 mm) in the ground.
  • Cold climates: go below your local frost line to prevent frost heave.
  • Hole width: about three times the post width (e.g. 10–12 in / 250–300 mm for a 4×4 in post).
  • Add 4–6 in (100–150 mm) of gravel at the bottom of each hole for drainage.

How much concrete per fence post?

It depends on hole size and depth. A typical 10 in wide, 30 in deep hole for a 4×4 post takes about 1–2 bags of 50–60 lb post-setting concrete. Use the fence post concrete calculator for your exact hole size. Crown the concrete at the top so water runs away from the post.

How far apart should fence posts be?

  • Wood privacy fences: up to 8 ft (2.4 m) apart.
  • Vinyl panels: match the panel width, often 6 or 8 ft.
  • Chain-link: up to 10 ft (3 m) for line posts.
  • Windy sites or tall solid fences: 6 ft (1.8 m) or closer.
  • UK panel fences: usually 1.83 m (6 ft) panels between posts.

Frequently asked questions

How many posts do I need for a 100 ft fence?

14 posts at 8 ft spacing or 18 at 6 ft spacing for a straight run, plus extra for gates and corners.

Do I need concrete for fence posts?

Concrete is most common and strongest. Packed gravel can work for some fences and drains better. Follow the fence manufacturer's advice and local practice.

How deep should a 6 ft fence post be?

About 2–3 ft (600–900 mm), or below the frost line in cold climates.

Do I need a permit for a fence?

Often, especially above a certain height or near a road or property line. Check local rules and HOA requirements first.

The bottom line

Posts = (run length ÷ spacing, rounded up) + 1 for each run, sharing corner posts, plus two per gate. Set them about a third of their length deep, below the frost line where needed, and call 811 before digging.
