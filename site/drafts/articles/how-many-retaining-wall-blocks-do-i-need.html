Quick answer

Blocks per course = wall length (in) ÷ block length (in). Courses = wall height (in) ÷ block height (in), plus the buried course(s). Multiply the two and add 5% for cuts. A 20 ft long, 2 ft high wall built from 12 × 4 in blocks needs 20 blocks per course and 6 courses above ground plus about 2 buried courses. That's 160 blocks, or about 168 with extra, plus 20 caps.

Retaining wall blocks are heavy and often sold by the piece, so a careful count saves money and trips. The retaining wall calculator does the math, including caps and gravel. This guide shows how it works and what else to order.

Key takeaways
  • Blocks per course: length (ft) × 12 ÷ block length (in).
  • Courses: height (in) ÷ block height (in), rounded up, plus buried course(s).
  • Buried depth: a common rule is 1 in per 8 in of wall height, minimum 6 in (150 mm). Follow your block maker's chart.
  • Base: about 6 in of compacted crushed stone in a trench about 24 in wide.
  • Height limit: walls over about 4 ft (1.2 m), or with loads above, usually need a permit and engineering.

How do you calculate retaining wall blocks?

  1. Blocks per course: wall length in inches ÷ block face length. 20 ft = 240 in ÷ 12 in = 20 blocks.
  2. Courses above ground: exposed height ÷ block height. 24 in ÷ 4 in = 6 courses.
  3. Buried courses: 24 in wall → 1 in per 8 in = 3 in, but the 6 in minimum applies, so 6 in ÷ 4 in blocks = 2 courses (round up).
  4. Total: 20 × (6 + 2) = 160 blocks, plus 5% = 168.
  5. Caps: wall length ÷ cap length. 240 ÷ 12 = 20 caps, plus a few spares.

For curved walls, measure along the front face. Blocks with angled sides make curves without cutting, but corners and steps add cuts, so allow 10% there.

How much gravel do you need for a retaining wall?

Base: trench length × about 2 ft wide × 0.5 ft deep ÷ 27 = cubic yards of compacted crushed stone. For 20 ft: 20 × 2 × 0.5 ÷ 27 = 0.74 cu yd. Drainage fill: about 12 in (300 mm) of clean, washed stone behind the blocks for the full height, with a perforated drain pipe at the base on taller walls. For 20 ft × 1 ft × 2.5 ft: 50 cu ft ÷ 27 = 1.85 cu yd. See how much gravel do I need to convert to tons.

When do you need a permit or engineer?

Many US jurisdictions require a permit for retaining walls over 4 ft (1.2 m), measured from the bottom of the footing, or for shorter walls that hold back a driveway, a slope or a building. UK rules vary by council and location, especially next to a boundary or highway. Small segmental block walls are a reasonable DIY job. A tall wall, or one with a load above it, holds back tons of saturated soil, so call your local building department before you build.

Frequently asked questions

How many retaining wall blocks do I need for a 3 ft high wall?

With 12 × 4 in blocks: 9 courses above ground plus 2 buried = 11 courses, so 11 blocks per linear foot of wall. With 16 × 6 in blocks: 6 courses plus 1 buried = 7 courses × 0.75 blocks per foot ≈ 5.25 blocks per linear foot. Add 5–10%.

How much of a retaining wall should be buried?

A common rule is 1 inch of buried depth for every 8 inches of wall height, with a minimum of 6 inches, but follow your block manufacturer’s guide.

Do I need gravel behind a retaining wall?

Yes. About 12 inches of clean drainage stone behind the wall lets water escape so pressure doesn’t build up and push the wall over.

How tall can a retaining wall be without a permit?

In many US areas, up to about 4 feet with no load above it, but rules vary, so check with your local building department.

The bottom line

Retaining wall blocks = blocks per course × (exposed courses + buried courses), plus 5–10%. Add caps for the top, about 6 inches of compacted base and 12 inches of drainage stone. Check local rules for anything near or over 4 feet.
