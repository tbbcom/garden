Quick answer

Multiply length × width × thickness in feet, then divide by 27 to get cubic yards, and add 5–10% for spillage and uneven ground. A 10 × 10 ft slab 4 inches thick needs about 1.23 cubic yards (about 0.94 m³), or roughly 1.35 yards with waste. In bags, that's about 56 bags of 80 lb concrete.

Ordering too little concrete means a cold joint or a second trip mid-pour; ordering too much wastes money. The math takes two minutes. Measure carefully, convert everything to the same unit, and always add a margin. Or skip the arithmetic with the concrete calculator, the slab calculator or the concrete bag calculator.

Key takeaways
  • Formula: length (ft) × width (ft) × thickness (ft) ÷ 27 = cubic yards.
  • Metric: length (m) × width (m) × thickness (m) = cubic metres.
  • Thickness: 4 in (100 mm) for patios and paths; 5–6 in (125–150 mm) for driveways.
  • Waste: add 5–10%, more on uneven ground.
  • Bags or truck: bags for under about 1 cubic yard; ready-mix above that.

How do you calculate how much concrete you need?

  1. Measure the length and width in feet.
  2. Convert thickness to feet by dividing inches by 12 (4 in = 0.333 ft; 6 in = 0.5 ft).
  3. Multiply length × width × thickness to get cubic feet.
  4. Divide by 27 to get cubic yards.
  5. Add 5–10% for waste.

Example: a 12 × 16 ft patio, 4 in thick: 12 × 16 × 0.333 = 64 cu ft ÷ 27 = 2.37 cu yd, so order about 2.5–2.6 cubic yards.

How thick should a concrete slab be?

  • Walkways and patios: 4 in (100 mm).
  • Driveways for cars: 5–6 in (125–150 mm).
  • Shed bases: usually 4 in; thicker for heavy equipment.
  • Footings and fence posts: sized by depth and diameter; see the fence post concrete calculator.

Thickness requirements, gravel base and reinforcement depend on your soil, climate and local building code, so check before pouring a structural slab.

Should you buy bags or order ready-mix?

As a rule of thumb, use bags for small jobs under about 1 cubic yard, such as post holes, steps and small pads. For larger pours, ready-mix delivered by truck gives a stronger, more consistent slab and saves hours of mixing. Many suppliers have a minimum order or charge a short-load fee for small quantities, so ask when you call.

Frequently asked questions

How much concrete do I need for a 10×10 slab?

About 1.23 cubic yards at 4 inches thick (1.85 at 6 inches), plus 5–10% for waste.

How many 80 lb bags of concrete make a yard?

About 45 bags, since each 80 lb bag yields roughly 0.6 cubic feet and a cubic yard is 27 cubic feet.

How much area does a yard of concrete cover?

About 81 sq ft at 4 inches thick and 54 sq ft at 6 inches thick.

How do I convert cubic feet to cubic yards?

Divide by 27. The cubic yard calculator does it for any shape.

The bottom line

Concrete needed = length × width × thickness (in feet) ÷ 27, plus 5–10%. A 4-inch slab uses about one cubic yard per 81 sq ft. Use bags for small jobs and ready-mix for anything over about a yard, and check local requirements for structural work.
