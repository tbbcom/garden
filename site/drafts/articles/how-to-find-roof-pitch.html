Quick answer

Roof pitch is the rise in inches for every 12 inches of horizontal run, written as rise/12. To measure it, hold a level horizontally against a rafter, mark 12 in along the level, and measure straight down from that mark to the rafter. A drop of 6 in means a 6/12 pitch, which is 26.6° or a 50% slope.

You need the pitch to order shingles, to check which roofing materials are allowed and to work out rafter lengths. You can measure it from inside the attic without climbing on the roof. The roof pitch calculator converts between pitch, angle and percent.

Key takeaways
  • Pitch = rise (in) per 12 in of run, for example 4/12, 6/12, 8/12.
  • Angle = arctan(rise ÷ 12); a 12/12 pitch is 45°.
  • Percent slope = rise ÷ run × 100 (6/12 = 50%).
  • Multiplier: roof area = flat footprint area × pitch factor (6/12 = 1.118).
  • Asphalt shingles: allowed down to 2/12 (2/12–4/12 needs double underlayment under the US IRC).

How do you measure roof pitch?

From the attic (safest)

  1. Mark 12 in on a level (a 24 in level is easiest).
  2. Hold one end of the level against the underside of a rafter and keep it horizontal.
  3. Measure vertically from the 12 in mark up to the rafter. That measurement is the rise.

From the gable end or plans

Pitch = total rise ÷ run × 12, where the run is half the building width for a symmetrical gable. Example: a 24 ft wide house with a ridge 6 ft above the wall plate: 6 ÷ 12 × 12 = 6/12.

From a ladder

You can lay the level on the shingles at the eave and measure the same way, but only with the ladder secured and someone holding it. Smartphone inclinometer apps give the angle directly, and the table below converts it to pitch. Don't walk on a roof steeper than about 6/12, or any wet roof, without fall protection.

What do you use roof pitch for?

  • Roof area and shingles: multiply the footprint (including overhangs) by the multiplier, then divide by 100 to get squares. See how many bundles of shingles do I need.
  • Rafter length: run × multiplier, plus the overhang. The rafter span calculator checks sizes.
  • Material choice: low slopes under 2/12 need membrane roofing rather than shingles. Metal and tile each have their own minimums, so check the product's instructions.

Frequently asked questions

What is a 6/12 roof pitch in degrees?

26.6 degrees, which is a 50% slope: the roof rises 6 inches for every 12 inches of horizontal run.

What is the most common roof pitch?

Pitches between 4/12 and 9/12 are most common on US homes, with 6/12 a typical middle ground.

Can I find my roof pitch without going on the roof?

Yes. Measure from a rafter in the attic, calculate it from the gable dimensions, or use a phone inclinometer on the gable trim from a secured ladder.

What is the lowest pitch for asphalt shingles?

2/12 under the US International Residential Code, and slopes from 2/12 up to 4/12 require double underlayment. Below 2/12, use a low-slope membrane.

The bottom line

Roof pitch = rise per 12 inches of run, measured with a level and a tape from inside the attic. Use the chart to convert it to degrees, and the multiplier to turn a footprint into true roof area for ordering materials.
