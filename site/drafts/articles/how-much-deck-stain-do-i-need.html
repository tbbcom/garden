Quick answer

Add up the deck floor, railings and stairs in square feet, then divide by the coverage on the can (usually 150–300 sq ft per gallon per coat). A 12 × 16 ft deck (192 sq ft) with 40 ft of railing (about 120 sq ft more) totals about 312 sq ft. At 200 sq ft per gallon, that's about 1.6 gallons per coat, so buy 2 gallons for one coat or 4 for two.

Stain coverage varies more than paint coverage, because old, rough or porous wood soaks up much more. The deck stain calculator adds the deck, railings and stairs for you, and the fence stain calculator does the same for fences.

Key takeaways
  • Floor: length × width.
  • Railings: a rough guide is about 3 sq ft per linear foot of railing with balusters (both sides).
  • Stairs: treads + risers + stringers; roughly 10 sq ft per step for a 3–4 ft wide stair.
  • Coverage: 150–300 sq ft per gallon (3.7–7.4 m² per litre); rough or weathered wood is at the low end.
  • Coats: follow the can; many semi-transparent stains need one coat or two thin coats applied wet-on-wet.

How do you calculate deck stain?

  1. Deck floor: length × width (subtract hot tub cut-outs and the like).
  2. Railings: linear feet × about 3 sq ft (more for dense balusters or lattice).
  3. Stairs: number of steps × about 10 sq ft.
  4. Total ÷ the coverage per gallon on the can = gallons per coat.
  5. Multiply by the number of coats and round up to whole gallons.

Example: a 14 × 20 ft deck (280 sq ft) + 50 ft of railing (150 sq ft) + 4 steps (40 sq ft) = 470 sq ft. 470 ÷ 175 sq ft per gallon (older, rough wood) = 2.7 gallons per coat. Buy a 5-gallon pail for two coats, or 3 gallons for one.

When and how should you stain a deck?

  • New pressure-treated wood: let it dry until water soaks in rather than beading. That can take weeks to a few months depending on climate.
  • Prep: clean with a deck cleaner, use a brightener if needed, let it dry for 24–48 hours, then do the water-drop test.
  • Weather: stain within the temperature range on the can, often about 50–90 °F (10–32 °C), out of direct midday sun, with no rain forecast for 24–48 hours.
  • Application: work 2–3 boards at a time along their full length to avoid lap marks, and back-brush any puddles.

Frequently asked questions

How much stain do I need for a 12x12 deck?

The floor is 144 sq ft, so about 0.6–1 gallon per coat depending on how porous the wood is. Railings and stairs add more, so most 12×12 decks with railings take about 1–2 gallons per coat.

How many square feet does a gallon of deck stain cover?

Usually 150–300 sq ft per coat. New smooth wood is at the high end; old, rough or weathered wood is at the low end.

Should I put one or two coats of stain on a deck?

Follow the product label. Many semi-transparent stains need one coat, or two thin coats applied wet-on-wet, while solid stains often need two. Too much semi-transparent stain stays tacky and peels.

How long should new pressure-treated wood dry before staining?

Until water soaks into the surface instead of beading, which can be a few weeks to a few months depending on weather and how wet the lumber was.

The bottom line

Deck stain needed = (floor + railings + stairs) ÷ the coverage on the can, × coats. Plan on 150–300 sq ft per gallon, use the low end for old or rough wood, and only stain dry, clean wood in mild weather.
