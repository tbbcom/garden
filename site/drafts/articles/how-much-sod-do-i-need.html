Quick answer

Measure the lawn area in square feet, add 5–10% for cuts and edges, then divide by the pallet or piece size your supplier uses. A 1,000 sq ft lawn needs about 1,050–1,100 sq ft of sod. That's roughly 2–3 pallets at 450–500 sq ft each, or about 400 standard 16 × 24 in pieces. In the UK, turf rolls are usually about 1 m² each.

Sod gives you a finished lawn in a day, but you pay by the square foot, so measure carefully. Too little leaves gaps that dry out, and a second delivery may not match. The sod calculator converts your area into pieces and pallets.

Key takeaways
  • Area: length × width (split odd shapes into rectangles, triangles and circles).
  • Extra: 5% for simple rectangles, 10% for curves, beds and slopes.
  • Pieces: a 16 × 24 in piece covers 2.67 sq ft (2 × 5 ft rolls cover 10 sq ft).
  • Pallets: typically 400–500 sq ft; it varies by farm.
  • Soil: lay sod over 4–6 in of loosened topsoil, within 24 hours of delivery.

How do you calculate how much sod you need?

  1. Measure each section and add the areas (see how to measure your lawn's square footage for odd shapes).
  2. Subtract large areas you won't sod, such as patios, beds and the shed footprint.
  3. Add 5–10% for trimming along edges and curves.
  4. Divide by the coverage of one piece, roll or pallet, and round up.

Example: a 30 × 40 ft backyard (1,200 sq ft) minus a 10 × 12 ft patio (120 sq ft) = 1,080 sq ft. Adding 10% gives 1,188 sq ft, which is 3 pallets of 450 sq ft (1,350 sq ft), or 2 pallets plus about 110 loose pieces if your supplier sells partial pallets.

How do you prepare and lay sod?

  • Prepare: remove old turf and weeds, then loosen or add 4–6 in of topsoil (see how much topsoil do I need). Rake the area level, set it about 1 in below paths so the sod sits flush, and roll it firm.
  • Lay on day one: sod heats up and dies on the pallet, so lay it the day it arrives. Start along a straight edge, stagger the seams like brickwork and butt the edges tight without overlapping.
  • Water: soak it right after laying, until the soil underneath is wet. Then keep it moist daily for 1–2 weeks and taper off as roots knit (tug test: rooted sod won't lift).
  • Timing: cool-season sod does best in early fall or spring, warm-season sod in late spring to early summer. See cool-season vs warm-season grass.

Frequently asked questions

How much sod do I need for 1,000 square feet?

About 1,050–1,100 sq ft with 5–10% extra, which is 3 pallets of 450–500 sq ft or roughly 400 pieces of 16 × 24 in.

How many square feet are in a pallet of sod?

Usually about 400–500 sq ft, but it varies by farm and grass type, so confirm before ordering.

How many pieces of sod are in a pallet?

Typically 150–200 pieces of 16 × 24 in (2.67 sq ft each).

Is sod or seed cheaper?

Seed is far cheaper per square foot but takes weeks to months to fill in; sod costs more but gives an instant lawn. See how much grass seed do I need for seeding rates.

The bottom line

Sod needed = lawn area + 5–10%, divided by your supplier's pallet or piece coverage. Prepare the soil before delivery, lay everything the same day and keep it moist for the first two weeks.
