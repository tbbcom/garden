Sheets for Walls and Ceilings ---
Quick answer

Add up the area of every wall and ceiling you're covering, divide by the area of one sheet (32 sq ft for a 4 × 8 ft sheet, 48 sq ft for 4 × 12 ft), and add 10–15% for waste. Don't subtract small openings like doors and windows; the offcuts are rarely reusable. A 12 × 12 ft room with 8 ft walls needs about 12 sheets for the walls and 5 for the ceiling with 4 × 8 sheets, plus waste.

Drywall (plasterboard in the UK) is sold in standard sheets, so the job is simply total area ÷ sheet area. Bigger sheets mean fewer seams to tape, but they're heavier to handle. The drywall calculator works out sheets, screws and joint compound for you.

Key takeaways
  • Sheet sizes: 4 × 8 ft (32 sq ft) and 4 × 12 ft (48 sq ft); UK sheets are usually 1200 × 2400 mm (2.88 m²).
  • Waste: add 10–15%.
  • Openings: subtract only large ones such as wide doorways or picture windows.
  • Thickness: ½ in (12.5 mm) for walls is standard; ⅝ in fire-rated where codes require it.
  • Moisture-resistant board for bathrooms; cement board behind tile in showers.

How do you calculate how many sheets of drywall you need?

  1. Walls: measure the room's perimeter and multiply by wall height.
  2. Ceiling: multiply the room's length by its width.
  3. Add wall and ceiling areas.
  4. Divide by sheet area (32 or 48 sq ft) and round up.
  5. Add 10–15% for waste and damaged sheets.

Example: a 12 × 12 ft room with 8 ft walls: walls 48 × 8 = 384 sq ft; ceiling 144 sq ft; total 528 sq ft ÷ 32 = 16.5, so 17 sheets, about 19 with waste.

Which drywall size should you use?

  • 4 × 8 ft: easiest to carry and lift, fits most vehicles; good for DIYers and small rooms.
  • 4 × 12 ft: fewer seams on long walls and ceilings, so less taping, but heavy and usually a two-person job.
  • Hang sheets horizontally on walls in most homes; it reduces seam length and puts joints at a comfortable height for finishing.

What else do you need besides sheets?

  • Screws: drywall screws about every 12 in (300 mm) on ceilings and 16 in (400 mm) on walls along each framing member, or as your local code requires.
  • Joint tape and compound for every seam and inside corner, plus corner bead for outside corners.
  • Three coats of compound on seams and screw heads for a smooth finish, sanding between coats.

Frequently asked questions

How many sheets of drywall do I need for a 12×12 room?

About 17 sheets of 4 × 8 ft for walls and ceiling with 8 ft walls, or about 19 with 10% waste.

Should I subtract doors and windows from drywall estimates?

Usually not. Offcuts around openings are rarely reusable, so the extra covers waste. Subtract only very large openings.

What thickness drywall should I use?

½ inch is standard for walls; ⅝ inch is common for ceilings and fire-rated walls such as between a garage and the house. Check your local code.

Is drywall the same as plasterboard?

Yes. Plasterboard is the UK term; UK sheets are usually 1200 × 2400 mm, covering 2.88 m² each.

The bottom line

Sheets = (wall area + ceiling area) ÷ sheet area, plus 10–15%. Don't subtract small openings, use moisture-resistant board in bathrooms, and pick 4 × 12 sheets if you can handle them to reduce taping.
