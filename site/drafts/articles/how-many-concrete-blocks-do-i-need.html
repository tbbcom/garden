Quick answer

Multiply the wall area in square feet by 1.125 for standard 8 × 8 × 16 in blocks, then add about 5% for breakage and cuts. A 20 ft long × 4 ft high wall is 80 sq ft, so it needs 90 blocks, or about 95 with extra. With metric 440 × 215 mm blocks (the UK standard), allow 10 blocks per m².

Concrete masonry units (CMUs, cinder blocks or breeze blocks) are sized so that one block plus its mortar joint covers an exact area. That makes counting easy. The concrete block calculator does the arithmetic and adds mortar.

Key takeaways
  • 8 × 8 × 16 in block: 16 × 8 in face with joint = 0.889 sq ft, so 1.125 blocks per sq ft.
  • Formula: wall length (ft) × height (ft) × 1.125, minus openings, plus 5%.
  • Courses: height (in) ÷ 8; blocks per course: length (in) ÷ 16.
  • Metric: 440 × 215 mm blocks with 10 mm joints come to about 10 per m².
  • Mortar: roughly one 80 lb bag of mortar mix per 30–40 blocks (check the bag).

How do you calculate concrete blocks?

  1. Measure the wall length and height in feet.
  2. Multiply to get the wall area, and subtract any doors or windows.
  3. Multiply by 1.125 for 8 × 16 in face blocks (or divide the area by the face area of your block plus joint).
  4. Add 5%, and order corner or half blocks for ends and openings.

Course method check: 20 ft = 240 in ÷ 16 = 15 blocks per course; 4 ft = 48 in ÷ 8 = 6 courses; 15 × 6 = 90 blocks. Both methods agree.

The thickness (4, 6, 8, 10 or 12 in) doesn't change the count, only the weight and strength. "Nominal" sizes include a ⅜ in joint, so an 8 × 8 × 16 block actually measures 7⅝ × 7⅝ × 15⅝ in.

How much mortar and concrete fill do you need?

Mortar: a common rule is one 80 lb bag of type S or N mortar mix for about 30–40 standard blocks. Coverage depends on joint thickness and how much is wasted, so read the yield on your bag and buy a spare. Core fill: walls that are reinforced or retain soil have some or all cores filled with grout or concrete. A fully filled 8 in wall uses roughly 0.25–0.3 cu ft of grout per sq ft of wall; see how much concrete do I need to convert that to bags.

Do you need a footing for a block wall?

Yes. Any block wall needs a continuous concrete footing, typically about twice as wide as the block and at least 8 in thick, set below the frost line in cold climates. Reinforcement, footing size and height limits depend on what the wall does and on your local code. Foundation and retaining walls need engineering or a permit in most places. For low terraced garden walls, segmental blocks are often easier; see how many retaining wall blocks do I need.

Frequently asked questions

How many concrete blocks do I need for a 100 sq ft wall?

About 113 standard 8 × 8 × 16 in blocks, or about 118 with 5% extra.

How many cinder blocks are in a square foot?

1.125 standard 8 × 16 in face blocks, because each covers 0.889 sq ft including the mortar joint.

How many blocks per square metre?

About 10 for the UK-standard 440 × 215 mm block with 10 mm joints, or about 12.1 for 8 × 16 in US blocks.

How many bags of mortar for 100 blocks?

Roughly 3 bags of 80 lb mortar mix, assuming about 30–40 blocks per bag. Check your bag’s stated yield.

The bottom line

Concrete blocks = wall area (sq ft) × 1.125 for standard 8 × 16 in face blocks, or about 10 per m² for UK blocks, plus 5% extra. Add mortar at roughly one bag per 30–40 blocks and plan a proper footing before the first course goes down.
