Area, Waste and Boxes to Buy ---
Quick answer

Measure the area in square feet (or m²), add 10% for waste on a straight layout or 15–20% for diagonal and herringbone patterns, then divide by the coverage printed on one box. A 10 × 12 ft floor (120 sq ft) with 10% waste needs 132 sq ft of tile, which is 10 boxes if each box covers 13.5 sq ft.

Tile is sold by the box, and dye lots vary, so buying the right amount the first time matters: a box bought later may not match. Measure every surface you're tiling, split odd shapes into rectangles, and always add a waste allowance. The tile calculator and shower tile calculator do the math, including boxes.

Key takeaways
  • Area: length × width for each rectangle; add them up.
  • Waste: 10% straight lay; 15% diagonal; 15–20% herringbone or large-format tile.
  • Boxes: total area ÷ coverage per box, rounded up.
  • Same dye lot: buy all tile at once and keep a few spares for repairs.

How do you calculate how much tile you need?

  1. Measure the length and width of the floor or wall.
  2. Multiply to get the area. Split L-shaped rooms into rectangles and add them.
  3. Subtract large areas you won't tile, such as a fixed cabinet or tub.
  4. Add waste for your layout.
  5. Divide by the coverage on one box and round up.

Example: a 10 × 12 ft kitchen floor: 120 sq ft + 10% = 132 sq ft ÷ 13.5 sq ft per box = 9.8, so buy 10 boxes.

Large-format tile (24 in / 600 mm and up) or many cuts15–20%

Keep at least a few spare tiles after the job for future repairs.

How do you calculate tile for a shower or backsplash?

  • Shower walls: width × height of each wall you're tiling, plus the floor if tiling it. Add niches and curbs separately.
  • Backsplash: length of the counter run × height (often 18 in / 450 mm between counter and upper cabinets), minus windows.

What else do you need for a tile job?

  • Thinset or tile adhesive, matched to the tile type and size.
  • Grout: the amount depends on tile size, joint width and depth; check the coverage chart on the bag.
  • Spacers, trowel (notch size per the adhesive label), backer board and waterproofing in wet areas.

Frequently asked questions

How much tile do I need for a 10×10 room?

100 sq ft plus 10% waste, so 110 sq ft for a straight layout, or 115 sq ft for diagonal.

Why buy extra tile?

For cuts, breakage and mistakes, and so you have matching spares later; a later batch may not match in color.

How do I tile around an irregular room?

Split it into rectangles and triangles, calculate each area and add them. The square footage calculator helps with odd shapes.

Do I subtract the toilet or vanity area?

Subtract fixed cabinets or tubs you won't tile under, but not a toilet: tile runs under it.

The bottom line

Tile needed = area + 10–20% waste depending on the pattern, divided by the box coverage. Buy it all at once from the same batch, and keep spares for future repairs.
