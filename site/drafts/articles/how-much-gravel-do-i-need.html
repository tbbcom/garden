Quick answer

Multiply length × width × depth in feet and divide by 27 to get cubic yards; multiply cubic yards by about 1.4 to get tons. A 10 × 10 ft area 3 inches deep needs about 0.93 cubic yards (0.71 m³), or roughly 1.3 tons. Add 5–10% for compaction and uneven ground.

Gravel is sold by the cubic yard, by the ton or in bags, so you need to convert between all three. The math is quick once everything is in the same unit. To skip it, use the gravel calculator, the driveway gravel calculator or the cubic yards to tons calculator.

Key takeaways
  • Formula: length (ft) × width (ft) × depth (ft) ÷ 27 = cubic yards.
  • Tons: cubic yards × 1.4 (most gravel weighs about 2,600–3,000 lb per cubic yard).
  • Metric: length × width × depth in metres = m³; × about 1.6 = tonnes.
  • Depth: 2–3 in for paths, 4 in for patio bases, 4–6 in on top of a firm base for driveways.
  • Extra: add 5–10%, because gravel compacts.

How do you calculate how much gravel you need?

  1. Measure the length and width in feet. For curved or odd shapes, split the area into rectangles and circles (see how to calculate square footage).
  2. Convert depth to feet: inches ÷ 12 (2 in = 0.167 ft, 3 in = 0.25 ft, 4 in = 0.333 ft).
  3. Multiply length × width × depth to get cubic feet.
  4. Divide by 27 to get cubic yards.
  5. Multiply by 1.4 if your supplier sells by the ton, then add 5–10%.

Example: a 3 × 30 ft path, 3 in deep: 3 × 30 × 0.25 = 22.5 cu ft ÷ 27 = 0.83 cu yd × 1.4 = about 1.2 tons. Order about 1.25–1.3 tons.

How deep should gravel be?

  • Walkways and garden paths: 2–3 in (50–75 mm) over landscape fabric or compacted soil.
  • Patio or paver base: about 4 in (100 mm) of compacted crushed stone; see how many pavers do I need.
  • Driveways: a new driveway is usually built in layers, with larger crushed stone at the bottom and 4–6 in of finer stone on top, totalling about 8–12 in (200–300 mm) on soft soil.
  • Drainage around foundations or French drains: as deep as the trench; use clean, washed stone.

Which type of gravel should you buy?

Crushed stone (angular, often #57 or 3/4 in) locks together and suits driveways and bases. Crusher run (also called dense grade or road base) mixes stone and fines and compacts very hard. Pea gravel is rounded and comfortable underfoot, but it shifts, so give it solid edging; the pea gravel calculator handles it. Bagged gravel is usually 0.5 cu ft per bag, so you need 54 bags per cubic yard. Bulk delivery is cheaper above about half a yard.

Frequently asked questions

How much gravel do I need for a 10×10 area?

About 0.62 cubic yards (0.87 tons) at 2 inches deep, 0.93 cubic yards (1.3 tons) at 3 inches, and 1.23 cubic yards (1.7 tons) at 4 inches, plus 5–10%.

How many tons are in a yard of gravel?

About 1.4 tons for most crushed stone and pea gravel, though it ranges from roughly 1.3 to 1.5 tons depending on the stone and moisture.

How much does a ton of gravel cover?

About 115 sq ft at 2 inches, 77 sq ft at 3 inches and 58 sq ft at 4 inches deep.

How many bags of gravel make a yard?

About 54 bags of 0.5 cubic feet each, because a cubic yard is 27 cubic feet.

The bottom line

Gravel needed = length × width × depth (in feet) ÷ 27 for cubic yards, × 1.4 for tons, plus 5–10% for compaction. Match the depth to the job: about 3 inches for paths, 4 for patio bases and more for driveways.
