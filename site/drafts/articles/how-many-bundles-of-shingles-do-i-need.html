Squares and Pitch ---
Quick answer

Most asphalt shingles come 3 bundles to a "square", and one square covers 100 sq ft (9.3 m²) of roof. Work out your roof area (footprint × pitch factor), divide by 100 to get squares, multiply by 3 for bundles, and add 10–15% for waste. A 2,000 sq ft roof is 20 squares, or 60 bundles, about 66–69 bundles with waste.

Roofing is measured in squares, not square feet, and the slope of the roof adds area you can't see from the ground. Get the roof area right and the rest is simple multiplication. The roofing calculator, shingle calculator and roof pitch calculator do it from your measurements.

Key takeaways
  • 1 square = 100 sq ft of roof surface.
  • 3 bundles per square for most 3-tab and architectural shingles; some heavy designer shingles need 4–5. Check the wrapper.
  • Pitch matters: a 6/12 roof has about 12% more area than its footprint.
  • Waste: 10% for simple gable roofs; 15% or more for hips, valleys and dormers.
  • Extras: starter strip, ridge cap, underlayment, drip edge and nails.

How do you calculate how many shingles you need?

  1. Measure the footprint of the roof including overhangs (length × width), or each roof plane separately.
  2. Find the pitch: inches of rise per 12 inches of run (e.g. 6/12).
  3. Multiply the footprint by the pitch factor from the table below to get the true roof area.
  4. Divide by 100 to get squares.
  5. Multiply by 3 for bundles, then add 10–15% waste.

Example: a 40 × 30 ft house with 1 ft overhangs (42 × 32 = 1,344 sq ft footprint) and a 6/12 roof: 1,344 × 1.118 = 1,503 sq ft = 15 squares × 3 = 45 bundles, plus 10% = 50 bundles.

What else do you need besides shingles?

  • Starter strip along the eaves and rakes (measured in linear feet).
  • Ridge and hip cap shingles for every ridge and hip (linear feet).
  • Underlayment to cover the whole roof area, plus ice and water shield in valleys and along eaves in cold climates.
  • Drip edge around the perimeter.
  • Roofing nails: usually 4 per shingle, 6 in high-wind areas, per the manufacturer.

How much waste should you allow?

  • Simple gable roof: about 10%.
  • Hip roof or several valleys: about 15%.
  • Complex roofs with dormers, turrets and many angles: 15–20%.
Safety first: roof work is one of the most dangerous DIY jobs. Measure from the ground or attic where you can, use fall protection on the roof, and hire a licensed roofer for steep or high roofs.

Frequently asked questions

How many bundles of shingles are in a square?

Usually 3 bundles per square (100 sq ft) for 3-tab and most architectural shingles; some heavy designer shingles need 4 or 5.

How many bundles do I need for a 1,000 sq ft roof?

10 squares × 3 = 30 bundles, plus 10–15% waste, so about 33–35 bundles. Make sure the 1,000 sq ft is roof area, not house floor area.

How do I measure my roof without climbing on it?

Measure the house footprint including overhangs from the ground, then multiply by the pitch factor. Satellite images can help with complex roofs.

How much does one bundle of shingles cover?

About 33.3 sq ft (3.1 m²) when it takes 3 bundles to cover a square.

The bottom line

Bundles = roof area (footprint × pitch factor) ÷ 100 × 3, plus 10–15% waste. Add starter, ridge cap, underlayment and drip edge, and leave steep or high roofs to a professional.
