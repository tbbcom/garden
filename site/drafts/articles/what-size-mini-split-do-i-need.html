BTU by Room Size ---
Quick answer

As a starting point, allow about 20 BTU per square foot of room (roughly 60 W per m²): a 400 sq ft room needs about 8,000–9,000 BTU, so a 9,000 BTU mini-split (12,000 if it is sunny). Add about 10% for sunny rooms, 600 BTU for each regular occupant beyond two, and 4,000 BTU for a kitchen; subtract 10% for heavily shaded rooms. For a whole house or a poorly insulated space, get a professional load calculation (Manual J in the US).

An undersized mini-split runs constantly and can't keep up on the hottest days; an oversized one cycles on and off, wastes energy and leaves rooms clammy because it doesn't run long enough to remove humidity. The square-footage rule gets you close for single rooms. The mini-split size calculator and the BTU calculator adjust for sun, ceiling height, occupants and climate.

Key takeaways
  • Rule of thumb: about 20 BTU per sq ft for cooling a typical room.
  • Common sizes: 9,000, 12,000, 18,000, 24,000 and 36,000 BTU (about 2.6, 3.5, 5.3, 7.0 and 10.6 kW).
  • Adjust for sun, shade, occupants, kitchens and high ceilings.
  • Heating in cold climates may need a larger or cold-climate model.
  • Whole house: use a professional load calculation, not a rule of thumb.

How do you adjust for your room?

  • Very sunny room: add 10%.
  • Heavily shaded room: subtract 10%.
  • More than two people regularly using the room: add 600 BTU per extra person.
  • Kitchen: add 4,000 BTU.
  • High ceilings (over 8 ft / 2.4 m): increase in proportion to the extra volume.
  • Poor insulation, hot climate or lots of glass: size up, or get a load calculation.

Example: a sunny 400 sq ft living room: 9,000–10,000 BTU + 10% ≈ 10,500–11,000 BTU, so a 12,000 BTU unit.

Should you size a mini-split for heating or cooling?

In hot and mild climates, size for cooling. In cold climates, check the heating capacity at your design low temperature, because heat pump output drops as it gets colder. Cold-climate models hold more of their rated capacity in freezing weather. Compare the manufacturer's capacity at low temperatures, not just the headline rating.

Single-zone or multi-zone?

  • Single-zone: one outdoor unit for one indoor head; simplest and most efficient for one room, garage or addition.
  • Multi-zone: one outdoor unit serving 2–5 indoor heads, each sized to its own room. Size each head for its room, then the outdoor unit for the combined load.

Frequently asked questions

What size mini-split do I need for 500 square feet?

About 12,000 BTU for an average room; more for a sunny or poorly insulated space.

Is it bad to oversize a mini-split?

Yes. It short-cycles, wastes energy and removes less humidity. Inverter mini-splits handle mild oversizing better than older units, but aim for the right size.

How many BTU per square foot for a mini-split?

About 20 BTU per square foot is a common starting point for cooling; adjust for sun, occupants and insulation.

How do I convert BTU to kW?

Divide BTU per hour by about 3,412. A 12,000 BTU unit is about 3.5 kW.

The bottom line

Start at about 20 BTU per square foot, adjust for sun, occupants, kitchens and ceilings, and choose the nearest standard size. For a whole house, cold climates or unusual rooms, get a professional load calculation before buying.
