Quick answer

To grow a banana plant indoors, choose a dwarf variety such as 'Dwarf Cavendish' or 'Super Dwarf Cavendish', plant it in a large pot of rich, well-drained mix, and give it your brightest light (6+ hours of sun or a grow light), warmth of 65–85°F (18–29°C), high humidity and regular water and feeding. Indoor bananas grow fast as leafy houseplants, but fruiting indoors is uncommon: it usually takes 2–3 years and near-greenhouse conditions.

A banana plant brings big tropical leaves to a sunny room or conservatory. Bananas aren't trees: they are giant herbs that grow from an underground corm, and each stem flowers once, fruits, and then dies back as new "pups" grow around it. Indoors, success comes down to light, warmth and pot size.

Key takeaways
  • Pick a dwarf variety that stays about 4–8 feet (1.2–2.4 m) tall in a pot.
  • Light is the limit: a south-facing window, conservatory or grow light.
  • Warm and humid: keep above 60°F (16°C) and mist or use a humidifier.
  • Big pot, rich mix: start at 12–15 inches (30–38 cm) and repot as it grows.
  • Hungry plant: water when the top inch is dry and feed every 2–4 weeks in summer.

How do you plant a banana in a pot?

  1. Choose a pot at least 12–15 inches (30–38 cm) wide with drainage holes; heavy pots resist tipping.
  2. Use a rich, well-drained potting mix with added compost and some perlite.
  3. Plant at the same depth as before and water thoroughly.
  4. Move to a pot about 2–4 inches (5–10 cm) wider each spring as the plant grows.

How do you care for an indoor banana plant?

  • Light: as much as possible: a bright south- or west-facing window, a conservatory, or a strong grow light for 12+ hours a day. Move it outdoors in summer if nights stay above about 55°F (13°C), hardening it off gradually.
  • Water: keep the soil evenly moist in summer; water when the top inch (2.5 cm) is dry and let excess drain away. Water less in winter.
  • Humidity: brown, crispy leaf edges usually mean dry air. Use a humidifier or a pebble tray, and keep it away from heaters.
  • Feeding: bananas are heavy feeders. Use a balanced or potassium-rich liquid fertilizer every 2–4 weeks from spring to late summer, following the label.
  • Leaves: torn leaves are normal for bananas. Remove leaves that are fully yellow or brown at the stem.
  • Pups: pot up offshoots with some roots once they are about 12 inches (30 cm) tall, or remove them to keep the main plant vigorous.

How fast do banana plants grow indoors?

In good light and warmth a banana can produce a new leaf every week or two in summer. Growth slows a lot in winter or in low light. That's normal; just water less and stop feeding until spring.

Will an indoor banana plant produce fruit?

Sometimes, but don't count on it. A stem needs roughly 2–3 years of strong, warm growth before it flowers, and it needs high light to set good fruit. Conservatories and greenhouses give the best chance. After fruiting, cut the old stem down and let a pup take over.

Frequently asked questions

Why are my banana plant's leaves turning brown?

Usually dry air, underwatering, cold drafts or too much direct hot sun through glass. Raise humidity and keep watering and temperature steady.

Can a banana plant survive winter outdoors in the UK or northern US?

Edible types can't. The ornamental Musa basjoo survives many UK winters with a thick mulch and wrapped stems. Bring edible types indoors before frost.

How often should I water an indoor banana plant?

Whenever the top inch of soil is dry, often every few days in summer and much less in winter. Never leave the pot standing in water.

Is a banana plant safe for pets?

Banana plants are generally listed as non-toxic to cats and dogs, but check the ASPCA plant list and ask your vet if your pet chews leaves.

The bottom line

Choose a dwarf banana, give it a big pot of rich soil, your brightest light, warmth, humidity and regular feeding, and enjoy it as a fast-growing tropical houseplant. Fruit is a bonus that needs years of excellent conditions.
