Quick answer

To build a raised garden bed, pick a sunny, level spot, build a frame about 4 feet (1.2 m) wide, 6–8 feet (1.8–2.4 m) long and 10–12 inches (25–30 cm) tall from untreated or naturally rot-resistant wood, line the bottom with cardboard to smother grass, and fill it with a mix of about 60% topsoil and 40% compost. A 4×8 ft bed 12 inches deep needs about 32 cubic feet (just over 1 cubic yard, or about 0.9 m³) of soil.

Raised beds warm up earlier in spring, drain better than heavy ground and are easier on your back. They also let you fill with good soil from day one, which is the biggest reason beginners succeed with them. Here is how to build one in an afternoon.

Key takeaways
  • Width: 3–4 ft (0.9–1.2 m) so you can reach the middle without stepping in.
  • Height: 10–12 in (25–30 cm) suits most vegetables; 24–30 in (60–75 cm) for easy access.
  • Materials: cedar, larch, untreated pine, or galvanized metal.
  • Bottom: cardboard over grass; hardware cloth if gophers or moles are a problem.
  • Fill: roughly 60% screened topsoil and 40% compost.

What is the best material for a raised bed?

  • Cedar or larch: naturally rot-resistant and attractive; lasts about 10–15 years.
  • Untreated pine or fir: cheapest; lasts about 3–7 years.
  • Galvanized steel: long-lasting and quick to assemble; warms up fast in spring.
  • Pressure-treated wood: modern treatments are considered acceptable by many extension services, but you can line the inside with heavy plastic if you prefer to keep soil away from the wood.
  • Avoid: old railway sleepers treated with creosote, and painted wood of unknown age that may contain lead.

How do you build a raised garden bed step by step?

  1. Choose the spot: at least 6–8 hours of sun, level ground and a water source nearby. Run long beds north to south for even light.
  2. Cut the boards: for a 4×8 ft bed, two 8 ft and two 4 ft boards, 2 in (5 cm) thick and 10–12 in (25–30 cm) wide.
  3. Assemble the frame: screw the corners together with 3 in (75 mm) exterior screws, using 4×4 in (10×10 cm) corner posts for strength.
  4. Level it: set the frame in place, dig out high spots and check with a level.
  5. Line the bottom: lay plain cardboard over grass and weeds, overlapping the edges. Add galvanized hardware cloth underneath if burrowing animals are a problem.
  6. Fill and settle: fill with your soil mix, water well and top up after a week as it settles.

What should you fill a raised bed with?

A simple, reliable mix is about 60% screened topsoil and 40% compost. Don't fill with bagged potting mix alone (it's expensive and dries out quickly) or with soil straight from a lawn full of weeds. For tall beds, you can fill the bottom third with logs, branches and leaves (a "hugelkultur" base) to save soil, then top with at least 12 in (30 cm) of good soil. Top up with 1–2 in (2–5 cm) of compost every spring. See how to compost kitchen scraps to make your own.

What grows best in a raised bed?

Almost any vegetable: lettuce, spinach, radishes, carrots, beets, onions, herbs, bush beans, peppers and tomatoes all thrive. Grow tall or sprawling crops on a trellis at the north end so they don't shade the rest. Use the plant spacing calculator to plan how many plants fit.

Frequently asked questions

How deep should a raised garden bed be?

10–12 inches (25–30 cm) is enough for most vegetables. Deep-rooted crops like tomatoes, squash and carrots do best with 18–24 inches (45–60 cm), or a bed over loosened native soil.

What do you put on the bottom of a raised bed?

Plain cardboard over grass and weeds, plus hardware cloth if burrowing animals are a problem. Avoid plastic sheeting, which blocks drainage.

Do raised beds need a bottom?

No. An open bottom lets roots reach the ground and water drain. Beds on patios or decks need a bottom with drainage holes.

How much soil do I need for a 4×8 raised bed?

32 cubic feet (about 1.2 cubic yards, or 0.9 m³) for a bed 12 inches deep.

The bottom line

Build your raised bed in full sun, about 4 feet wide and 10–12 inches deep, from rot-resistant or untreated wood, line the bottom with cardboard and fill with topsoil and compost. Work out the exact soil you need with the raised bed soil calculator.
