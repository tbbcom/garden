Quick answer

The fastest way to grow dragon fruit is from a cutting: let a 12-inch (30 cm) stem segment dry for a week, plant it 2 inches (5 cm) deep in fast-draining cactus mix, and give it a sturdy post to climb, full sun to light shade, and temperatures above 50°F (10°C). Plants from cuttings can fruit in 1–3 years; plants from seed take 4–7 years. Dragon fruit is a climbing cactus, so it needs warmth, excellent drainage and support more than frequent watering.

Dragon fruit (pitaya, Selenicereus species, formerly Hylocereus) is a vining cactus from Central America. It grows outdoors in frost-free climates such as southern Florida, Southern California, Hawaii and the tropics, including Malaysia. Elsewhere, including most of the UK and northern US, grow it in a large container you can bring indoors for winter.

Key takeaways
  • Cuttings beat seeds: fruit in 1–3 years instead of 4–7.
  • Support: a strong post about 5–6 feet (1.5–1.8 m) tall with a ring or frame at the top.
  • Drainage: cactus mix or sandy loam; never let roots sit wet.
  • Warmth: protect from frost; bring containers in below 50°F (10°C).
  • Pollination: some varieties need hand-pollinating at night.

How do you grow dragon fruit from a cutting?

  1. Get a healthy stem segment about 12 inches (30 cm) long from a nursery or a friend's plant, and mark which end is up.
  2. Let it callus in a dry, shaded place for 5–7 days until the cut end is dry.
  3. Plant 2 inches (5 cm) deep in cactus or succulent mix, cut end down, next to its support post.
  4. Water lightly once, then only when the top inch of soil is dry. Roots form in a few weeks.

How do you grow dragon fruit from seed?

  1. Scoop seeds from a ripe fruit, rinse off the pulp and dry them for a day.
  2. Sprinkle on moist seed-starting mix and press in lightly; don't bury deeply.
  3. Cover with clear plastic and keep warm (70–80°F / 21–27°C) in bright, indirect light.
  4. Seeds usually sprout in 1–3 weeks. Move seedlings to individual pots when they are a few inches tall.

Where should you plant dragon fruit?

  • Light: full sun in mild climates; light afternoon shade in very hot areas, where stems can scorch.
  • Soil: fast-draining sandy soil or cactus mix with some compost. Raised mounds help in heavy soil.
  • Containers: at least 15–20 gallons (60–75 litres), with drainage holes and a sturdy trellis.
  • Support: a post with a ring or crossbar at the top so stems can cascade down; fruit forms on hanging stems.

How do you care for a dragon fruit plant?

  • Water: deeply when the top 1–2 inches (3–5 cm) of soil are dry; less in winter. Overwatering causes root and stem rot.
  • Feed: a balanced or low-nitrogen fertilizer every 1–2 months during the growing season, following the label.
  • Prune: remove damaged stems and thin crowded growth after harvest to improve airflow and fruiting.
  • Cold: cover outdoor plants during cold snaps and bring containers indoors below about 50°F (10°C).

Why isn't my dragon fruit flowering or fruiting?

  • Too young: plants from cuttings usually need 1–3 years; from seed, longer.
  • Not enough light or support: fruit forms on mature, hanging stems in good light.
  • Pollination: flowers open for a single night. Some varieties are self-sterile and need pollen from another variety. Hand-pollinate with a soft brush between the flower's anthers and stigma at night or early morning.

When is dragon fruit ready to harvest?

Fruit is usually ready about 30–50 days after flowering. Ripe fruit has bright, even skin color, the "wings" or bracts start to wither, and the fruit gives slightly when pressed. Twist it gently off or cut it with pruners.

Frequently asked questions

How long does dragon fruit take to grow?

About 1–3 years to fruit from a cutting and about 4–7 years from seed.

Can dragon fruit grow indoors?

Yes, in a large pot in your sunniest window or under grow lights, though it fruits more reliably outdoors in summer.

Does dragon fruit need a lot of water?

No. It's a cactus: water deeply only when the top of the soil is dry, and make sure the pot drains well.

Do you need two dragon fruit plants?

Not always. Many popular varieties are self-fertile, but some need cross-pollination from another variety. Check when you buy.

The bottom line

Start dragon fruit from a dried cutting in fast-draining soil, give it a sturdy post, warmth and sun, water only when the soil is dry, and hand-pollinate if fruit doesn't set. Expect fruit in 1–3 years.
