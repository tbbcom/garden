Quick answer

Sow pumpkin seeds outdoors 1–2 weeks after your last spring frost, once the soil is at least 65°F (18°C), in full sun and rich soil. Plant 2–3 seeds per hill, 1 inch (2.5 cm) deep, with hills 4–8 feet (1.2–2.4 m) apart, then thin to the strongest 1–2 plants. Water about 1 inch (2.5 cm) a week at the base, and harvest when the rind is hard, the color is deep and even, and the stem has turned woody, usually 90–120 days after sowing.

Pumpkins need three things: a long, warm season, plenty of space and steady feeding. Get those right and even a beginner can grow a wheelbarrow of pumpkins for pies and carving. For a Halloween harvest in most of the US and UK, sow in late May to mid June; count back from your target date using the "days to maturity" on the seed packet.

Key takeaways
  • When: after the last frost, soil 65°F (18°C) or warmer.
  • Space: vining types need 50–100 sq ft (5–9 m²) each; bush types about a quarter of that.
  • Feeding: lots of compost at planting, then a balanced feed when vines start to run.
  • Water: about 1 inch (2.5 cm) a week, at the soil, not on the leaves.
  • Harvest: hard rind, full color, dry woody stem; cut with 3–4 inches (8–10 cm) of stem.

Short on space? Choose a bush or semi-bush variety, or train vines up a sturdy trellis and support small fruits with slings.

When should you plant pumpkins?

Pumpkins are killed by frost and sulk in cold soil. Sow outdoors 1–2 weeks after your average last frost, when the soil has warmed to about 65°F (18°C); find your last frost date by ZIP code. In short-season areas, start seeds indoors in 3–4 inch (8–10 cm) pots 2–3 weeks before transplanting, and handle the roots gently because pumpkins dislike disturbance.

How do you plant pumpkin seeds?

  1. Pick a sunny spot with at least 6–8 hours of direct sun and well-drained soil.
  2. Make hills (low mounds about 3 feet / 1 m wide) and work in 2–3 inches (5–8 cm) of compost or well-rotted manure. Mounds warm up faster and drain better.
  3. Sow 2–3 seeds per hill, 1 inch (2.5 cm) deep, flat side down.
  4. Space hills 4–8 feet (1.2–2.4 m) apart for vining types, 3–4 feet (about 1 m) for bush types.
  5. Thin to the strongest 1–2 seedlings per hill once they have true leaves, by snipping the others at soil level.

How do you care for pumpkin plants?

  • Water: about 1 inch (2.5 cm) a week, more in hot spells. Water in the morning at the base to keep leaves dry and reduce powdery mildew.
  • Mulch: straw or shredded leaves keep soil moist, suppress weeds and keep fruit off bare soil.
  • Feed: side-dress with compost or a balanced fertilizer when vines start to run, then switch to a lower-nitrogen feed once fruit sets.
  • Pollination: each plant has male and female flowers; females have a tiny pumpkin at the base. If fruit shrivels, bees may be scarce, so hand-pollinate early in the morning with a small brush.
  • Fewer, bigger pumpkins: for large fruit, keep 2–3 pumpkins per vine and pinch off later flowers.
  • Turn the fruit gently now and then, and set it on a board or straw to avoid rot.

What pests and problems affect pumpkins?

  • Powdery mildew: white dusty patches on leaves; improve airflow, water at the base, and remove badly affected leaves.
  • Squash bugs and cucumber beetles: check leaf undersides and hand-pick; cover young plants with insect netting until flowering.
  • Squash vine borer (US): sudden wilting of a vine; look for a hole and sawdust-like frass near the base.
  • Slugs (common in the UK): protect young seedlings.

Can you grow pumpkins in containers?

Yes, with a compact or miniature variety. Use a container of at least 15–20 gallons (60–75 litres) per plant, rich potting mix with compost, and a trellis for the vines. Containers dry out quickly, so water daily in hot weather and feed every two weeks.

When and how do you harvest pumpkins?

  • Ripe signs: deep, even color; a hard rind your fingernail can't dent; a dry, woody stem; and dying vines.
  • Cut, don't pull: leave 3–4 inches (8–10 cm) of stem attached; pumpkins without stems rot quickly.
  • Harvest before a hard frost.
  • Cure for about 10 days in a warm, dry spot (80–85°F / 27–29°C is ideal), then store in a cool, dry place around 50–55°F (10–13°C). Well-cured pumpkins keep for 2–3 months or longer.

Frequently asked questions

How long do pumpkins take to grow?

Most varieties take 90–120 days from sowing to harvest; miniatures can be ready in about 90 days and giants take longer.

How many pumpkins does one plant produce?

Typically 2–5 for full-size varieties and more for miniatures. Limiting fruit per vine gives bigger pumpkins.

Why are my pumpkin flowers falling off?

The first flowers are usually male and drop naturally. Female flowers falling or tiny fruits shriveling usually means poor pollination.

When should I plant pumpkins for Halloween?

Late May to mid-June in most of the US and UK. Count back from late October using the days to maturity on the seed packet.

The bottom line

Sow pumpkins after the last frost in warm soil, give them sun, space, compost and steady water, help pollination if fruit doesn't set, and harvest when the rind is hard and the stem is woody. Cure them before storing.
